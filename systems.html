Systems

Some questions become clearer only when the system is viewed as a whole. This page maps the institutions, rules, technologies, incentives and cultural assumptions that interact before an outcome reaches an individual.

SYSTEMS MAP

Why a whole-system page

My interest in systems is broader than discrimination alone. It includes the way government, law, technology, economics, education, health, media, faith, culture and administrative procedure overlap, and the way responsibility can become difficult to locate when each institution sees only its own part.

That perspective matters especially when a rule looks neutral in isolation but produces a different practical effect once combined with data systems, organisational incentives, resource constraints or historical inequalities.

Clarifying what is unclear

As a student, researcher and Black woman, I am interested in where systems become difficult to read from the outside: who made the decision, which rule governed it, what data travelled between organisations, what discretion was exercised and what route exists for correction.

Systemic discrimination is one important application of that method, but not the only one. The same mapping can be used to study AI governance, higher education, welfare, transport, public administration, markets or institutional failure without assuming the conclusion before the mechanism has been traced.
