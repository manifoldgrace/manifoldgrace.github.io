# Federated AI Research Consortia: Governance, Collaborative Intelligence and the Measurement of Collective Reliability

**Research-paper initiative — November 2026**  
**Status:** Conceptual protocol and paper framework. No completed experimental results, endorsements, formal partnerships or preprint asserted.

## Conceptual context
An independent research consortium could permit multiple organisations and AI systems to cooperate in evaluation, provenance checking and calibrated safety research without merging their ownership or control. Whether it actually improves accuracy, scientific reproducibility, safety or access remains an empirical question.

## Main research question
**Under what measurable conditions does an independently governed, cross-organisational multi-model research consortium produce greater safety, epistemic reliability and public-interest value than either corporate consolidation or independent single-provider research?**

## Subquestions
1. Do blind independent outputs followed by evidence-indexed critique outperform single-provider reasoning on matched tasks and budgets?
2. When do model correlations, persuasive errors or consensus pressure overwhelm apparent plurality?
3. Which governance safeguards sustain independent challenge while protecting consent, private data, intellectual property and competition?
4. How do openness, membership barriers, review costs, accountability and auditability compare among institutional structures?
5. Which claimed gains are actually explained by more compute, larger context or stronger constituent models rather than collaboration?

## Testable hypotheses and falsification
H1: Federation with independent blind evaluation and provenance-aware adjudication improves calibrated accuracy on selected tasks relative to matched single-provider baselines. H1 is not supported if results fail across independent test sets or safety/privacy guardrails worsen.

H2: Organisational separation preserves error diversity better than shared ownership. H2 is not supported if error-correlation measures do not differ after controlling for architecture, training overlap and task selection.

H3: Open reproducible benchmarking improves external scrutiny. Test with external replication rate, provenance coverage, review accessibility and observed correction latency; do not assume effect without comparison.

## Method and evidence plan
A phased mixed-methods design: literature/overlap audit; comparative governance case studies; documented benchmark design; three- and four-model experimental conditions versus single-model and cost-matched baselines; stakeholder governance review; preregistered safety and privacy thresholds; transparent negative-result reporting. Distinguish proposed collaboration from actual signed participation.

## Risks and limitations
Correlated hallucinations, deliberate manipulation, captured governance, anticompetitive coordination, data leakage, uneven access, cost, regulatory uncertainty and ambiguous attribution of collective gains.
