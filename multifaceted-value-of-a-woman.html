The Multifaceted Value of a Woman

A woman does not have to prove her value to anyone. Her inherent dignity is not earned by appearance, marriage, fertility, physical ability, income, compliance, domestic labour or approval from others. Genesis 1:27 affirms women and men as made in God's image. The following catalogue examines virtues, knowledge, capabilities and contributions that may express additional practical, relational, economic, cultural and spiritual value. No woman needs to have every quality; these abilities are not exclusively female.

References in parentheses are abbreviated biblical passages associated with a theme, not claims that every modern skill is explicitly stated in Scripture. Biblical references should be interpreted in context. Proverbs 31:10–31 describes activity across commerce, judgement, caring, speech, strength and stewardship, not simply childbearing.

How value should be understood

Inherent worth: personhood, dignity and rights do not depend on productivity. Character: moral conduct and relational qualities. Capability: knowledge, skills and potential, whether or not exercised. Contribution: benefits to others, some measurable and others intangible. Preference: attraction and compatibility are subjective and must not be confused with objective human worth.

Childbearing and motherhood are included where applicable, but are not a primary or universal definition of womanhood. Whether someone has children does not establish their virtue, competence or the future behaviour of those children. Good parenting can influence outcomes without guaranteeing them.

Evidence and limitations

The UK Office for National Statistics estimated unpaid household service work in 2016 at £1.24 trillion, equivalent to 63.1% of GDP at that time. This is a valuation of total household unpaid production by people of all genders, not a monetary price assigned to women. ONS also reported women spent more time than men in unpaid work. Economic measurement does not capture every form of care, companionship, wisdom, spiritual service or cultural influence. No universal count of 'high-value women' is scientifically established by this catalogue.

Sources: Genesis 1:27, Galatians 3:28, Proverbs 31:10–31; ONS household satellite account; ONS gender distribution of unpaid work.

Research questions

How should non-market contributions be recognised without commodifying human beings? How do different institutions distinguish inherent dignity from capability, social contribution and subjective attractiveness? How does reducing women to fertility or caregiving obscure contributions in knowledge, enterprise, governance, arts, community and faith? Which contributions remain systematically invisible in economic and social indicators?
