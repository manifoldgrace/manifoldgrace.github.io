How to document non-traditional work experience and learning

People acquire knowledge through employment, self-directed projects, volunteering, education, creative practice and other experiences. A chronological record can help explain the work undertaken while separating personal interpretation from independently verifiable evidence.

The Chronological Ethical Self-Inventory (CESI) is an exploratory Manifold Grace research framework connecting life-course reflection with evidence, ethical interpretation and applied strategy. It is not an accredited qualification or an alternative to formal recognition-of-prior-learning assessment.

1. Record the activity and its date

Identify when the work happened, its purpose and relevant context. Differentiate duties carried out from duties only planned or discussed.

2. Link each claim to specific evidence

Where available, use dated documents, deliverables, job descriptions, reports, correspondence, portfolios, feedback and publications. Avoid treating a personal chronology as proof of every result.

3. Explain the skills demonstrated

Describe methods, tools, independent decisions, collaboration, quality controls and what was learned. Make claims proportionate to what records show.

4. Distinguish contribution from external adoption

Submitting a recommendation or research proposal documents a contribution; it does not establish that another organisation adopted, endorsed or implemented it. Record review and outcomes separately.

5. Map evidence to a defined standard

For university recognition of prior learning (RPL/APL), use the receiving institution’s specific learning outcomes, level descriptors, time limits and formal assessment process. For employment, map achievements to relevant job criteria without inflating titles or responsibilities.

6. Protect private material

Public summaries should omit unnecessary identifying details and sensitive case records. Keep underlying documents securely and disclose them only when appropriate for the recipient and purpose.
