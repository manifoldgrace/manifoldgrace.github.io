MILES

Multilevel, Interdisciplinary, Longitudinal Evaluation of Systems — a proposed research architecture for discovering, relating and critically evaluating research points across disciplinary, institutional and historical boundaries.

Published: 8 November 2026 · Version 0.1 · Working concept by Grace R. Nettey · External review pending

Knowledge graphsEvidence provenanceLongitudinal evaluationMilestone evaluationSystems research

Purpose and research question

A research point should remain discoverable even before all relevant disciplines or relationships are known. MILES proposes to record individual claims, questions, findings and events; connect them to multiple subject umbrellas; and independently test those connections without treating thematic proximity as causal proof.

Overarching question: How can interdisciplinary knowledge, historical evidence and institutional relationships be systematically discovered, connected, evaluated and revised across time to improve the completeness, transparency and reliability of research and policy reasoning?

Research status: Proposed synthesis and evidence-evaluation architecture. Not independently validated, not a completed implementation and not a claim to have invented provenance or knowledge graphs. The working acronym also overlaps with a distinct higher-education instrument published by Xu and colleagues (2023).

Three linked layers

LAYER 01

Knowledge mapping

Durable research-point identifiers, concept and alias resolution, evolving research umbrellas and prospective relationships.

LAYER 02

Evidence evaluation

Source and relationship-level provenance, alternative explanations, contradictions and explicit evidence status.

LAYER 03

Systems analysis

Historical sequences, institutional power, delayed effects, feedback, adaptation, possible causal paths and outcomes.

These layers can operate iteratively: discovering a new contradiction can reopen a mapped relationship or change an analytical conclusion.

For example, underemployment, skills underutilisation and precarious work may connect without being considered synonyms. A proposed edge is not proof of causation.

Milestone Evaluation

A cross-layer module records policy and research inflection points: announcement, enactment, commencement, implementation, observed outcomes, unintended consequences and possible system “snap-back.” It also records methodological discoveries, review decisions and corrections.

Cabaj's systems-change distinction between strategic learning, systems change and mission impact informs evaluation of results. These types of results are distinct from macro, meso and micro levels of analysis.

Initial study — UK economic participation, 2007–2027

The proposed study considers employment, education, welfare, migration, technology, equality, finance and economic mobility, examining possible interactions rather than presuming a single cause. The 2007 baseline precedes the financial crisis; 2027 is a prospective follow-up period as of November 2026.

2007 — Pre-crisis baseline

Employment structures, financial conditions and institutional arrangements.

2008–2012 — Crisis and recession

Financial disruption, workforce restructuring and policy responses.

2013–2019 — Labour-market shifts

Outsourcing, gig markets, zero-hours arrangements, pay and occupational progression.

2020–2021 — Pandemic response

Furlough, support eligibility, employer decisions and disruptions.

2022–2026 — Post-pandemic adjustment

Inflation, skills recognition, recruitment, migration policy and changing institutions.

2027 — Follow-up (future)

New evidence, policy outcomes, revisions and comparison with earlier expectations.

Methods, comparison and independent review

Review against realist evaluation, process tracing, system dynamics, longitudinal econometrics and existing knowledge-graph approaches including OpenAlex, Wikidata and nanopublications. Their prior contributions must be credited; MILES' additional usefulness remains to be demonstrated through comparative tests.

External reviewers are invited to examine disciplinary completeness, concept resolution, source independence, causal validity, reproducibility, missing mechanisms and the feasibility of a bounded pilot.

Proposed pilot: Trace one documented UK employment-policy sequence using persistent point IDs, independently evidenced edges and dated milestones. Distinguish observed changes from contested explanations and hypotheses.

Research workings — academic foundations, prior art and validation

This working record shows how published research informs MILES, which parts are already established elsewhere, and what remains untested. It separates support for underlying methods from evidence that the combined MILES architecture is distinctive or effective.

Review finding: Cross-disciplinary discovery, research knowledge graphs, evidence provenance, complexity-informed evaluation and process tracing have established scholarly precedents. These sources do not independently validate MILES 0.1. Its contribution must be tested against existing approaches rather than assumed from the presence of relevant literature.

Known overlap and originality safeguards

OpenAlex, Wikidata, nanopublications, the Open Research Knowledge Graph and evidence-centred research graphs already provide important parts of the proposed functionality. In particular, individual assertions, qualifiers, references, provenance and structured scholarly comparisons are not unique inventions of MILES. A distinct contribution would require demonstrated utility from connecting those capabilities to institutional power, dated policy sequences, contradictory evidence and reproducible systems analysis.

Proposed comparative pilot — UK employment 2007–2027

Scope: Begin with one bounded, documented employment-policy sequence, using 2007 as a pre-crisis baseline where supported by data. Assess evidence available by 8 November 2026; treat 2027 as a future follow-up, not observed history.

  1. Pre-register a research question and inclusion criteria for an ordinary literature-review comparator.
  2. Apply the same source corpus and comparable researcher time to the MILES-assisted method.
  3. Record point IDs, conceptual aliases and distinctions, research umbrellas, edge-level sources, timestamps, claims and counterclaims.
  4. Compare discovery recall and precision, claim-source correctness, temporal accuracy, false causal edges, reviewer agreement, reproducibility and effort.
  5. Retain negative results, competing explanations, missing evidence and dated corrections.

Limits and outstanding review questions

The architecture is an unpublished working concept. Neither superior discovery performance nor causal validity has been demonstrated. Potential risks include false graph links, category drift, duplicate concepts, incomplete source coverage, hindsight bias, excessive complexity and conflation of institutional association with responsibility. Independent academic assessment, technical prototyping and a reproducible pilot are still required.

Next milestone: Request interdisciplinary criticism on novelty, ontology reuse, causal validity and the minimum feasible benchmark. Publish any response or correction with its own date; do not describe an enquiry as endorsement.
