INFORMATION SCIENCE · EVIDENCE QUALITY · INTERDISCIPLINARY APPLICATIONS

Evidence Verification Across Sectors

How accurate source attribution, chronology, historical-version retrieval, contradiction analysis and accountable human review can support different professions—without conflating their purposes or standards.

Independent conceptual research · Clarified 3 November 2026 · No claim of deployment or professional accreditation

One family of methods, different professional questions

Many industries need to know whether a record is authentic, whether its contents support a particular assertion, how it relates to other information, when it was applicable, what is missing, and who reviewed the conclusion. These are shared information-science and evidence-assurance questions, not a claim that evidence used for academic assessment is the same as evidence used for a criminal investigation.

The same record may also be evaluated differently according to the legitimate purpose of the review. A training certificate may demonstrate completion of a course for academic recognition; it does not automatically establish a person's whereabouts on a particular date for a criminal investigation.

Clear boundary: Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL/APL) is an educational credit and competency-recognition process. Criminal investigation is a distinct legal and evidential process. They must not share a single assessment category, outcome label, threshold or professional decision rule.

Shared technical functions

Source provenance

Identify the originating record, custodian, retrieval authority, document location and any derivative copies.

Temporal accuracy

Distinguish event, creation, effective, publication and amendment dates. Retrieve the version that applied at the relevant time.

Claim-to-record coverage

Link exact assertions to the records that support, challenge or do not address them. Preserve qualification and uncertainty.

Contradiction and gaps

Flag inconsistent claims, copied sources, missing material and unresolved conflicts without inventing explanations.

Review and correction history

Keep human reviewer decisions, revisions, source changes and downstream corrections auditable.

Privacy and proportionality

Limit collection, processing, access and retention to the lawful needs of each specific sector and task.

EDUCATION · SEPARATE APPLICATION

Academic Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL/APL)

RPL asks whether previous study or demonstrated experience satisfies clearly identified academic learning outcomes and credit requirements. A traceable evidence portfolio can map each claimed competency to course descriptors, assessed work, qualifications or validated professional learning. It can also flag missing module information, credit-level mismatches, duplicate credit and unresolved questions for academic assessors.

Possible review fields include learning-outcome ID, awarding or teaching institution, qualification or module identifier, academic level, credit volume, assessment method, dated source evidence, institutional equivalence criteria, assessor comments and appeal or correction history.

Not a criminal evidence process: An RPL decision concerns academic recognition, not liability, guilt, innocence or criminal credibility. Academic credit decisions remain subject to the relevant institution's lawful academic regulations and competent judgment.

JUSTICE · SEPARATE APPLICATION

Criminal investigation and case-evidence evaluation

Criminal casework may require disciplined comparison of documents, disclosures, timelines and accounts. A proposed AI-assisted workflow could help an authorised reviewer retain exact source references, examine contradictory records and avoid conflating document authenticity with truthfulness or evidential weight.

Not an academic recognition process: Even where a relevant workplace or education record appears in a criminal case, it is reviewed for the case-specific legal question—not treated as an RPL assessment. Access, disclosure, privacy and competent review are governed by the relevant legal duties.

Architecture and feature-request relevance

The proposed common infrastructure is modular: authorised record intake; source and identity provenance; historical as-of-date lookup; claim-to-source mapping; contradictions and missing-record reporting; versioned human review; and correction propagation. Each sector must supply its own rules and context rather than inheriting a universal “truth score”.

  1. Common evidence layer: Source IDs, provenance, dates, versions, integrity information and authorised access.
  2. Sector-specific assessment layer: Academic credit criteria; clinical interpretation; legal disclosure duties; financial assertions; audit evidence standards, as appropriate.
  3. Human judgment layer: Named reviewers, reasons, challenge and correction mechanisms, limits and downstream reliance.

These principles connect to the independent claim-to-evidence and contradiction feature request, historical as-of-date retrieval, authorised evidence registry and correction tracking. Links describe recorded proposals, not confirmed adoption or implementation.
