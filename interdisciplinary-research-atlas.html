Interdisciplinary Research Atlas

Explore where research questions, site pages, academic themes, methods and sectors intersect. This atlas is a navigational and evidence-mapping tool—not a claim that reading or producing a page completes a degree module.

Interpretation: Links show a thematic affinity between a site page and themes contained in the uploaded programme specifications, or a broader research extension. They do not indicate matriculation, assessment, module equivalence, academic credit, professional recognition, curriculum endorsement or work completed to a particular level. The programmes are 2024–25 reference documents, not a comprehensive taxonomy of society.

How the associations work

Programme theme↔ research domain↔ site page↔ method / evidence↔ sector and collaborator

Ontological layer: distinguish subjects, methods, actors, organisations, institutions, industries, outputs and outcomes. A research area is not an assessment, and a case study is not a programme qualification.

Epistemological layer: record whether each link comes from a documented module, a thematic inference, an independent experiment or a reviewed outcome. This first release is a manually curated semantic crosswalk, not a validated knowledge graph.

Governance layer: use connections to identify who may need to collaborate: policy researchers, professional bodies, regulators, technologists, economists, lawyers, educators, practitioners and affected communities. Collaboration is a possible application of the map, not evidence that these parties have participated.

Future schema extensions

Module learning outcomes; credit values; course levels; HECoS codes; UK SIC sectors; organisation and profession types; research methods; public governance levels (local, national, international); SDG targets; RPL evidence; expert collaborators; provenance; conflicting classifications; dates and revisions. These are proposed additional dimensions, not yet populated.
