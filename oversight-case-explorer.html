Additional source catalogues for systematic expansion

The source archives below collectively contain more than 1,000 published proceedings or releases, but these are not automatically unique cases of oversight capture. Records must be extracted, deduplicated by proceeding, checked against primary findings, and classified before the case counter can increase.

  • US Securities and Exchange Commission — Litigation Releases. Thousands of dated federal civil enforcement litigation releases; multiple releases may describe the same lawsuit, and a complaint is not a finding of liability.
  • World Bank — Sanctions Board Decisions. 122 publicly indexed decisions as of November 2026; some are reconsiderations or related proceedings.
  • UK Accountability Index — Public Inquiries. 116 public inquiry entries; an inquiry is not proof of institutional corruption.

Expansion protocol: One record per distinct investigation or underlying proceeding; direct source URL and date; allegation versus finding; oversight relevance; duplicate or appeal linkage; verification stage. Existing 116 screening candidates remain visible and unaltered.

How to interpret these records

This is a discovery and screening dataset, not an independently audited register of 116 instances of corruption. Many entries document the existence of an inquiry or investigation only. A named inquiry does not demonstrate deliberate interference by its oversight body. Before drawing conclusions, consult the primary report, record its actual findings, identify any judicial rulings, and separately assess conflict of interest, investigative independence, obstruction, remedies and unresolved questions.

The dataset preserves source-directory links and research limitations. Records are not legal findings or accusations against named individuals. The CSV is machine-readable and may be analysed offline.
