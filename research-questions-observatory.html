RESEARCH QUESTION · NOVEMBER 2026 · PROPOSED

Cross-domain evidence and evaluator fallibility

Main question: Can provenance-aware cross-domain and longitudinal evaluation distinguish missing knowledge, mistaken evaluation criteria and genuinely unsupported contributions more accurately than single-domain, single-context or conventional consensus baselines?

Context: Evidence can be distributed across personal histories, institutions, culture, practical work and conflicting perspectives. Existing research documents context-position effects, cultural evaluation limits and meaningful disagreement. The proposed work tests whether structured comparison and evaluator self-audits improve interpretation without compromising safety, consent or correction accuracy.

Supporting questions: Which evidence is lost across time and domains? When is disagreement informative? How can an AI detect that its own classification rubric is inadequate? What would falsify an apparent improvement?

RESEARCH QUESTION · NOVEMBER 2026 · CONCEPTUAL

Independent AI consortiums and collective reliability

Question: Under what measurable conditions does an independently governed, cross-organisational multi-model research consortium produce greater safety, epistemic reliability and public-interest value than either corporate consolidation or independent single-provider research?

Context: Multi-model coordination may enhance verification but also amplify correlated errors, persuasion and governance risk. Comparisons must distinguish shared ownership from voluntary technical federation and compare equally resourced alternatives. This is a proposed investigation, not a finding or established partnership.

Independent Research Questions Observatory

A public register of questions arising from observation, professional experience, interdisciplinary enquiry and emerging societal changes—whether or not they become funded projects or published papers. It enables readers to preview possible enquiries and connect them to appropriate evidence, geographical comparisons and existing research work.

Why keep an independent questions register?

Funding institutions help shape research agendas by setting strategic remits, eligibility and assessment criteria. They do not determine all knowledge production: UKRI offers applicant-led and targeted funding; Wellcome supports discovery research; institutional researchers and independent researchers can also formulate questions without a specific call.

As contexts change, a previously overlooked question may become relevant to a funder, research team, public body or different research population. The observable contribution here is the documented formulation and structured examination of a question, not a claim to be the first person ever to think of it.

Attribution and status: The original register reflects questions documented or linked to existing work by 11 November 2026; additional exploratory questions were added 12 November 2026. That date is the register date, not a claim that each question was first conceived on that day. Earlier provenance must be supported by independently dated records. A question is not evidence that its implied hypothesis is true.

Publication-history transparency, historical evidence and long-term AI reliability — questions recorded 13 November 2026

Eight linked exploratory research questions build on the existing Manifold Grace evidence-verification and knowledge-continuity programme. Their publication here records a research agenda, not completed studies or proof that any specific ASI failure will occur. The initial practical archive window may be 20 years, while historical preservation can extend to the earliest surviving records wherever legally accessible and verifiable.

RQ-047: Can a publisher-maintained, persistent, machine-readable directory of all historical publication URLs measurably improve public accountability and historical discoverability?

Research context: Compare canonical sitemaps, CMS exports and independently archived publications across multiple publication years; measure missing-URL rates, recoverability, broken links and chronology. A suggested 20-year starting scope is an initial evaluation window, not a historical limit.

Verification and limitations: A publicly absent URL is not by itself evidence of deliberate removal. Lawful withdrawals, archival gaps, publication rights and data protection require explicit treatment.

Current status: Question recorded 13 November 2026; no new experimental result or legal requirement asserted. Return to register entry.

RQ-048: Which metadata and preservation standards best retain original publication dates, stable article identities, correction histories, withdrawals and source provenance across decades or centuries?

Research context: Test canonical IDs, original timestamps, revisions, first-seen archive dates, publication formats and version history using held-out records and independently dated evidence.

Verification and limitations: The original record must remain distinguishable from amended versions, reprints, syndicated editions and the date a research system found it.

Current status: Question recorded 13 November 2026; no new experimental result or legal requirement asserted. Return to register entry.

RQ-049: Does broad, provenance-preserving access to primary historical evidence reduce inherited factual errors, rare-knowledge loss or confident misinterpretation in successive AI systems?

Research context: Compare identical question sets using original-record retrieval, metadata-only access, model-generated summaries and no retrieval; measure fact retention, uncertainty calibration, correction and contradiction resolution.

Verification and limitations: Observed recursive-training model collapse is not proof of inevitable ASI collapse; avoid conflating synthetic-data training with evidence loss.

Current status: Question recorded 13 November 2026; no new experimental result or legal requirement asserted. Return to register entry.

RQ-050: What minimum independently verifiable historical knowledge infrastructure is necessary for the long-term epistemic resilience of advanced AI and possible ASI?

Research context: Specify testable failure modes: archive loss, selective omission, corrupted revision history, unavailable sources and misleading retrieval; measure recovery, auditability and reproducibility with independent reviewers.

Verification and limitations: ASI existence, eventual architecture and requirements remain uncertain. 'Necessary' must be tested against credible alternative designs.

Current status: Question recorded 13 November 2026; no new experimental result or legal requirement asserted. Return to register entry.

RQ-051: Can a longitudinal evidence observatory compare historical predictions with later outcomes without hindsight bias, retrospective rewording or unsupported causal conclusions?

Research context: Timestamp and freeze original statements; define measurable forecasts, horizons and outcomes before scoring; distinguish reporting, opinion, scenarios, predictions and corrections; compare against explicit baselines.

Verification and limitations: Later outcomes may be unknown or ambiguous. Coverage gaps and retrospective selection can distort apparent accuracy.

Current status: Question recorded 13 November 2026; no new experimental result or legal requirement asserted. Return to register entry.

RQ-052: How can cross-publisher and institutional publication directories detect meaningful contradictions and omissions without mistaking editorial scope or reporting differences for concealment?

Research context: Develop common entity/event identifiers, temporal alignment, claim-to-evidence links, jurisdiction context and human-reviewed mismatch labels; test precision, recall and false-accusation rates.

Verification and limitations: Absence or discrepancy is a research lead, not evidence of misconduct; publishing standards, privacy and lawful access differ.

Current status: Question recorded 13 November 2026; no new experimental result or legal requirement asserted. Return to register entry.

RQ-053: What proportionate legal, professional or voluntary standards could require preservation of historical publication indexes while protecting privacy, copyright and legitimate corrections?

Research context: Compare existing sitemap, records-retention, archive and public-sector disclosure duties; examine proportionality, historical burden, exceptions, independent appeals and implementation costs.

Verification and limitations: A proposed 20-year baseline or longer preservation duty is not automatically current law; distinguish legal obligations from policy recommendations.

Current status: Question recorded 13 November 2026; no new experimental result or legal requirement asserted. Return to register entry.

RQ-054: Can AI-assisted archival reconciliation recover original publication metadata across legacy domains and content-management systems, and verify completeness without unauthorised access?

Research context: Test human-checked entity resolution, DOI-like identifiers where applicable, archival snapshots, redirects, hashes and confidence labels on legally accessible datasets.

Verification and limitations: Respect website terms, licensed access, copyright and personal information. Do not bypass paywalls or claim that missing historical sources can always be reconstructed.

Current status: Question recorded 13 November 2026; no new experimental result or legal requirement asserted. Return to register entry.

From a question to potential research

1. Record the question and its origin

Assign a persistent identifier, clear wording, first supported date, observation category and links to available source records. Protect personal and third-party information.

2. Establish what is already known

Search high-quality prior work; record strongest contrary evidence, established methods, remaining limitations and the precise uncertainty. Distinguish gaps in data, theory, context and implementation.

3. Specify testability and relevant populations

Define outcomes, comparisons, alternative explanations, feasible data sources, ethics and falsification criteria. State what would count against the hypothesis.

4. Add international extensions without assuming equivalence

Begin with England when relevant; then Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland; Europe as a separate regional comparison; subsequently Africa, the Americas, Asia, Oceania and the Pacific with appropriate subregional divisions. Legal definitions and source comparability must be checked per jurisdiction.

5. Map possible research interest—not guaranteed funding

Identify potential alignment with published funder remits or public consultations without implying endorsement, eligibility or active applications. A question can remain independent of any funding strategy.

6. Attach optional project or paper initiative

Link to prior outputs or a scoped research concept only where justified. Avoid creating fictional manuscript progress or presenting idea capture as a completed study.

7. Maintain revision and collaboration records

Document question revisions, competing interpretations, evidence dates, collaboration enquiries and subsequent published outcomes with a visible correction history.

Using questions for collaboration

Researchers, institutions and funders can cite a stable question ID when proposing a comparative study, methodological critique, policy assessment, replication or interdisciplinary collaboration. A question can be developed in multiple contexts without assuming a single paper or owner has answered it comprehensively. Contact or collaboration pathways may be added later subject to privacy and moderation controls.
