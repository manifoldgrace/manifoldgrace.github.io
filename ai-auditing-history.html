What the proposal investigates

Specialised agents would inspect an AI deployment's architecture and operation through authorised evidence; reconcile the observed design against declarations, policies and compliance duties; detect discrepancies or hidden violations where access permits; produce signed, structured assurance findings; and pass verified corrections into a separately authorised improvement and testing pipeline. The research question is whether this combination can improve evidence completeness, reliability and independence in complex AI assurance.

The design prohibits ordinary audit agents from unilaterally rewriting binding controls or deploying updates. No system can be presumed entirely inspectable or immune to privileged alteration; missing access, false positives, uncertain causal conclusions and maliciously inaccurate declarations remain central test conditions.

Testable contribution and falsification criteria

A systematic overlap review should ask what published systems already accomplish, then specify a remaining limitation: Can independent agents detect undeclared architecture changes or breaches of externally enforced controls, preserve provenance under partial access and improve quality without compromising oversight? Candidate metrics include discrepancy recall and precision, missing-evidence reporting, audit reproducibility, false-positive rates, remediation approval integrity and quality change after redeployment. These are proposed tests; no results are asserted.

Evidence integrity and attribution notes
