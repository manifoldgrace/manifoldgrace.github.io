# Working paper initiative B — Public-interest publication accessibility and archival duties
**Manifold Grace policy and comparative legal research manuscript, initiated 13 November 2026. Status: prospective legal/policy analysis, not legal advice, a legislative proposal adopted by a government, or a completed systematic review.**

## Problem and rationale
A continuous stream of news releases can create substantial research and accessibility burdens. A publisher's searchable catalogue may omit historical records because of migration, retention choices, correction procedures, archival technology or lawful withdrawal. Public transparency should not require every citizen to read every article.

## Proposed norm for examination
Qualifying publishers and public-interest institutions could maintain: (1) original-source URLs or persistent identifiers; (2) first publication dates and later correction/version dates; (3) year-by-year human-readable archives; (4) daily, weekly, monthly and annual downloadable catalogues; (5) structured machine-readable metadata; (6) a declared scope and audit record; and (7) proportionate privacy, safety, archival and rights exceptions.

This is an **advocated research proposition**, not an existing universal legal requirement.

## Testable questions
Does the intervention improve retrieval task completion and transparency across different user groups? What is the marginal implementation cost for small and large publishers? Which alternatives (HTML, JSON, accessible EPUB, PDF) work better for different users? Could mandatory full-article bundling breach copyright or privacy rights? How should a removed article be recorded without republishing harmful or unlawful material?

## Legal and governance boundary
The UK copyright exception for text-and-data mining is constrained by lawful access and qualifying non-commercial research; it does not amount to general permission to redistribute source articles or train commercial models. Source URLs and brief bibliographic metadata can be indexed without providing the articles' bodies, subject to applicable legal and contractual checks. Separate analyse: database rights, personal data, journalism exemptions, transparency and retention duties, platform security, accessibility obligations, freedom of expression and legitimate withdrawals.

## Proposed method
Comparative review of UK and international records law, accessibility standards, open government data practice, scholarly publishing standards and private newsrooms; stakeholder consultation with disabled readers, librarians, historians, publishers, creators and independent researchers; cost and impact modelling; competing policy scenarios; fair and falsifiable implementation trials.

## Falsification and alternatives
Evaluate whether existing open sitemaps and licensed archives already achieve equivalent benefits; whether mandated PDFs impose barriers or costs; whether records omission rates are lower than claimed; and whether additional regulatory intervention would be disproportionate. Prefer multiple accessible formats to PDF exclusivity.

## Continuity
The Manifold Grace implementation demonstrates a voluntary publisher-owned archive. General legal conclusions and legislative wording require qualified legal review and evidence of necessity.
