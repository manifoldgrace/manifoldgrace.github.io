Architecture, Assurance & Research Project Pathways

Working framework published 13 November 2026. A connected programme for studying software and AI architectures, comparing original source code with authorised educational derivatives, assessing legal and regulatory duties, testing cybersecurity and safeguarding controls, and documenting measurable outcomes. It supports academic research, professional learning, reproducible engineering and independent scrutiny. The project pathways below are proposals and reusable specifications unless explicitly linked to an implemented repository and tested result.

Difficulty pathways and repository architecture

Both arrangements are supported: one project repository with beginner/, intermediate/ and advanced/ directories, or three separately versioned code repositories where licensing, deployment, ownership or independent releases justify separation. In either approach, all levels should link back to the same source paper, upstream version and project identifier.

Academic and professional paper options

Publication type should be selected to fit the question and evidence, not the other way around. Multiple outputs may share a project, provided their contributions and source records are distinguishable. These are potential formats, not claims that publication has occurred.

Project dossier and linked research records

Reusable project dossier — minimum fields

Project ID; concept and context; audience and intended use; project owner and responsible reviewers; current maturity (concept / design / prototype / tested / deployed); architecture and data-flow diagrams; upstream paper and repository/release/commit; licences and attribution; downstream derivation and code-diff record; beginner, intermediate and advanced learning objectives; relevant legal jurisdictions; compliance requirements; threat model and safeguards; privacy and ethical approval; test baselines; CI/CD evidence; expected and observed outcomes; limitations, counter-evidence and incidents; accessibility; stakeholder feedback; maintainers; dependencies and funding; reproducibility; release and review dates; correction and deprecation policy.

Five linked research records

Conceptual idea: problem, prior work, context and proposed contribution. Research project: objectives, ownership, work packages, milestones and resources. Paper initiative: prospective paper type, working outline, evidence needs and publication status. Contextual research question: scope, variables, counter-hypotheses and falsification plan. Productivity log: dated action, verified evidence link, why it matters and next step. Link all five by stable project ID. Create them as actual records when a specific project begins; do not label a proposal as a completed paper.

Review gates and independent oversight

Intake and relevance → upstream licence and citation verification → architecture review → privacy, safeguarding and regulatory assessment → threat model → implementation and peer review → synthetic-data testing → CI/CD checks → authorised deployment → post-deployment evaluation → independent replication → release, incident correction and retirement. Different risks warrant different levels of oversight; legal interpretation should be reviewed by a qualified specialist where needed.

Acceptance criteria and comparison

Every implemented level should have a measured baseline, testable functional requirements, security and accessibility checks, evidence of approved deployment, date-stamped results, known failure cases and a reviewer-visible change log. A passing test is not proof of legal compliance or absence of vulnerabilities. Compare like-for-like versions and retain negative results.

Licensing, intellectual property and lawful evaluation

Prefer documented open-source licences or the original author's explicit permission. Preserve attribution, notices, licence compatibility and dependency obligations; do not assume public repositories are licensed for cloning or modification. Never bypass authentication or paywalls, retrieve private code without authority, collect sensitive data without suitable safeguards, or test third-party systems without permission.
