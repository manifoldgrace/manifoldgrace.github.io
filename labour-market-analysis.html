AI Research Engineers

A dedicated research-engineering workspace: experimental judgement, reproducibility and scaling evidence.

Role evidence. DeepMind describes research engineers as experimentalists who implement, optimise and scale systems to test ideas, combining mathematical research with software engineering.
“critical bridge between theory and implementation”

01 · Translate research

DeepMind’s description connects research ideas with implementation and evaluation. Employer evidence

Suggested evidence: paper-to-code map, reproduced baseline, deviations from the paper, controlled comparisons and a report of unsuccessful runs.

02 · Train & scale

DeepMind identifies model optimisation and distributed research infrastructure in research engineering. Employer evidence

Suggested evidence: training configuration, dataset provenance, seed sensitivity, compute budget, checkpoints and scaling bottlenecks. Fine-tuning methods should follow the actual role and research question, rather than a universal tool checklist.

03 · Evaluate claims

Research engineers build systems to test and evaluate new ideas. Employer evidence

Suggested evidence: held-out tasks, ablations, uncertainty, leakage checks, failure slices and human adjudication. If using a model judge, validate agreement against human labels; a judge score alone is insufficient evidence.

04 · Engineer reliability

The role includes implementation, optimisation and systems at scale. Employer evidence

Suggested evidence: measured latency and memory, reproducible environments, profiling, regression results and documented research-to-service limits.

Doctoral depth · assess the work, then the qualification

DeepMind says research scientists normally hold a PhD; it does not impose that statement on every research engineer. Anthropic describes a mixed academic background and substantial overlap between engineering and research. A doctorate is valuable evidence of research training, not a universal job-title requirement. DeepMind · Anthropic hiring criteria

“engineers here do lots of research”

Suggested doctoral portfolio: original question → literature gap → defensible method → reproducible experiment → limitations → contribution. Explain negative findings, competing hypotheses and what would falsify your conclusion.

Interview window · architecture, signal path, security

Suggested prompts, not an employer’s prescribed interview:

Architecture: Why this model, baseline and compute design? What alternatives did you reject, and with what evidence?

Signal path: Trace data collection → validation → representation → training → inference → decision. Identify leakage, uncertainty and human decision points.

Security: Explain trust boundaries, access, secrets, supply chain, adversarial inputs and containment. Show test evidence, residual risk and who can stop release.

Research contribution: Which improvement survives ablation, reruns and a fair comparator? What failed, and what did that teach you?

Where agents fit

Agents are a system pattern, not a replacement for the human roles above. They sit around models, retrieval and tools, selecting steps and responding to results.

Architectural distinction: Anthropic separates predefined workflows from agents whose models dynamically direct their process and tool use. More autonomy can increase costs and compound errors. Anthropic — Building effective agents, December 2024

The source now notes that tooling has changed; its architectural distinction remains a useful reference, not a current framework checklist.

Architecture & signal path

Suggested design: user goal → context and permissions → model-selected step → tool execution → verified observation → next step or stop. Keep an auditable trace and an accountable human owner.

RAG supplies retrieved context; a tool interface provides an action; neither automatically makes a system autonomous. The design should justify dynamic decisions rather than assume an agent is necessary.

Anthropic — agentic building blocks

Human responsibilities

Suggested division: research engineers investigate agent behaviour, evaluation and failure; ML / software engineers build reliable execution; architects define integration and authority; domain and safeguarding specialists assess risks to people. The organisation retains accountability for authorised actions.

Role evidence · Separate safeguarding responsibilities

Break, fix & prove

Suggested tests: malicious retrieved instructions, unauthorised tool actions, stale context, fabricated success, repeated actions and runaway loops. Use synthetic data and sandboxed tools.

Suggested controls: least privilege, explicit action boundaries, approval for consequential steps, time and spending limits, verifiable tool results, stopping conditions, recovery and human escalation.

Safeguarding is its own discipline

Design principle for this site: assess safeguarding competence independently of coding, architecture and research credentials. Approval needs evidence about protecting people, not only passing software tests.

People · safeguarding

Adult safeguarding guidance identifies empowerment, prevention, proportionality, protection, partnership and accountability. It concerns abuse and neglect and person-centred responses. DHSC — chapter 14

“Accountability and transparency in delivering safeguarding”

Suggested AI parameters: affected people, vulnerability, coercion and exploitation risks, consent boundaries, accessible reporting, trained human referral, response ownership and follow-up. Record domain and jurisdiction before choosing the applicable procedure.

Systems · cybersecurity

ASVS provides requirements for application technical security controls. OWASP ASVS

Suggested evidence: authentication, authorisation, input handling, supply-chain checks, incident containment and tested recovery. A secure application can still cause interpersonal harm.

Models · AI assurance

NIST AI RMF is voluntary guidance for incorporating trustworthiness into AI design, development, use and evaluation; its 1.0 framework is under revision. NIST — current framework status

Suggested evidence: contextual risks, validity, reliability, harmful outputs, fairness and human oversight. A benchmark score is not a safeguarding qualification.

Context · education & adults

England’s DfE product safety standards apply to generative AI in educational settings; the January 2026 update adds cognitive, emotional, social, mental-health and manipulation considerations. DfE — scope and update

Keeping Children Safe in Education is statutory guidance for schools and colleges in England. Adult safeguarding duties have their own scope and conditions in chapter 14 of the Care Act guidance. These are not universal duties imposed on every AI engineer. KCSIE · Adult safeguarding guidance

Separate safeguarding gate · suggested evidence checklist
  1. Define people: identify the population, setting, potential abuse and exclusions.
  2. Name owner: identify the competent safeguarding lead and referral route.
  3. Set boundaries: define consent, confidentiality, access and escalation rules with domain specialists.
  4. Test scenarios: use synthetic cases of coercion, exploitation, distress and failed referral.
  5. Exercise response: show a human can receive, assess and act on a concern.
  6. Review evidence: record failures, fixes, unresolved risks and the accountable decision.

This is a proposed engineering evidence gate informed by the guidance above, not a certification or substitute for local safeguarding procedures. Do not enter real safeguarding cases into this public demonstration.

Interpretation · opportunities and counter-signals

Suggested interpretation: skills scarcity and a weaker overall vacancy market can coexist. Demonstrate practical experience and evaluate actual roles rather than treating an AI-growth narrative as a job guarantee.

Watch hypotheses: research-to-service reliability, efficient inference, agents with oversight, scientific AI and assurance. The sources above motivate these questions; they do not prove future hiring growth.

Counter-evidence: track removed or frozen roles, narrower eligibility, revised adoption plans and poor customer outcomes. Do not infer salaries, workforce demand or investment returns from fundraising headlines.

Monthly review · supply, demand and fluctuations

Suggested method: use a fixed basket of official role pages, deduplicate job IDs, record openings and closures, compare required skills and experience, and distinguish location, contract and seniority.

Record advertised pay only when the employer discloses it; separate base salary, currency, equity and benefits. Leave missing values unknown. Add official funding announcements, customer evidence and filings as separate signals, then revisit your interpretation.

Use ONS vacancies for the broad economy and DSIT survey for AI employer context. Different populations and periods cannot be combined into one supply–demand ratio.

Research engineer · evidence before outreach

Choose one reproducible research question. Show a fair baseline, controlled changes, uncertainty and an honest contribution. Match that evidence to an actual role’s requirements, then explain its relevance in a short approach. Keep safeguarding evidence separate and identify specialist review needs.

Solution architect · design before delivery

Prepare a requirements map, architecture decisions, trust boundaries, costs, acceptance criteria and a live-service handover. Demonstrate stakeholder judgement and accountable governance alongside technical implementation.

Commercial & trading awareness · test the thesis

Use a paper to propose a mechanism, then check adoption, customer value, costs and financial disclosures. Record evidence that would invalidate the thesis. Technical capability alone does not establish valuation or a trade.
