Before calling something a research gap

State a bounded claim, examine existing literature and implementations, reproduce evidence where possible, record competing explanations and credit earlier progress. A gap is confirmed only within the supported scope. If a solution is only designed or partially tested, publish the response with its real validation status rather than claiming the problem has been solved.

Types of questions include: descriptive, causal, comparative, methodological, architectural, evaluative, safety/regulatory, historical and counterfactual. Expert replies must be attributed to real named sources or explicitly marked as not yet obtained.

Architectural alternatives and trade-offs

ArchitectureAdvantagesTrade-offs and safety considerations
Rule-based / typed comparisonPredictable and inspectable, inexpensive, clear abstention rulesLimited language coverage; safest in narrow, explicitly typed conditions
Hybrid extraction + typed reasoningBroader input handling with stable comparison layerParsing errors may propagate; require field-level tests and abstention
Evidence-first / provenance-awareTraceable citations, versioned claims and reviewer accountabilityHigher data and operational complexity; evidence quality still requires judgment
Agentic multi-step evaluationFlexible information gathering and tool orchestrationMore attack surfaces, nondeterminism and costs; requires access controls and human oversight

There is no universally safest architecture. Choose according to context, authority, expected harm, validation evidence and monitoring. Restrict autonomy when consequences or uncertainty are high.
