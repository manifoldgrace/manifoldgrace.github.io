Ministerial accountability proposal

Scope, dates and evidence standards

Coverage snapshot: 11 November 2026. This is a selected catalogue from the 1956 Dartmouth workshop onward. Some cases use the date of a ruling, report, public disclosure or regulatory update, as indicated by their context. Empty years do not establish an absence of incidents. Historical precursors, allegations, studies and adjudicated findings are labelled. The lessons and future consequences are analysis.

To assess foreseeability, identify the warnings and evidence available before a decision, who had authority and budget to act, which controls were feasible, and which outcomes were recorded. Later findings can inform this inquiry. Date new evidence and link it from the original entry so prior wording and citations remain traceable.

2026

Specialist funding crowded out

11 November 2026 · Foreseeable funding risk

Context and sources

Giving interdisciplinary proposals priority over specialist proposals can weaken the expertise that cross-disciplinary projects depend on, especially where both draw from the same budget. Warning signs include shrinking specialist calls, falling real-terms awards and loss of specialist posts or facilities. UKRI’s 2023 cross-council pilot documents support for interdisciplinary research. Whether such funding displaces specialist work depends on the allocation and award data.

UKRI cross-council funding pilot · Research on data quality in high-stakes AI

Lessons for prevention

Track awards and budgets by discipline over time, maintain routes for specialist work, and require funded AI projects to involve relevant specialists in data design, annotation and testing.

Consequences if unresolved

Loss of specialist capacity can make rare cases harder to recognise, leave training data without important distinctions and allow models to pass broad evaluations while failing on consequential exceptions.
