Equitable Positive-Sum Growth: Mapping Where It Can, Cannot and Might Work

A prospective framework for testing the feasibility of shared economic benefit, disclosing uncertainty and identifying unacceptable harm—not presuming that a win–win solution exists in every case.

Core proposition and research perimeter

Investigate whether and where economically productive arrangements can increase value without imposing avoidable, unmitigated losses on others; make the feasibility conditions, affected populations and evidence boundaries publicly inspectable. Where such arrangements are demonstrated, investigate the safeguards that could make them routine and resilient. Where they cannot be demonstrated, disclose the limits, who may lose, and what remedies or alternatives remain available.

This proposal is a conceptual research specification attributed to Manifold Grace; it is not an empirically established universal theory, existing regulatory standard, AI-validated global map or proof that every conflict can be rendered positive-sum. Research should examine both Pareto improvements (no included participant worse off), distributional or equity objectives (fairness of outcomes across groups), and minimum living-standard constraints. Equal absolute benefits, equal proportional improvements and priority to those with greatest needs are different normative aims: they must be selected transparently rather than smuggled into a single word, “equal”.

Cases and boundaries: a “case” is a defined policy, product or allocation design within a stated jurisdiction, time horizon, population, alternative baseline, evidence set and constraints. New versions or changed assumptions create a new assessment, not a permanent verdict on all uses of a technology.

Three evidence-status and risk zones

Green · demonstrated within scope

An option satisfies defined welfare, legal, distributional and risk thresholds on adequate evidence, including relevant groups and side effects. No material, unmitigated harms identified within the declared assessment scope.

Decision: eligible for controlled use with monitoring, periodic review, remedy and rollback. Green is never a permanent all-clear or global certification.

Amber · uncertain or conditional

Benefits are plausible but evidence is incomplete, assumptions are sensitive, stakeholder impacts conflict, or necessary safeguards are untested.

Decision: restrict to proportionate research, independent review or controlled pilots where lawful and safe; disclose unanswered questions, avoid promoting as validated win–win.

Red · unacceptable harm or violated constraints

Credible evidence establishes material unmitigated loss, unlawful conduct, violations of essential rights or services, or risk beyond the selected acceptable boundary. An infeasible solution may also be marked red for a specific proposed design, with its reason stated.

Decision: do not deploy or continue the unsafe design under those conditions; disclose affected parties, halt/modify as appropriate, provide remedy and assess alternatives.

Critical distinction: missing evidence normally means amber, not green. Credible serious danger can warrant a provisional red precaution while facts are investigated. Red denotes danger of an option, not the character or intrinsic worth of a person, occupation or community. An analytically impossible win–win within fixed constraints is not automatically misconduct; it must be explained as a feasibility limit and addressed through fair choice, compensation or redesign.

Minimum ethical disclosure when loss is foreseeable

Every assessment should identify who might lose, what might be lost, whether the loss is temporary or irreversible, whether it is avoidable, whether the affected party consents or has a choice, whether compensation is real and accessible, and who bears responsibility for remedy. Publication must not disclose confidential information, identifiable vulnerable individuals or sensitive operational detail without a lawful basis. A public summary may be accompanied by restricted evidence and independently auditable confidential records.

It is insufficient to report “net gain” where loss of housing, food, essential care, lawful work or basic rights is hidden by large gains elsewhere. Conversely, evidence of a trade-off must be documented rather than taken as proof that another party intended harm. The output should distinguish observed adverse effect, credible forecast risk, scenario assumption and unresolved allegation.

Research integrity: an AI-generated zone is a decision-support suggestion until the underlying case is validated. A predictive model cannot ethically guarantee future outcomes, and a simulation cannot establish real-world causation on its own. Human decisions remain accountable and contestable.

Making equitable practice the norm without freezing mistakes

“Gridlock as the norm” is interpreted here as institutionalising verified safeguards through procurement criteria, service-level requirements, impact assessments, contracts, audit trails, monitoring, transparency and correction rights—not forbidding reconsideration or allowing algorithmic classifications to become unchallengeable. Green assessments must expire or be reviewed when evidence, technology, parties, environmental conditions or legal standards change. For amber and red, preserve unsuccessful or infeasible alternatives so later researchers can understand both the obstacle and potential ways around it.

Government appraisal guidance already considers distributional effects and uncertainty; the proposed contribution is an integrated, case-addressable, AI-assisted feasibility-and-loss disclosure map across stages, not an assertion that the underlying ideas of Pareto efficiency, distributional appraisal or traffic-light risk triage were invented here.
