Evidence quality, conversion methodology and annual revisits

Evidence ladder: submission confirmation with reference and timestamp; confirmation email; saved application portal receipt or PDF; recruiter acknowledgement; personal entry alone. The last is useful for organisation, but not independent proof that the application reached an employer.

Conversion denominators: submitted, acknowledged, interview, offer and hired. Rates here use all recorded applications except drafts as the denominator. Status is a simplified current-stage view; to preserve stage history, maintain dated notes or a separate outcome record. Rates are descriptive, not evidence of employer bias or the quality of a particular applicant.

Returning after a year: employer and role history can reveal repeat adverts and different recruitment cycles, but a revisit is a prompt to re-evaluate, not a reason to resend identical applications or breach employer instructions.

Advantages: avoids duplicates, helps focus a manageable daily workload, supports follow-ups, preserves decisions and reveals conversion patterns. Limitations: recording can consume time, results depend on accurate updates, personal data requires protection, job advertisements expire, and an unverified self-entry does not prove submission.

Privacy and reliability: data is stored locally in this browser only, not in your website's public source or a secure server. It does not synchronise across devices, and clearing browser storage may erase it. Export an encrypted or otherwise protected backup regularly; do not save sensitive attachments or confidential employer correspondence in public links. Browser-local timestamps can be edited and are not tamper-proof evidence.
