Doctoral AI Research Scientist: Skills & Paper Standards

A practical, discipline-sensitive standard for developing rigorous AI research papers, theoretical contributions, reproducible software, empirical studies and responsible scientific judgement.

RESEARCH SKILLS REFERENCE · 9 NOVEMBER 2026

Purpose and scope

These competencies reflect common expectations in advanced AI research and widely recognised publication practices. They are a developmental reference, not a doctoral qualification, a university-approved syllabus or a claim that every paper requires all techniques. Research excellence is demonstrated through methods suited to the question, credible evidence, transparency and a defensible original contribution.

Publication-quality manuscript architecture

ComponentRequired intellectual work
Title, abstract & keywordsState exactly what is studied, the contribution, approach, evidence and supported findings; do not present planned results as completed.
Introduction & research questionsDefine the problem, significance, gaps and claims with measurable boundaries.
Related workCompare relevant studies, contradictory findings and alternative explanations without overstating novelty.
Theory, model or methodSpecify assumptions, definitions, equations, design decisions, datasets and procedures to the necessary depth.
Experiments / analysesPresent baselines, ablations, evaluation metrics and uncertainty if empirical; complete proofs or arguments if theoretical.
ResultsReport actual outcomes, tables and figures with denominators, units, uncertainty and appropriate comparisons.
Discussion & limitationsAnalyse competing interpretations, generalisability, omitted variables, failure cases and ethical implications.
Conclusion & referencesRestrict conclusions to what evidence supports, present future questions, cite traceable sources and document contributions.
Appendices & artifactsProvide detailed proofs, protocols, code, model and data documentation, and reproduction instructions where appropriate.

Research paper and coding project relationship

Where a research question can be operationalised, maintain a linked, versioned code project with a README, research question, installation instructions, environment dependencies, permitted dataset sources, tests, reproducible experiment scripts, expected outputs, limitations, model or dataset documentation and explicit licence terms. A GitHub repository is useful for code and version history; a Zenodo record/DOI can archive a specific deposited release. Neither should be cited as an existing artifact before creation or deposition.

For literature reviews and normative work, a transparent selection protocol, evidence register and versioned analysis may be more appropriate than forcing an unnecessary code project.

Quality gates before submission

GateWhat must be checked
Scientific contributionQuestion, prior art, originality claims and value of the contribution.
Technical validityProofs, implementation fidelity, robust baselines, correct experimental design, absence of leakage.
Evidence qualityProvenance, measurement, uncertainty, counterevidence, domain limitations and claims-to-results consistency.
Responsible researchPermissions, confidentiality, privacy, risks, fairness and appropriate institutional review.
ReproducibilityDocumented methods, software and dataset versions, compute details and independent testability.
Publication complianceVenue template, anonymity, pages, references, ethics/reproducibility statements and LLM-use disclosures where required.

A paper may be theoretically rigorous without code, empirically rigorous without inventing a new model, or technically rigorous without an exhaustive social-policy analysis. Match the standards to the contribution and venue.

Current research and industry guidance

NeurIPS Paper Checklist — claims, limitations, theoretical assumptions, reproducibility, ethical and societal impacts. ICLR 2026 Author Guide — venue submission, reproducibility, ethics and disclosure of substantial AI contributions. ACM-style artifact evaluation example — documented, consistent, complete and exercisable research artifacts. NIST AI RMF Playbook — governing, mapping, measuring and managing applicable AI risks. Venue policies differ and should be reviewed at submission time.

Best-practice layout and reproducibility tests

Use the publisher's original font, margins, columns, page size, headings and citation rules without custom restyling. Verify readable vector figures, accessibility of labels without colour alone, complete table units, embedded fonts, equations, bibliography data, anonymised author metadata where required, and aligned text/figure citations. Confirm that abstract, claims and actual findings agree. Ensure that significant comparisons include proper controls, and report missing data, uncertainty, limitations, research ethics, use of AI tools and code/data availability as the applicable venue requires.

Publication-integrity rule: GitHub is for versioned code and research artifacts; Zenodo and preprint servers are for appropriate public archives, not replacements for journal or conference peer review. Do not make a paper publicly visible during double-blind evaluation without checking venue rules; avoid false published/accepted status.

Editorial quality: Nature Machine Intelligence explicitly weighs methodological soundness, support for conclusions, significance and broad relevance, not just document appearance. Editorial process. NeurIPS requires a transparency/reproducibility checklist. Paper checklist.
