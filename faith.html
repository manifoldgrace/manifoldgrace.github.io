FAITH · FORMATION

Faith

Christian formation has been a continuous strand of my life rather than a single period or institution. The record combines family church life, baptism, Bible-school study, ministry participation, theological inquiry and an ongoing personal practice of faith.

FORMATION

Church and theological formation

Church attendance formed part of childhood, adolescence and early adulthood. My private pastoral record documents baptism in 2009, Bible-school study at Rhema Church London in 2013 and Believers' Loveworld South Norwood in 2015. The same record identifies foundational study in Scripture interpretation, Christian doctrine, evangelism, pastoral care, church history and practical ministry.

My church history has included St John's, Rhema, Potter's House and Christ Embassy, with particularly intensive participation during the 2010s. This means that my formation has taken place across more than one congregation and church tradition while retaining a continuous emphasis on Scripture, prayer, worship, service and the life of the church.

FORMATION IN PRACTICE

Service, music and community

The pastoral archive records practical church participation through music, greeting, fellowship, work with children, giving, hospitality and service-oriented responsibilities. These experiences sit alongside daily or regular private practices of Bible study, prayer and reflection.

Music has been especially important because it joins formal musicianship with worship and communal service. Church participation also developed a practical vocabulary of stewardship, responsibility, fellowship, teaching, care and vocation that continues to influence how I think about professional life, public service and relationships with institutions.

ACTIVE INQUIRY

Faith, technology & public life

I continue to study how Christian thought engages questions of technology, work, education, governance, stewardship, justice and human flourishing without reducing complex public questions to slogans.

This overlaps naturally with systems research: values influence institutions, institutions influence technology, and technology can amplify or constrain the choices people are able to make.
