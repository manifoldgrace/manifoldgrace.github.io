2. All nine official programme learning outcomes

  1. Identify and explain core AI principles, technical underpinnings and societal impacts.
  2. Evaluate ethical and societal implications across technology, law, policy, philosophy and business.
  3. Analyse implementation case studies and propose viable solutions.
  4. Apply ethical theories and frameworks to real AI cases.
  5. Develop and critique AI policy and regulation using philosophical, ethical and technical knowledge.
  6. Demonstrate critical thinking in clear, coherent debate.
  7. Communicate AI ethics to policymakers, technologists and the general public.
  8. Conduct independent research culminating in a well-argued thesis.
  9. Collaborate across academic and professional sectors on AI governance.

These outcomes are paraphrased for navigation; the programme specification remains the authoritative wording. Assessment examples include written exercises, essays, quizzes, case studies and problems. 6. Dissertation and viva rehearsal — explain, defend, revise

These are optional doctoral-style oral defence exercises, not an asserted compulsory MA viva. For each prompt, first give an unaided two-minute explanation; then check citations, counterarguments and possible disconfirmation. You may type a private practice response below and copy it for your own records.

  1. State the precise research question in one sentence. What is explicitly outside its scope?
  2. What existing results make the research necessary, and what exact limitation remains unresolved?
  3. What is the thesis contribution: conceptual, empirical, methodological, normative or technical?
  4. Why is the chosen method appropriate? Which alternative design would challenge your conclusions?
  5. What are your operational definitions for bias, personhood, delegation, values and accountability?
  6. Which evidence would falsify your preferred explanation?
  7. What is the strongest objection from moral philosophy or political theory?
  8. How do you distinguish correlation, mechanism, causality and normative judgment?
  9. What does the literature already establish, and how does your work differ?
  10. How have you addressed sampling, construct validity, measurement error and reproducibility?
  11. What are the governance, privacy, fairness and participant-risk implications?
  12. What remains uncertain; which claims cannot yet be made?
  13. How would you defend your conclusions without access to an AI assistant?
  14. How would the study be extended to PhD scale without merely restating the MA dissertation?

Six-part self-validation rubric

CriterionSelf-test
Recall and date precisionCan I give the correct year, name and nature of the event, and distinguish online preprint from peer-reviewed publication?
Definition accuracyCan I define the term and distinguish it from adjacent concepts without prompts?
Argument and counterargumentCan I reconstruct the strongest opposing position rather than a weak version?

Gap-first protocol: (1) precise proposition; (2) strongest related work and first-publication dates; (3) established result; (4) demonstrable limitation; (5) falsifiable hypothesis; (6) study design; (7) measurable contribution; (8) limitations and rival explanations; (9) publication and provenance trail.
