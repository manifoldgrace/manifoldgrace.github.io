Historical reconstruction: shared evidentiary standard

1. Before sustained Atlantic contact

Material culture, languages, settlement, land, ritual and household organisation. Chronology must be particular to each region.

2. Contact and regional transformation

Trace trade, conflict, alliances, migration, religious practice and institutional change; avoid assuming every custom remained fixed.

3. Colonial administration and later records

Compare court files, land claims, mission registers, civil registration and modern oral histories with earlier evidence.

Evidence rules: primary document or artefact; scholarly interpretation; attributed oral tradition; and unresolved or conflicting claim are distinct categories. A surname, contemporary ORCID profile or chiefly title does not itself prove kinship.

AI and the records future generations inherit

AI can help make hard-to-read archives searchable, but should not silently construct relationships. A useful system preserves the source image and transcription, labels uncertainty, and permits human correction.

  1. Capture: digitise letters, registers, court records, oral-history recordings, photographs and maps with consent and provenance.
  2. Transcribe: use handwriting recognition and language support, retaining the original alongside any AI-generated text.
  3. Resolve identities cautiously: compare names, aliases, places and dates; propose candidate links without auto-merging people.
  4. Map evidence: connect each relationship claim to exact document pages or oral testimonies, plus corroboration and contradictions.
  5. Preserve family control: restrict information about living people, sensitive lineage claims and culturally restricted knowledge; support corrections and takedowns.
  6. Export for future generations: use open formats, accessible backups and version histories; consider GEDCOM 7 for family-tree exchange and Records in Contexts for wider archival relationships.

Possible prototype: Evidence-linked ancestry graph

Person → claimed relationship → supporting record → repository URL/page → date → reviewer → verification status. Add alternative spellings, language/script, maternal and paternal links, opposing evidence, and a revision history. ORCID is a researcher identifier, not a genealogy registry.
