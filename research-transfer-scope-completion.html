Research completion does not require perpetual perfection

A bounded research contribution is complete when its specified questions, methods, observations, limitations, risks, decisions, evidence provenance and handover are adequately documented. It need not solve every adjoining scientific problem or wait for all future research to conclude. Uncompleted independent validation, comparative architecture tests and production release must remain explicitly pending; completing a documentation phase does not convert those claims into verified findings.

This page is a research-transfer index and perimeter declaration, not a new benchmark report or a replacement for the original AI Research Lab. The underlying experimental records, reviewer packet, original feature proposals and previously dated work remain authoritative.

Next experimental deliverable: Phase 6 protocol

The Phase 6 Comparative Research Protocol and Successor Decision Record translates the existing lessons into falsifiable architecture comparisons, preregistration-style evaluation conditions, error and safety metrics, a coverage-exception proposal, and bounded phase closure. It is a prospective experiment specification, not an additional completed validation result. Original milestones and evidence remain authoritative below and in the laboratory.

Next bounded stage: validation before architectural comparison

  1. Freeze and identify source materials: confirm case IDs, source-file versions, model outputs, environment dependencies and hashes; preserve the v0.8 and v1.1 records unchanged.
  2. Check review eligibility: use the existing v0.9 12-case packet as a methodology pilot, not a new independent blind generalisation benchmark, because its authorship and exposure are documented.
  3. Obtain genuinely independent labels: involve two separate reviewers without sharing predictions, with consent, relevant expertise, a clear rubric and protected submission routes; log disagreements and unanswered cases.
  4. Construct a further non-exposed test set: independently sourced and controlled for licences, privacy, language, domain, scope and temporal complexity; freeze before model scoring.
  5. Evaluate baseline A, hybrid B and evidence-first C: keep conditions, endpoints, datasets, acceptance thresholds and compute accounting comparable; label any unimplemented candidate as proposed.
  6. Publish a stage report: pinpoint observed findings, negative results, trade-offs, safety limitations, unresolved questions and evidence-grounded architectural decisions.
  7. Close or transfer: mark the bounded stage complete, conditionally complete or blocked; preserve future hypotheses as a successor agenda without pretending they were tested.

Handover package minimum: research question and novelty boundary; dataset and permissions; code and dependency versions; original source records; reference labels and their independence status; test definitions and outputs; error analyses; architectural decision records; safety/legal review; contact and correction history; and a one-page successor decision tree. If any component is unavailable, mark it explicitly rather than reconstructing original evidence and calling it authentic.

What a defensible “360° AI research” perimeter requires

There is no valid numerical percentage for “everything historically, currently and in the future” without a finite, independently justified population of research topics and a coverage method. AI research is open-ended; future discoveries cannot be exhaustively catalogued in advance. Instead, the following ten dimensions identify coverage obligations and known blind spots. The site currently maps portions of these dimensions; it is not a complete, independently validated world census of AI research.

1. Temporal

Historical origins; as-of-date evidence; 2026 current state; future hypotheses

Limit: Do not infer a complete future or comprehensive historical census.

2. Disciplinary

Computing, mathematics, philosophy, linguistics, information science, law, policy, economics, sociology, safety and education

Limit: Subject affinity is not assessed achievement or specialist authority.

3. Sectoral

Public services, health, justice, finance, education, industry, science, media, infrastructure and civil society

Limit: Sector-specific obligations and empirical conditions differ.

4. Institutional

Individual, team, supplier, organisation, regulator, state and international coordination

Limit: Accountability can cross institutional boundaries.

5. Research lifecycle

Problem discovery, literature, evidence collection, ontology, design, experiment, review, integration, monitoring, archival transfer

Limit: Methods may be iterative; progress is not necessarily linear.

6. AI capability

Learning, prediction, language, multimodal, robotics, agents, reasoning, retrieval and simulation

Limit: Tool use and benchmark success are task-specific.

7. Evaluation

Functional correctness, semantic validity, external review, reliability, fairness, robustness, calibration, security and cost

Limit: No one pass rate substitutes for all dimensions.

8. Epistemic state

Verified record, developer-authored result, independent finding, provisional inference, hypothesis, unknown and disputed

Limit: Maintain provenance, confidence and competing explanations.

9. Governance

Identification, ownership, legal applicability, transparency, privacy, safety, monitoring, remedy and retirement

Limit: Do not assume every AI deployment has identical statutory requirements.

10. Professional transfer

Study themes, prior work, QA, published research, feature requests, CPD, professional bodies, collaboration

Limit: Document relevance without conferring titles, membership grades or credits.

Formal coverage reporting should therefore use bounded statements such as “34 of 40 defined historical topics have at least one primary source dated by X” or “8 of 10 selected disciplines have an expert-reviewed synthesis,” rather than an invented percentage of universal AI knowledge.

Additional phases and non-linear connections

Research can involve pre-discovery framing and terminology negotiation, rights and consent review, horizon scanning, historical archaeology, community co-design, epistemic audit, requirements and threat modelling, experimental construction, governance approvals, implementation, comparison, red teaming, stakeholder interpretation, correction, policy transfer, procurement, public communication, retirement and future re-evaluation. These phases can recur or proceed in parallel. The relation between phases should be encoded as dependencies, feedback loops, contradictions and evidence updates—not a single irreversible sequence.
