The wider shape of society

This is an explanatory coverage framework, not an exhaustive register of every organisation. It preserves the earlier distinction between government proper, the arm’s length state, territorial and service delivery institutions, delegated delivery and constitutional or historical machinery.

Structural audit and further findings

The framework is broad, but not exhaustive. The complete recovered directory and its associated lists remain below. These additions distinguish supported categories, historical guidance, current organisational changes and research still required. The twelve layers are analytical views: one organisation may belong in more than one view.

Verified machinery-of-government changes

The official Prime Minister profile records Andy Burnham taking office on 20 July 2026. The Cabinet Office fact sheet describes the Office for the Prime Minister and Cabinet (OPMC), No.10 North, AI functions moving to the Cabinet Office, and a proposed OneGov Delivery Agency. DBT is described as being renamed the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (BIST), while DSIT functions are redistributed. This is more precise than describing both departments as legally dissolved and replaced. Announced transfers and proposed bodies must be distinguished from confirmed operational commencement.

Additional subcategories and cross-cutting relationships

Make joint authorities explicit alongside councils and combined authorities, including shared fire, policing and transport arrangements. Cross-border bodies need links to the affected devolved administrations rather than an assumed single vertical chain. Crown NDPBs and Special Health Authorities deserve explicit subtype fields. An unnamed subtype is a structural refinement; it is not proof that all corresponding organisations are absent.

The 2016 handbook recognises Crown NDPBs, cross-border consultation/reporting, Special Health Authorities and department-specific entities. Its legacy examples and policy statements require current body-specific verification before being used as current classifications.

  • Cabinet Office classification handbook (2016): historical policy guidance
Classification corrections and historical policy boundaries
  • Independent Monitoring Boards: handbook sections 2.3.2 and 3.6.2 discuss department-specific characteristics alongside NDPB classification. Preserve both historical descriptions rather than deleting one.
  • Multiple administrative classifications: section 7.1 discourages new dual classifications, but allows existing arrangements to remain where review finds changing them inappropriate.
  • Trading funds: section 6.2 describes a financing mechanism and discourages new ALB trading funds; it does not prohibit all new non-ALB funds or abolish existing funds.
  • Tribunal NDPBs: the handbook states that its tribunal subtype is unavailable for new bodies and describes new functions within HMCTS. This historical policy is not a universal appeal route or proof that every UK tribunal belongs to HMCTS.
  • Electoral Commission: parliamentary accountability, including devolved arrangements, must remain explicit. Directory inclusion does not establish ministerial control.
  • ESFA: closed on 31 March 2025; functions transferred to DfE. Retain ESFA in historical records and use the current responsible organisation for operational mapping.

Counts and directory dynamics

The recovered August capture and October snapshot each contain 618 top-level entries. The agency/public-body count moves from 421 to 419, high-profile groups from 131 to 132, and public corporations from 19 to 20. These are measured differences, not explanations of their cause. DCMS associated listings change from 42 to 44; identify the individual entries and effective changes before attributing this to creation, consolidation, transfer or ONS reclassification.

“Works with” relationships may record administrative associations; they are neither automatically statutory sponsorship nor necessarily superficial. Check each relationship against legislation, framework documents and accounts.

    Separate fields for a reliable institutional record

    Keep the following fields independent: organisation identifier and name; predecessor/successor; legal basis and legal personality; Crown status; administrative classification; ONS sector and decision date; territorial remit; funding/levy/fee basis; staffing status; sponsor; funder; commissioner; provider; statutory duty holder; decision maker; regulator; complaint body; appeal body; source and effective dates.

    Neither separate legal personality plus departmental funding nor a particular staff type proves Crown NDPB status. More than 50% cost recovery does not alone establish public-corporation classification: ONS also assesses control, qualifying sales and qualitative market conditions over time. A trading fund needs its own establishment evidence. Levy funding should be recorded as a financial attribute, with the specific ONS transaction decision; do not assume every statutory levy is direct taxation.

    • ONS market and non-market classification methodology
    Register coverage and limits
    • Local and territorial institutions: combine official geographical identifiers with legislation, establishment orders and authority records. A boundary/code database is not a complete record of responsibilities.
    • Health: ODS publishes organisational reference data with geographical coverage varying by product. Its download catalogue includes England and Wales, and Scotland, Northern Ireland and Isle of Man material. It is not correctly described as England-only, nor as the only valid source. Reconcile national health-system registers and distinguish organisations from sites.
    • Education: GIAS is an England register. Use devolved sources for their own school systems; do not assume England’s academy-trust model exists identically elsewhere. Higher education, research and qualifications need additional sources.
    • Civil society: the Charity Commission register covers registered charities in England and Wales; exempt, excepted and other unregistered charities require other evidence. Add OSCR and CCNI for Scotland and Northern Ireland. Companies House alone does not cover all legal forms or all civil society.
    • Legal status: neither a Gazette notice, an estimate, a directory nor a register alone proves all aspects of establishment, accountability and operation.

    Detailed twelve-layer responsibility views

    These are structural prompts, not case-specific legal advice or claims that one funding and appeal chain applies to every institution.

    1. People and households

    Institutions and scope

    Individuals, families, informal carers, neighbourhoods and unconstituted groups. These are not government bodies.

    Responsibility and redress distinctions

    Map the service and decision affecting the person, rather than assigning everyone one complaint body. Council social-care complaints may reach LGSCO within its jurisdiction; welfare decisions may have statutory reconsideration and appeal routes.

    Coverage still to reconcile

    People-level datasets and service-specific routes remain separate from organisational directories.

      2. Constitutional institutions

      Institutions and scope

      The Crown, legislatures, courts and parliamentary watchdogs.

      Responsibility and redress distinctions

      Separate constitutional accountability from executive sponsorship. Parliamentary privilege, court appeals, judicial review and complaints about administration are different routes; no universal High Court route is asserted.

      Coverage still to reconcile

      Record each institution’s legal basis, funding and jurisdiction individually; independent estimates are not a blanket rule for the whole layer.

        3. Government proper

        Institutions and scope

        Ministerial departments, Cabinet structures and executive offices, including OPMC and No.10 North.

        Responsibility and redress distinctions

        Separate policy minister, accounting officer and operational decision maker. Internal complaints, statutory appeals and ombudsman jurisdiction may differ. The Public Service Ombudsman normally requires an MP referral for covered UK-government complaints.

        Coverage still to reconcile

        Distinguish announced changes, effective transfers, operational units and separately established bodies.

          4. Arm’s-length and regulatory bodies

          Institutions and scope

          Executive agencies, NDPBs, NMDs, regulators and relevant public corporations; include Crown and department-specific exceptions.

          Responsibility and redress distinctions

          Record actual governance and sponsor arrangements. Independent adjudicators and ombudsman coverage are body-specific. Financial audit is not an individual appeal route.

          Coverage still to reconcile

          Reconcile administrative, legal and ONS classifications with dates and retained legacy categories.

            5. Devolved and territorial government

            Institutions and scope

            Devolved legislatures/executives, English combined and combined county authorities, and cross-border relationships.

            Responsibility and redress distinctions

            Reserved/devolved competence, shared sponsorship and transferred powers need separate links. English combined authorities do not automatically share devolved-nation ombudsman arrangements.

            Coverage still to reconcile

            Complete national and regional registers, legal orders and cross-border responsibilities remain to be reconciled.

              6. Local democracy and services

              Institutions and scope

              County, district, unitary, metropolitan and London borough councils, parish/town councils and joint authorities.

              Responsibility and redress distinctions

              Distinguish councillors, executives, statutory officers and delegated service decisions. Use the relevant council process, statutory appeal or ombudsman jurisdiction; not every issue is an LGSCO complaint.

              Coverage still to reconcile

              Record service boundaries, joint committees, authority-owned entities and changes over time.

                7. Health, social care and public health

                Institutions and scope

                ICBs, NHS trusts and foundation trusts, Special Health Authorities, local-authority care and public health, and independent/voluntary providers.

                Responsibility and redress distinctions

                Separate commissioning, provision and safety regulation. CQC does not resolve individual complaints. Eligible unresolved NHS complaints in England may reach the Public Service Ombudsman; care and devolved routes differ.

                Coverage still to reconcile

                ODS is an identifier/reference source, not a full record of duties, contracts or every patient complaint route.

                  8. Education, research and skills

                  Institutions and scope

                  Schools, academy trusts, colleges, universities, research bodies, qualification organisations and skills providers.

                  Responsibility and redress distinctions

                  DfE succeeds ESFA functions; it is not the sole funder of all education and research. Inspectors/regulators are distinct from individual redress. OIA covers eligible complaints concerning participating providers within its scheme, not every education dispute.

                  Coverage still to reconcile

                  Add devolved systems, higher-education, research, skills and qualification registers beyond GIAS.

                    9. Justice, policing, fire and safety

                    Institutions and scope

                    Police forces and governance bodies, fire authorities, prosecutors, prisons, probation, courts and inspectorates.

                    Responsibility and redress distinctions

                    Separate operational leadership, governance, inspection, criminal/civil appeals and service complaints. Police review routes depend on the case and appropriate authority; not all escalate to IOPC. Prison/probation ombudsman coverage requires eligibility checks.

                    Coverage still to reconcile

                    Map territorial exceptions, court administration versus judicial decisions, and individual statutory appeal routes.

                      10. Delegated delivery and infrastructure

                      Institutions and scope

                      Utilities, transport bodies/operators, contractors, subcontractors and public/private delivery partnerships.

                      Responsibility and redress distinctions

                      Record contracting authority, commissioner, actual provider and duty holder separately. Sector regulation, consumer redress, contract enforcement and judicial routes must not be collapsed into one chain.

                      Coverage still to reconcile

                      Contracts, procurement records, licences and subcontracting links remain additional evidence requirements.

                        11. Markets, professions and civil society

                        Institutions and scope

                        Companies, partnerships, sole traders, mutuals, trade unions, professional bodies, charities, faith and community organisations.

                        Responsibility and redress distinctions

                        Legal form, charitable status and public control may overlap. Registration does not establish service quality or give Companies House general consumer-redress powers. Use issue-specific regulators and complaint schemes.

                        Coverage still to reconcile

                        Include unregistered and exempt bodies, devolved charity registers, mutuals and other forms outside Companies House.

                          12. Oversight, records and historical machinery

                          Institutions and scope

                          Auditors, ombudsmen, information regulators, archives, committees and historical records.

                          Responsibility and redress distinctions

                          Separate audit scrutiny, maladministration complaints, information decisions and statutory appeals. ICO-related tribunal routes concern eligible decisions, not every information dispute. PAC scrutiny is not a universal individual remedy.

                          Coverage still to reconcile

                          Preserve predecessors, abolished bodies, legal instruments, source versions and effective dates; oversight bodies may also appear in other layers.

                            What is possibly still missing — completion checklist
                            • Full council, joint-authority and combined-authority inventories with legal powers and effective boundaries.
                            • Complete devolved registers and shared/cross-border relationships.
                            • Health organisations, sites, commissioners and care providers reconciled by identifier.
                            • Education, skills, research and qualifications registers beyond schools.
                            • Police/fire governance, courts, tribunals and case-specific appeal jurisdictions.
                            • Authority-owned companies, mutuals, partnerships, statutory/chartered bodies, exempt charities, faith and informal community groups.
                            • Public contracts, subcontractors, concessions, licences, levy schemes and actual funding flows.
                            • Temporary taskforces, expert committees, unclassified entities and operational commencement dates.
                            • Predecessor/successor histories, aliases, duplicates and dated classification decisions.
                            • Sources for each asserted relationship and a documented reconciliation against the relevant register’s scope.

                            These are remaining audit areas, not a claim that every example is missing from the existing directory. A confirmed omission requires a named entity or relationship, evidence it falls within the declared scope, and evidence it is absent. Absence of evidence is recorded as unresolved.

                              Evidence labels and audit method

                              Use DOCUMENTED for a directly sourced fact; SUPPORTED for an interpretation consistent with identified evidence; UNDER RESEARCH for an unresolved claim. Classify differences as confirmed omission, possible omission, scope exclusion, historical change, classification difference or unexplained directory difference. Keep the cause unresolved until supported.

                              Completeness applies to a defined source capture, not automatically to all of society. The original August source and October directory remain intact. Incoming audit prose is a research lead; its unsupported universal rules and purported verbatim quotations are not adopted as authoritative evidence.

                                Coverage and omissions

                                • Recovered source complete: the saved expanded August directory has been recovered through its final devolved-government section.
                                • Current source complete: all top-level entries and the nested relationship lists present on the retrieved October GOV.UK page are included.
                                • Outside this directory’s scope: full lists of councils, NHS providers, schools, universities, police and fire services, private contractors, congregations and civil organisations require separate registers.
                                • Classification difference: directory categories, Cabinet Office administrative classifications and ONS economic classifications answer different questions.
                                • Possible omission: absence from one directory is a lead to investigate, not proof that an institution was omitted improperly.
                                • Historical scope: predecessor departments and abolished or transferred bodies need date-specific evidence; the saved snapshots retain the source names.
                                • Insufficient evidence: the full original conversation and every underlying statute or contract are not present in this record.

                                Tracing responsibility

                                For any issue record the policy owner, funding route, commissioning body, provider, statutory duty, decision maker, regulator, complaint route and independent appeal route. A contractual relationship, policy relationship and legal duty may point to different organisations.

                                Use the correspondence register to connect an enquiry to its response, referral and outcome. Acknowledgement and referral remain distinct from delivery.
