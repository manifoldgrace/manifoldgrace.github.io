02 · Payment gateways & money into the accountVerify payments, reconcile settlements, track fees and protect account access.

The browser success screen is not proof of payment. A server verifies the payment provider’s event and authoritative status before releasing goods or access. Payment collection, a recorded sale, gateway balance and bank payout are different states.

ControlSpecification / evidence
Merchant setupConfirm legal seller, supported business activity, currency, bank ownership, verification and settlement terms.
CheckoutUse provider-hosted payment fields or checkout; keep card numbers and secret keys out of website code, analytics and logs. Confirm applicable PCI scope with the provider.
LifecycleTrack pending, authorised, captured, failed, refunded and disputed states; fulfilment and subscription states are separate.
ReliabilityVerify raw-body signatures and timestamp freshness; deduplicate event IDs and business effects. Handle retries, delayed events and out-of-order delivery. Queue work durably before acknowledging receipt.
SettlementMatch payment IDs → order IDs → payout IDs → bank transactions; explain fees, refunds, currency differences and reserves. Flag unmatched items.
AuthoritySeparate view, refund and payout permissions; require approval for sensitive changes. Alert on bank-account changes.
Commercial metricsGross sales, net revenue, cash received, fees, refunds, disputes, settlement delay and contribution margin. Use an accountant-approved treatment for tax and revenue recognition.
03 · GUI, backend & connector architectureA usable interface controls workflows through authenticated, observable service boundaries.

A Zapier-style connector subscribes to approved events or polls an API. A trigger starts a versioned workflow; filters check conditions; actions call approved services. The browser must never contain connector secrets or unrestricted payment credentials.

BoundaryWhat crosses itRequired protection
Public website → backendValidated request, session and venture identifierTLS, server-side authorisation, CSRF protection where relevant, rate limits and schema validation.
Provider → webhookEvent ID, signature, timestamp and minimum payloadVerify provider authenticity, reject stale/replayed requests and store durable processing state.
Workflow → external platformScoped API operation and correlation IDLeast-privilege OAuth/service account; secret vault; timeouts, backoff and quotas.
AI → action executorProposed structured actionAllowlisted tools, argument checks, explicit authority and human approval for consequential actions.
Services → GUISanitised result, processing state and errorsVenture-level isolation; no secrets, raw personal records or internal stack traces.
Failure and recovery path

Timeout → bounded retry with backoff → dead-letter queue → named operator → inspect and replay safely. Use transaction/outbox patterns where needed, idempotency keys for business effects, correlation IDs across services and an audit record of every replay. A connector does not guarantee exactly-once processing.

Salesforce / Einstein integration example

Website enquiry → authenticated ingestion → CRM record → approved transformation → analytics dataset → deployed prediction model → score displayed in a record or workflow → authorised follow-up. CRM Analytics connects and explores data; Einstein Discovery supplies predictive modelling capabilities in supported editions. The specific Sales Cloud Einstein analytics bundle has restrictions and is not interchangeable with the full analytics platform. Validate the exact licence, external-data permissions, costs, deployment method and connector contract.

Maintain a data catalogue: field definition, purpose, source, system of record, controller/processor role, sensitivity, quality threshold, refresh interval, recipients, location, retention and deletion route. Avoid silently combining unrelated ventures’ customer data.

07 · Machine learning signal pathTraining learns parameters; inference applies them to new eligible inputs.
Precisely what the model computes

An illustrative logistic model receives features x (for example enquiry age and expressed product interest). It computes z = b + Σwᵢxᵢ and p = 1 / (1 + exp(−z)). Training adjusts weights w and intercept b to reduce prediction loss on historical labelled examples. The output p estimates the chosen outcome under the training conditions; it is not a guarantee or an explanation of causation.

Fit imputers, encoders and scalers on training data only. Split by time and, where necessary, customer/group to prevent leakage. Never include a future purchase or post-outcome status among the features. Tune on validation data and evaluate the locked model on held-out test data.

Assess calibration, precision/recall and relevant subgroup error; compare with rules and a simple baseline. Choose thresholds by consequences and capacity, not an arbitrary confidence number. Keep a human route for missing inputs, out-of-distribution cases and consequential decisions. Obtain independent evaluation data rather than assuming past actions represent unbiased labels.

Forecasting, LLMs and small-model routing are different

Forecasting estimates future quantities using time-aware backtests, intervals and seasonality; track MAE or another appropriate error against a naïve baseline. An LLM predicts token sequences and may propose tool calls. Retrieval supplies permitted context; it does not itself retrain the LLM. A small-model router can delegate uncertain decisions to a larger model, but confidence must be evaluated against real task outcomes. Larger-model escalation is not human approval.

Confirm the current rules and relevant commencement provisions for the actual use case; do not infer that a generic template establishes compliance.

Transformation tracking and project controls

Track scope, milestones, dependencies, accountable roles, risks, assumptions, issues, decisions, budget actual/forecast, change requests and evidence links. Separately track benefits: baseline → target → actual → measurement date → owner. Include adoption, training, time saved net of review, failure rate, service quality, accessibility, customer impact and operating cost.

Change requests record purpose, affected architecture/data, cost, delivery impact, new risk, approver and rollback. Procurement or payment milestones depend on accepted evidence, not a demonstration alone. Review unresolved defects and access expiry at every gate.

18 · Sources, definitions & review boundariesDistinguish vendor capability, practical guidance, legal duties and illustrative design.

Source review: 26 October 2026. Architectural examples and milestone gates are proposed reference designs. Product editions, duties and interfaces require checking at implementation.

  • Stripe: webhook verification, retries and duplicate handling
  • OWASP: webhook threat controls
  • NIST AI RMF: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage — voluntary framework, not certification.
  • UK government: AI Risk Management Toolkit
  • ICO: AI and data protection — guidance under review following legal changes.
  • Salesforce: CRM Analytics and Sales Cloud Einstein analytics limitations.

Terminology: GUI = graphical user interface; API = application programming interface; CRM = customer relationship management; ERP = enterprise resource planning; BI = business intelligence; DPIA = data protection impact assessment; RACI = responsible, accountable, consulted and informed; MLOps = machine-learning operations.

Learning check
  1. Why is a checkout success page insufficient evidence for fulfilment?
  2. Which workflow steps require rules, and which may benefit from prediction?
  3. What evidence distinguishes model training from inference?
  4. Who can accept residual risk and approve a release?

Answers: verify the provider’s authoritative payment state; use deterministic rules for known conditions and evaluate models for uncertainty; training changes parameters while inference applies a versioned model; the named authorities recorded in the project and governance registers.
