BOTANICAL PRODUCTS · CONCEPT DEVELOPMENT

Grace in Eden · Botanical Beauts

GRACE IN EDENEST. 2026BOTANICAL BEAUTS

An English-garden-inspired botanical soap and personal-care product concept, developed around a decorative mould, considered formulations, and carefully presented packaging.

Early collection concepts: Rose Garden and Apple Orchard, including a translucent glycerine-style Apple Orchard bar. Original concept boards explored a floral label, botanical green and cream palette, a serif wordmark, a soap mould design and wrapped or boxed product presentation.

Status: design and product concepts established; production, testing and sales not yet launched. A soap-mould order of £34.99 was reported as confirmed in August 2026 with delivery expected around 10 September, but the moulds had not arrived as of October 2026. The order requires checking before a production timetable is set.

Expand development milestones, readiness checks and next steps
  1. Retrieve the order confirmation, seller messages, shipping/tracking information and payment record; resolve the missing mould delivery or seek the appropriate remedy.
  2. Review the original Grace in Eden brand boards and approve the final spelling, logo artwork, labels and collection names.
  3. Define intended soap product category, claims, formula and ingredient sourcing; verify permissions and supplier information.
  4. Before selling cosmetics in Great Britain, establish the required cosmetic product safety assessment, responsible person, product information file, notification and legally compliant ingredient labelling and claims.
  5. Trial the intended moulds and manufacturing method in an appropriate setting using suitable training, equipment and safety measures; document repeatability and batch records.
  6. Validate packaging dimensions, durability, presentation, mailing protection and recyclable-material claims with actual materials.
  7. Calculate full unit economics including materials, packaging, compliance, labour, seller fees, taxes, shipping, breakage and refunds.
  8. Set up a small online product listing only after product compliance and testing are complete; start with limited demand testing rather than committing to unnecessary stock.
  9. Review customer feedback, fulfilment quality and actual net results before expanding to additional botanical products.

Artwork status: earlier branding and product-board imagery has been located in the private working collection. This public wordmark is a simplified presentation, not a claim that finished product photography or a final commercial logo has been uploaded.

BUSINESS PROCESS MANAGEMENT · AI SOLUTIONS · PROJECT ANALYSIS

Agency.ai — designing an end-to-end agent service

Agency.ai is an emerging business-process-management and artificial-intelligence solutions venture. Its interface currently presents a catalogue of 50 potential agent-handled services across customer support, sales, marketing, administration, research, data, websites, operations, finance administration and education. A client can explore workflows and express interest in a scoped pilot.

This development record examines how a business problem can be translated into a bounded, testable agent service. It serves as an evolving project portfolio and a reusable build framework, not a claim that all proposed integrations and agents are active.

The proposed architecture

Client journey: discover service → define outcome → submit brief → agree scope and approvals → test pilot → review results.

Technical journey: public website → authenticated application/API → organisation-scoped database → workflow orchestrator → agent and approved tools → human review → logs and reporting.

Build process — twelve stages

Open each step for its purpose and the evidence to retain when reviewing the implementation.

01 · Define the operational problem

Identify business owner, trigger, volume, current time/cost, bottlenecks, boundaries and measurable outcomes. Compare an AI agent with a simpler rules-based workflow.

Record for the portfolio: Business case and baseline.

02 · Map the process

Document the current and proposed workflow, hand-offs, decisions, exceptions and people accountable for outcomes.

Record for the portfolio: As-is and to-be process diagrams.

03 · Choose a scoped pilot

Select one repeatable service, narrow its permissions and define success, exclusions and stopping conditions.

Record for the portfolio: Pilot brief and acceptance criteria.

04 · Design inputs and outputs

Specify request forms, required fields, knowledge sources, output formats, validation rules and missing-data responses.

Record for the portfolio: Data contract and sample cases.

05 · Plan architecture

Separate the public GitHub Pages interface from an authenticated application, API, database, agent runtime and third-party connectors. Draw data flows and trust boundaries.

Record for the portfolio: System and data-flow diagrams.

06 · Implement identity and tenancy

Use an established identity provider, email verification, MFA and server-enforced roles. Keep each client organisation's data isolated; do not store passwords or private datasets in static pages.

Record for the portfolio: Auth test results and access matrix.

07 · Connect authorised tools

Create narrowly scoped integrations for email, CRM, documents or other permitted services. Manage secrets server-side, validate incoming data and limit read/write privileges.

Record for the portfolio: Connector inventory and permissions.

08 · Implement the agent workflow

Define agent instructions, tool-call schemas, bounded execution steps, structured outputs, uncertainty rules, retry limits and escalation points. Use deterministic automation where it fits.

Record for the portfolio: Workflow specification and versioned prompts.

09 · Add approvals and safeguards

Require human approval for consequential actions, including payments, external publishing and sensitive account changes. Include privacy notices, retention rules, audit trails and failure recovery.

Record for the portfolio: Risk register and approval checkpoints.

10 · Test systematically

Test normal cases, malformed inputs, missing permissions, ambiguous requests, prompt injection, privacy leaks, service failures and unexpected tool outputs; compare results with a human-reviewed baseline.

Record for the portfolio: Test matrix and reproducible findings.

11 · Deploy and monitor

Deploy the backend securely; configure environments, version control, logging, monitoring, alerts, rollback and cost limits. Confirm the production service actually works before marketing it as active.

Record for the portfolio: Release checklist and operational runbook.

12 · Evaluate and iterate

Compare baseline with measured time, quality, escalation rate, errors, satisfaction and cost. Record what was built, changed, deferred or rejected and why.

Record for the portfolio: Post-pilot evaluation and portfolio reflection.

Evaluation questions for the project portfolio
  • Why was the problem selected, and was an agent necessary?
  • Which architecture, API, model and orchestration trade-offs were made?
  • What actually worked in tests, and which failures or constraints changed the design?
  • Were privacy, access controls, source provenance and approval boundaries demonstrable?
  • What measurable value did the pilot create relative to the previous process?
  • Which assumptions should be revisited in the next iteration?
