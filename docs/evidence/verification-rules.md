# Claim-level verification and corrections

**Working protocol v0.1 — 2026-11-06**

1. Define an atomic, falsifiable claim with entity, jurisdiction, date range, source population and measurement.
2. Identify primary sources where available; distinguish an organisation reporting a number from independent validation of its underlying data.
3. Preserve source URL/identifier, publication and retrieval dates; record applicable amendments and effective dates for law.
4. Assess whether the source supports the exact wording, not merely the general topic.
5. Record contrary evidence, sampling limits, uncertainty and absences in coverage.
6. Assign one of: `supported`, `contradicted`, `disputed`, `insufficient_evidence`. Track human review separately.
7. For corrections, append an event containing the earlier claim version, correction, rationale, source and reviewer. Do not silently erase historical versions.
8. Label hypotheses and disputed conclusions explicitly. Missing accessible records never by themselves demonstrate that an event did not happen.
9. Record who can access a source, and apply lawful privacy, confidentiality, copyright and security constraints.
10. Never automatically label missing records criminal, or treat a file hash as proof of source authenticity.

A statement is “independently validated” only when the relevant underlying claim has been checked against sufficiently independent evidence; a corporate blog may verify what the company claimed, not the underlying outcome. Reviews cannot guarantee absolute historical completeness.
