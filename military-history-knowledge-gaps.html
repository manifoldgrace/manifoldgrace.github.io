Military History: Truth, Knowledge Gaps & Prevention

Established 12 November 2026 | Future-team research agenda | Exploratory, not completed findings

Core inquiry: How did historical knowledge gaps arise in military conflicts, how can their causes be demonstrated rather than assumed, and what proportion of preventable gaps can be reduced through responsible contemporaneous documentation?

How historical gaps form

Different mechanisms require different responses: testimony never elicited; family or generational non-transmission; records never created; accidental destruction; deliberate destruction or suppression (only where evidenced); classification and access restrictions; displacement; language and translation barriers; unrepresentative institutional archives; and digital loss or manipulation. A missing record does not, by itself, establish concealment. A source's survival does not establish its accuracy.

Historical example: UK First World War military service records were substantially lost to bombing and fire during the Second World War. This is an established archive-loss mechanism distinct from veterans choosing not to discuss personal experiences.

Application to conflicts affecting people in 2026

Iran and the Gulf: As of November 2026, reports describe an unresolved US–Iran conflict and regional maritime and security incidents. The immediate research opportunity is independently documented chronology, separated civilian and official perspectives, protected testimony and careful preservation of verifiable public reporting. Neither reported claims nor inferred motives should be treated as independently verified facts without corroboration.

Sudan: Continued fighting and displacement create immediate risks of lost testimony, disrupted family histories and missing-person records. Prioritise protection, consent, representation and non-exposure of vulnerable witnesses.

Gaza and the occupied Palestinian territory: Documentation needs to distinguish periods of ceasefire from ongoing humanitarian harm, individual incidents, missing-person records, access restrictions and disputed claims.

Other conflicts: Apply a consistent, updatable inclusion criterion: independently documented armed violence, affected civilians or service members, observable record-loss risks, and an ethically feasible public research question. Do not imply that the examples above constitute a complete conflict inventory.

Evidence-first protocol for future research teams

For each proposition, preserve exact original wording, date, location at safe precision, source identity or protected anonymity, source type, consent and lawful access basis, original-language version, translation caveats, independent corroboration, contradictory evidence, uncertainty, archive gaps, changes/corrections and retrieval references. Maintain secure restricted archives for sensitive testimony and publish only appropriately verified, non-identifying summaries. Do not collect or publish live tactical positions, real-time movements, or identifying details that could endanger civilians or witnesses.

Falsification test: What evidence, if discovered, would refute the proposed explanation of the gap? Distinguish actual record loss from failures of indexing, transmission, access or interpretation. Outcome measures might include proportion of claims with retrievable primary sources, corroboration coverage, time to correct false claims, and continuity of accessible records after platform or institutional change.
