Purpose and evidence standards

The watchlist exists to investigate real business formation and operating conditions before inferring that a proposed Manifold Grace product could launch on optimism alone. For each company distinguish money invested, loans borrowed, grants awarded, revenue earned, profit reported, cash generated, and assets sold. Do not describe a customer contract as government investment without proof. When documents are incomplete, record an explicit gap rather than manufacture a founding narrative.

Research status: Three source-grounded opening dossiers and a twenty-one-company research queue. Being on the watchlist does not imply endorsement, partnership, independent audit or that every financing description has already been verified.

Palantir Technologies Source-grounded first dossier

Founded in 2003, Palantir initially built software for intelligence analysts and later expanded into commercial enterprise markets. Its business illustrates long product development cycles, institutional sales, data integration and the governance of consequential information.

Documented milestones: Founded 2003; its prospectus identifies Gotham for intelligence agencies and Foundry for enterprise customers; began public trading 30 September 2020. It reported 2025 revenue of US$4.475 billion, of which US$2.402 billion came from government customers and US$2.073 billion from commercial customers.

Finance questions still requiring archival tracing: Founder financing amounts; timing, instruments and terms of early investments; customer-funded development; rounds and dilution; cash flow and profitability chronology. Do not classify In-Q-Tel investment as a loan or mistake a government customer contract for grant funding.

Engineering questions: How are data access rules, ontology, applications, models and operational decisions separated? What is visible from public APIs and demonstrations, versus proprietary? What safeguards are documented and independently evaluated?

Governance and institutional questions: Evidence for procurement outcomes; customer benefit; privacy, public trust, supplier dependence and independent scrutiny. Record documented disputes with sources and responses rather than assume a single interpretation.

Manifold Grace transfer question: What is the minimum credible, ethically governed deployment that solves one institutional problem, and which elements of Palantir's scale cannot reasonably be copied by a small early-stage organisation?

First research action: Read the SEC prospectus product/revenue sections alongside the 2025 annual report; annotate one source-backed company timeline and one capital-versus-revenue distinction.

Zoho Source-grounded comparator

Zoho describes itself as privately held, profitable and independent of venture capital. Its earlier company history explains how a profitable niche product business financed expansion into adjacent software categories.

Reported trajectory: The company describes starting in network management, developing ManageEngine, and subsequently expanding into business and productivity tools while reinvesting income. These are company-reported accounts and need financial corroboration for quantitative analysis.

Lesson to investigate: How much product breadth can a firm fund through internal earnings, and what conditions make that route feasible? What trade-offs arise in ownership, speed, hiring and control?

First research action: Compare Zoho's reinvestment claims with product-release timelines and, if accessible, independently sourced financial information.

Atlassian Source-grounded comparator

Atlassian's founders describe beginning in 2002 with roughly US$10,000 on credit cards to buy computers; the company later took a US$60 million minority equity investment from Accel in 2010.

Financing distinction: The 2010 announcement describes the initial approach as self-funded and profitable before this external equity round. The investment was not simply a bank loan: it involved a minority ownership position and an investor joining the board.

Research question: Why did an already profitable organisation accept a large investor, and how did expansion, acquisitions and employee liquidity factor into that decision?

First research action: Annotate the 2010 capital event and compare it with the company's earlier product and distribution strategy.

360-degree research dimensions

  1. Origins and founders: date, context, problem, company structure and verified chronology.
  2. Starting capital: savings, loans, founder capital, angel/VC, public funding, contracts, strategic investors and dates.
  3. Revenue economics: first customers, pricing, margin, collection periods, cash generation, working capital and customer concentration.
  4. Product and engineering: original code, architecture, demos, APIs, security, R&D, patents and open-source licensing.
  5. Customers and procurement: buying process, value demonstrated, pilots, retention, tenders and implementation evidence.
  6. Ownership and governance: boards, voting, shareholder rights, executive incentives, independent controls and disclosures.
  7. Partnerships and suppliers: integration partners, strategic alliances, dependencies, distribution and potential conflicts.
  8. Scale and staffing: hiring, training, international expansion, operations, culture and organisational structure.
  9. Risk and accountability: audits, incidents, litigation, regulatory findings and responses; separate allegation from verified finding.
  10. Capital transitions: later financing, acquisitions, divestments, restructuring, IPO/direct listing or other liquidity events.
  11. Industry and influence: public presentations, recurring conferences, standards groups, professional associations and policy engagement.
  12. Evidence and media: annual reports, filings, white papers, articles, independent reviews, technical demonstrations and versioned source captures.
  13. Outcomes and distribution: benefit to customers, workers, investors, public institutions and communities; include negative and disputed evidence.
  14. Manifold Grace transfer: what is replicable at small scale, what depends on exceptional capital/access, and what ought not to be imitated.

Investigate, illustrate, then draw conclusions

Session 1: open one reliable original source and a small part of the company's system, timeline or financing story. Session 2: link a second independent source and locate the principal contradiction or gap. Session 3: add an annotated graphic, quotation within rights limits, timeline, code demonstration or comparative chart. Later: prepare sourced conclusions and revise when new evidence appears. Capture every source's publication and event dates; do not treat current websites as historical snapshots.

Suggested dossier fields: dated question, source and author, exact claim, source type, corroboration, contrary evidence, implication for the business model, applicability to Manifold Grace, unresolved question, experiment or diagram, and versioned result. Public information only: no employer confidential information or privileged customer data.

Career intelligence: learning business through employment

When considering roles in an organisation, distinguish credibility of present CV alignment from potential breadth of commercial learning. Technical documentation and enterprise support can match existing professional evidence; deployment strategy may expose more of customer discovery, implementation and expansion while imposing greater client-facing and travel demands. Corporate finance and business operations may reveal financing processes but have separate entry requirements.

Linked professional evidence: Digital CV · Professional profile · Technical portfolio. Company research is not an application, offer or employment endorsement.
