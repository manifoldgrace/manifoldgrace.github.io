FOUNDATIONAL RESEARCH PRINCIPLE

The Open Frontier of Knowledge and Innovation

Research and engineering developments may exist beyond the knowledge represented in an AI system's training data, accessible sources or current retrieval capabilities. This includes unpublished, privately developed, newly emerging or otherwise unrepresented work.

An AI system's inability to identify an advancement does not establish that it does not exist, or that a known approach represents the highest achievable level of scientific or technological development.

Frontier inquiry therefore requires epistemic openness: recognising the limits of available evidence, considering alternative architectures and independent discoveries, examining unfamiliar claims critically, and updating conclusions when new evidence becomes available.

Important distinction: assume that knowledge may be incomplete; do not assume that any particular unverified breakthrough exists. Openness to discovery should coexist with rigorous evaluation, uncertainty reporting and appropriate safety controls.

MY RESEARCH AVENUES · MA FOUNDATIONS · 5 NOVEMBER 2026

Urgent research gaps: developing my MA foundations

These ten avenues form part of my independent research, developing from my 2024–25 study of AI, Ethics and Society at Birkbeck, University of London. They connect technical mechanisms with philosophical questions, social research, institutional practice and the consequences of deployment. They sit within the wider arena index below; they are not ten new, separate project pages.

The programme specification combines technical knowledge with philosophical and ethical inquiry, case analysis, policy development, communication and independent research. The History and Foundations of AI syllabus supplies the more specific connections identified here. Specialist applications extend those foundations; they are not all named subjects in the taught syllabus.

  1. Real-world AI reliability and evaluation

    Research gap: Establish when test performance transfers to unfamiliar inputs, incomplete evidence and changing operational conditions. Investigate calibration, abstention, reproducibility and the difference between correct output and justified conclusions.

    MA foundation: Meeting 4, probabilities and prediction; Meeting 6, feedback loops and model collapse; Meeting 9, explainability and accountability. Existing laboratory and experiment records →

  2. Autonomous agents, cybersecurity and human control

    Research gap: Test permissions, resistance to malicious instructions, recovery after errors and the effectiveness of human intervention when agents act through tools.

    MA foundation: Meeting 3, artificial advisers and human–AI interaction; Meeting 9, design and accountability. Cybersecurity and agent-control engineering are further technical extensions. Existing agent architecture study →

  3. Evidence integrity, provenance and dependent revision

    Research gap: Distinguish independent corroboration from repeated reporting; preserve conditions and source ancestry; update affected conclusions when a premise changes while retaining historical records.

    MA foundation: Meetings 1–2, intelligence, meaning and rules; Meeting 5, data; Meeting 6, recursion; Meeting 9, accountability. Provenance systems and revision benchmarks develop these connections. Existing evidence architecture →

  4. Institutional accountability and effective oversight

    Research gap: Determine which review, challenge, escalation and correction arrangements actually protect people affected by automated decisions, and where responsibility becomes dispersed.

    MA foundation: Meeting 7, AI, power and the state; Meeting 8, unfairness and alternative approaches; Meeting 9, accountability. The programme also includes developing and critiquing AI policy and regulation. Existing systems and institutional research →

  5. Healthcare AI, evidence quality and research ethics

    Research gap: Examine evidence standards, sensitive-data use, unequal performance and oversight before findings or tools are relied upon in health settings.

    MA foundation: The programme's cross-domain ethical analysis, case-study and applied-framework outcomes; Meeting 9, accountable design. Clinical validation requires additional health-methods expertise and is an application beyond the named syllabus themes. Existing cross-sector evidence applications →

  6. Employment, income distribution and beneficial automation

    Research gap: Measure changes to work, income, entry routes and bargaining power; distinguish potential task exposure from observed displacement, and investigate how gains can be shared.

    MA foundation: Meeting 5, AI data work and data–compute–labour relationships; Meeting 7, power and inequality; Meeting 10, human welfare and material consequences. Existing labour-market research →

  7. Energy, computing efficiency and physical infrastructure

    Research gap: Investigate resource measurement, efficient computation and the environmental and distributional consequences of AI infrastructure. Distinguish scientific uncertainties from grid, procurement and construction delays.

    MA foundation: Meeting 10, the materiality of AI; Meeting 5, data, compute and labour. Hardware optimisation and power-system engineering are specialist extensions. Existing platform-design reference →

  8. Language, culture and unequal model performance

    Research gap: Test interpretation and reliability beyond dominant languages and well-represented cultural settings; investigate whose knowledge and experience are omitted from data and evaluation.

    MA foundation: Meeting 2, grounded cognition; Meeting 5, data and values; Meeting 8, fairness and counter-imaginaries; Meeting 10, colonial power. Existing research and culture study →

  9. Scientific discovery and independent verification

    Research gap: Establish whether AI-assisted literature analysis, hypotheses and experiments survive independent checking and improve research outcomes, rather than merely generating plausible material.

    MA foundation: Meeting 1, thinking machines; Meeting 4, prediction; Meeting 6, feedback loops; the programme's independent-research outcome. Domain-specific scientific validation is a further methodological extension. Existing evaluation protocol →

  10. Public communication, ambiguity and consequential omissions

    Research gap: Test whether assisted review preserves qualifications, identifies foreseeable misleading interpretations and improves readers' understanding without inventing intent or suppressing legitimate uncertainty.

    MA foundation: Meeting 2, rules and meaning; Meeting 3, AI interfaces; Meeting 9, design and accountability; the programme's communication outcome for diverse audiences. Existing semantic-integrity study →

Programme mapping and evidence for urgency

Programme basis: MA AI, Ethics and Society: Programme Specification 2024–25, revised 18 June 2024, course aims and structure (pp. 2–4), learning outcomes 1–9 (p. 6); and MA History and Foundations of AI, Meetings 1–10 (pp. 1–5). Both documents were supplied by Grace and read for this mapping; the PDFs are not republished here. Existing programme-reference atlas →

Current evidence: The International AI Safety Report 2026 identifies gaps in evaluation, uneven reliability and safeguards. WHO's 2026 report on AI-related health research identifies ethics and oversight gaps. The ILO's occupational-exposure research informs the distinction between potential exposure and observed employment outcomes. The IEA's Key Questions on Energy and AI and Modernising Grids in the Age of Electricity inform the infrastructure questions.

These are selected research priorities, not an official global ranking or claims that the gaps have been resolved. The mapping records intellectual continuity with my MA study and subsequent independent extensions. It does not change the status of existing research results or academic records.
