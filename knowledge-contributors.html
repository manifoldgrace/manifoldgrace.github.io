Knowledge Contributors & Participation

A proposed framework for inviting qualified specialists, historians, independent researchers, educators, students and practitioners to contribute to public learning. No individuals are named or presented as associated with this programme.

Status: planning framework only. No published roster, invitations, engagements or paid positions.

Voluntary participation and mutual benefit

Participation principle: Collaboration is entirely voluntary and based on each person’s abilities, interests and availability. Expectations, attribution, boundaries and any payment are agreed in advance; no one is obliged to contribute or work without fair terms. Read the full Contribute & Collaborate principles.

Ways to contribute

Guest specialist

Present a carefully scoped subject within their competence, with evidence references and an agreed Q&A format.

Workshop facilitator

Teach practical research, interpretation or communication methods.

Research collaborator

Co-develop a question, protocol, working paper or reproducible investigation with documented authorship.

Moderator or respondent

Support fair discussion, contextualise competing arguments and identify unanswered questions.

Research and production support

Assist with citations, transcripts, accessible materials, editing and quality review under appropriate terms.

Public participant

Raise questions or submit sources, with clear moderation and privacy safeguards.

Academic titles and professional expertise should be verified where material to a contribution; a doctorate does not automatically establish expertise in every subject.

Shared editorial and recording protocol

  1. Agree the topic and purpose. Define the research question, intended audience, format, and what is outside scope.
  2. Agree the terms. Specify voluntary participation, honorarium or professional fee; preparation requirements; cancellation and accessibility arrangements.
  3. Set recording permissions in writing. Decide whether Zoom is recorded, where recordings are stored, who owns original and edited materials, where they may be published and how permission can be withdrawn.
  4. Protect attribution and meaning. Credit each creator; agree substantial edits and excerpts; never remove essential caveats solely to increase engagement.
  5. Protect participants. Give advance notice of recording, moderate questions, handle personal data carefully and define who may attend. Where children might participate, apply an appropriate safeguarding policy first.
  6. Review outputs. Link sources, correct errors transparently, and distinguish a guest's views from organisational positions.

Independent publication by participants may be possible under a mutually agreed licence; a workshop does not automatically grant unlimited reuse rights to either party.

From discussion to research

For questions warranting deeper study, establish a separate research brief with existing literature, hypotheses, falsification criteria, ethics, methods, responsible contributors, authorship and reproducibility provisions. A recorded discussion is not itself a peer-reviewed paper.

Any coauthored publication requires substantive intellectual contribution, appropriate attribution, agreement on responsibility and disclosure of conflicts of interest. Students' participation should respect their institutions' assessment and academic-integrity rules.
