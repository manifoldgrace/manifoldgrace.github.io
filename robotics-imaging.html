Autism, ADHD & mental health

A condition-focused layer: research signals, practical support and accessible design.

Terms & scope

ASD and autism refer to the same spectrum. Asperger’s is a historical diagnostic term within current autism descriptions; preserve it when discussing older diagnoses or papers. NHS Dorset · terminology

Autism and ADHD are neurodevelopmental conditions. Anxiety, depression and other mental-health needs may coexist and require their own evidence and support. NHS · autism and co-occurrence · NHS · adult ADHD

Accessibility & safeguarding layer

Proposed design checklist: predictable instructions, adjustable sensory demands, breaks, clear consent and an accessible way to stop. For children, include appropriate consent and assent. Ask each person what works for them; do not assume one autistic profile.

Co-design goals around communication, independence and chosen participation. Record distress, adverse effects and privacy risks alongside performance. Avoid optimising apparent compliance or masking as a substitute for wellbeing.

Research, diagnosis & support

Keep three questions separate: what is measured, what is inferred, and whether using the technology improves a person’s life. NICE advises against routine neuroimaging for adult autism diagnosis. NICE CG142 · clinical assessment

MRI feature fusion · Dekhil et al. Retrospective classification

Autism · Mixed / unspecified · 2020

Finding: Across 18 ABIDE datasets (1,060 participants), the abstract reports accuracy ranges of 75–100% for structural MRI and 79–100% for fMRI.

Limit: Ranges across datasets are not one clinical accuracy estimate. Abstract-level catalogue entry; age-specific performance and independent clinical validation are not established here.

Engineering question: Check participant-level splits, site effects, calibration, missing modalities and external validation before claiming generalisation.

Auditory response timing · Roberts et al. Group-level association

Autism · Children / young people · 2010

Finding: An average 11 ms delay in right-hemisphere M100 responses was reported in children with ASD compared with controls.

Limit: A group average is not a universal autistic pattern, personal capability measure or validated individual diagnostic threshold.

Engineering question: Control stimulus frequency, acquisition timing and artefacts; assess overlap between individuals and reproducibility.

Roberts et al. · primary MEG study

Graph attention classification · Cai et al. Small-sample AI experiment

Autism · Children / young people · 2025

Finding: A graph attention model using resting fNIRS data from 25 autistic and 22 typically developing children reported 97.92% accuracy.

Limit: This is the authors’ experimental result, not clinical diagnostic performance. Full split design, confidence intervals and external replication need appraisal.

Engineering question: Audit participant separation, window leakage, class balance, motion and optical coupling; test an independent cohort.

Cai, Zeng & Li · primary study

Neurofeedback with an active comparison · Barth et al. Randomised trial · no superiority

ADHD · Adults · 2021

Finding: SCP neurofeedback, fNIRS neurofeedback and EMG biofeedback groups all improved; neither neurofeedback approach showed superior symptom effects over the comparison.

Limit: Adult eligibility began at 18; no exclusively over-25 finding is claimed. Improvement within a group does not prove a technology-specific effect.

Engineering question: Retain credible controls, blind outcome assessment where possible and track learning separately from symptom ratings.

Barth et al. · primary randomised trial

Robot-assisted PRT · children aged 3–8 Exploratory randomised trial

Autism · Children / young people · 2021 / 2022

Finding: Among 73 assessed children, groups did not differ significantly on reported general social-communication/global-function outcomes directly after treatment; the largest follow-up gains occurred with robot-assisted PRT.

Limit: The robot was part of a supported intervention, not an autonomous cure. Delayed results and different outcomes need separate interpretation.

Engineering question: Separate robot contribution from therapist contact, novelty and treatment content; assess transfer beyond the robot.

van den Berk-Smeekens et al. · primary trial

VR-supported phobia intervention · adult study Small pilot + individual account

Autism + anxiety · Adults · over-25 case · 2019

Finding: A pilot involved eight autistic adults aged 18–57. The university reports maintained everyday phobia-related improvement in five at six months; its individual account features Rebecca, aged 26.

Limit: The cohort was mixed-age, not all over 25. An uncontrolled pilot and personal account do not establish efficacy for every adult or isolate VR from CBT.

Engineering question: Separate the immersive display from clinician-guided graded exposure; measure real-world goals and longer-term outcomes.

How to add and appraise a finding

Record authors, date, DOI/URL, population and ages, sample size, technology, comparator, outcome and follow-up. Then record uncertainty, adverse events, funding/conflicts and replication. Mark absent information as unverified.

For AI: check leakage, external validation, subgroup performance and calibration. For hardware: check signal quality and measurement repeatability. For intervention: check benefit beyond practice, novelty or clinician contact. A high classifier score alone is not a proven support outcome.

Case studies · separate age groups

Children · robot-assisted communication support

Study: van den Berk-Smeekens and colleagues, online 2021 / journal 2022. Seventy-three assessed autistic children aged 3–8 received PRT, robot-assisted PRT or usual care.

Sequence: 1. Baseline assessment → 2. Supported sessions → 3. Post-treatment assessment → 4. Follow-up comparison.

Outcome: No significant group differences on the abstract’s reported general outcomes immediately after treatment; largest gains at follow-up occurred in the robot-assisted group.

Takeaway: Test generalisation and delayed outcomes. This is an exploratory trial, not proof that robots replace human support. Primary trial · methods and results

Adults over 25 · Rebecca, aged 26 in the published account

Context: Newcastle University’s 2019 account describes an autistic woman whose doorway/corridor phobia interrupted university attendance. It reports four Blue Room sessions and subsequent practice in everyday settings.

Technology: Personalised room-scale VR with therapist-guided exposure; no headset was required. VR itself is not necessarily AI.

Reported outcome: She described greater independence and returning to university, completing her degree. Her age is 26 at publication; age at treatment is not established here.

Takeaway: Judge transfer to personally meaningful goals. This individual account supports understanding lived outcomes, not a controlled estimate of benefit for all adults over 25.

Design to sign-off

Different systems follow different evidence routes. Engineering approval, market conformity and hospital acceptance are distinct decisions.

1 · Define purpose

Specify users, use environment, measurement and acceptance criteria.

Responsibility & evidence: Systems engineer and clinical/research partners: agreed requirements and intended use.

2 · Design architecture

Allocate mechanical, electrical, software and safety responsibilities.

Responsibility & evidence: Design team: drawings, interfaces, signal budget and traceable risk controls.

3 · Build prototype

Integrate components and document configurations and calibration.

Responsibility & evidence: Engineering team: controlled build records and bench results.

4 · Verify requirements

Test each specification against predefined acceptance criteria.

Responsibility & evidence: Test and quality teams: protocols, results, deviations and requirement traceability.

5 · Validate use

Evaluate whether the complete system meets intended user needs.

Responsibility & evidence: Relevant users and clinical specialists: usability and performance evidence appropriate to the device.

6 · Review conformity

Determine the applicable market, device classification and conformity route.

Responsibility & evidence: Manufacturer and, where required, assessment body: technical documentation and conformity evidence. Routes differ by jurisdiction and class.

7 · Accept installation

Check site readiness, installation, commissioning and user training.

Responsibility & evidence: Local service and clinical teams: acceptance records and operational readiness. This is separate from market conformity.

8 · Monitor operation

Maintain calibration, service records, incident handling and change control.

Responsibility & evidence: Operator and manufacturer: monitoring evidence; changes may require renewed testing and regulatory review.

Educational lifecycle synthesis, informed by FDA design and development guidance, MHRA conformity assessment guidance and UK device regulation guidance. Consult the current applicable route rather than treating one checklist as universal.

Where AI and agents fit

Reconstruction, analysis & control

AI may support reconstruction, segmentation, artefact detection or robotic perception. These are possible application areas, not capabilities of every system. An agent may coordinate bounded software tasks; authority to affect physical equipment requires explicit limits, safety controls and validated behaviour.

Safeguarding & cybersecurity

Proposed review prompts: who may command movement or acquisition, what happens on lost communication, what is logged, how patient data is protected, and how updates are tested and rolled back? Separate safety controls from model confidence; uncertain predictions must not silently authorise hazardous actions.

Learn · test · explain

Use synthetic signals or public demonstration data to introduce noise, timing errors or missing measurements. Compare a baseline with a correction, record assumptions and explain residual uncertainty. Physical fault testing belongs in an authorised controlled facility.

Self-check: Which component senses? Which component acts? What is measured directly? What is inferred? Who accepts the evidence at each gate?
