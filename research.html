Research

My research interests sit at the intersection of artificial intelligence, ethics, governance, technology, institutions and society, with particular attention to the gap between systems as designed and systems as experienced in practice.

CURRENT INQUIRY

Research focus

My research is deliberately interdisciplinary. It connects questions that are often separated institutionally — technical design, law and regulation, organisational behaviour, public administration, education, ethics and social consequences.

The aim is not simply to describe emerging technologies, but to examine how they are governed, implemented and experienced, including the points where formal policy and operational reality diverge.

  • AI ethics, governance and responsible technology
  • Technology, institutions and public administration
  • Regulation, accountability, data protection and due process
  • Education, work and social change under automation
  • Research provenance, attribution and preservation of human contribution
  • Socio-technical systems and the operational reality behind policy

Method & current questions

My working method combines source mapping, comparative reading, timeline reconstruction, public-record research, technical systems analysis and cross-disciplinary synthesis. I am interested in preserving where information came from, who surfaced it, and how interpretations change as evidence accumulates.

Current questions include: How should AI be governed when capability moves faster than institutions? How can regulation remain connected to operational reality? How should organisations evidence fairness and accountability? How can provenance and human contribution remain visible in AI-mediated knowledge systems?

These questions are treated as research problems rather than fixed conclusions. Where evidence is incomplete or competing interpretations exist, I retain the disagreement and assess the relative strength of the available sources instead of forcing a premature binary answer.
