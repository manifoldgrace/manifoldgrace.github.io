Research

My research interests sit at the intersection of artificial intelligence, ethics, governance, technology, institutions and society, with particular attention to the gap between systems as designed and systems as experienced in practice.

CURRENT INQUIRY

Research focus

My research is deliberately interdisciplinary. It connects questions that are often separated institutionally — technical design, law and regulation, organisational behaviour, public administration, education, ethics and social consequences.

The aim is not simply to describe emerging technologies, but to examine how they are governed, implemented and experienced, including the points where formal policy and operational reality diverge.

Preparation for future doctoral research has also been in formation through institutional pathways, including within the University of London context. This remains preparatory research development rather than a claim of current doctoral candidacy.

Research development and community contribution

Birkbeck, University of London

Within the AI, Ethics & Society cohort, I contributed informal peer mentoring, conceptual-development support, curriculum engagement and knowledge exchange. I was invited to contribute a segment of a class session, drawing on professional experience in AI ethics, reflective methodology and socio-technical systems. I developed and shared the Chronological Ethical Self-Inventory as a reflective framework for ethical reasoning and personal knowledge management.

Social Research Association

I have liaised with the Social Research Association and engaged with external academic networks, alongside voluntary representation and inclusion advocacy within my postgraduate community.

ORCID — scholarly contribution and attribution

I submitted proposals on recognising participatory research contributions, preserving attribution and documenting evolving forms of AI-assisted scholarly work.

OpenAI — participatory product and governance proposals

I submitted feature proposals concerning conversational memory transparency, accessible knowledge management, attribution, profile synchronisation and recognition of human contributions to socio-technical systems.

Independent higher-education research and advocacy

I documented institutional processes, prepared correspondence and appeals, and examined procedural fairness, data governance and accessibility. This work connects lived institutional experience with questions about research progression and accountable public systems.
