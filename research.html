Research focus

My research is deliberately interdisciplinary. It connects questions that are often separated institutionally — technical design, law and regulation, organisational behaviour, public administration, education, ethics and social consequences.

The aim is not simply to describe emerging technologies, but to examine how they are governed, implemented and experienced, including the points where formal policy and operational reality diverge.

Preparation for future doctoral research has also been in formation through institutional pathways, including within the University of London context. This remains preparatory research development rather than a claim of current doctoral candidacy.

Five AI projects for doctoral research

Explore representative doctoral-level workflows, from gathering evidence to running reproducible experiments. Each example includes the research task, practical steps, intended output and links to established tools. These are proposed starter projects, not claims of completed experiments or connected laboratory services.

1 of 5
01 / EVIDENCE

AI-assisted literature mapping

Identify themes and research gaps across a scholarly literature corpus using NLP, while keeping researcher review central.

  1. Collect papers and record search criteria.
  2. Deduplicate, embed and cluster abstracts.
  3. Review inclusions, interpretations and gaps.

Output: Evidence map, bibliography and auditable screening log.

Explore: Zotero · OpenAlex · Jupyter

02 / RESEARCH DATA

Dataset construction & annotation

Build a trustworthy dataset for scientific AI work, with explicit permissions, quality checks and traceable transformations.

  1. Import appropriately licensed data.
  2. Clean, label and test data quality.
  3. Version datasets, splits and metadata.

Output: Versioned dataset, data dictionary and provenance record.

Explore: DVC · Label Studio · Jupyter

03 / EXPERIMENTATION

Model training & benchmarking

Compare a defensible baseline against alternative machine-learning models, with reproducible evaluation.

  1. Define hypotheses, baseline and metrics.
  2. Train models with appropriate data splits.
  3. Compare performance, uncertainty and errors.

Output: Results table, experiment log and reproducible notebook.

Explore: scikit-learn · MLflow · Colab

04 / AI GOVERNANCE

Bias, reliability & explainability audit

Investigate where an AI model performs inconsistently and document governance implications using measurable tests.

  1. Choose evaluation criteria and test sets.
  2. Inspect failures, robustness and subgroup metrics.
  3. Document limitations and mitigation options.

Output: Evaluation report, model card and risk register.

Explore: NIST AI RMF · SHAP · Model cards

05 / SCIENTIFIC MODELLING

Simulation & scenario analysis

Use computational models to explore complex systems, uncertainty and the consequences of changing assumptions.

  1. Specify mechanisms and testable assumptions.
  2. Simulate alternative scenarios and conditions.
  3. Check sensitivity against observed evidence.

Output: Reproducible simulation, uncertainty plots and methods note.

Explore: Mesa · PyMC · Jupyter

Research architecture

The research area is divided by function rather than by prestige: reference links for navigation, case studies for bounded problems, papers for longer outputs, and Systems for questions that only make sense when institutions are mapped together.

This keeps working material, published material and source infrastructure separate while allowing them to connect.
