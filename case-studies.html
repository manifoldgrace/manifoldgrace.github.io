Tottenham Hotspur: AV testing, user training & operational readiness

Scope and responsibilities

In my AV engineering lead work, I supported integrated meeting-room and audiovisual systems through setup, installation checks, functional testing, commissioning/UAT-related activity, troubleshooting and ongoing support. I used third-party schematics to understand equipment relationships and connectivity, alongside manuals and research into equipment types, functions, features and navigation.

Testing and practical problem-solving

Much of the work involved routine functionality and setup issues. I used equipment documentation, system knowledge and practical trial-and-error testing to identify working configurations and resolve problems, often through toggling settings or resets. I also provided user familiarisation and training across the equipment ranges.

Interface development

A touchscreen graphical user interface required programmatic adaptation during the development lifecycle. This example records the adaptation requirement within the work; it does not assign personal authorship of the programming.

QA reporting and outcome

After tests, I reported QA results to confirm whether rooms and equipment were fully installed, tested and ready for use. The professional contribution was practical verification and communication of readiness, alongside helping people operate the systems.

Evidence and relevance

The supplied Tottenham schematics document integrated AV configurations. Third parties produced the drawings and the handwritten annotations are not mine. My account describes how I used the documentation in engineering practice; the drawings alone do not establish individual test results. This experience illustrates schematic interpretation, systems understanding, functional assurance, issue resolution, technical research, training and readiness reporting—capabilities relevant to technology assurance, operational governance and responsible adoption.

Practice example: evaluating an AI automation supplier

28 October 2026 · Independent enterprise evaluation

Question

What evidence would be needed to assess whether a proposed AI automation platform was suitable for the intended business use?

My contribution

I participated in a supplier demonstration, questioned the distinction between autonomous agents and predefined workflows, and examined third-party dependencies, integrations, access controls, scalability, costs, testing and human oversight. I developed a structured technical due-diligence questionnaire and prepared follow-up correspondence to obtain written clarification.

Judgement demonstrated

I treated demonstration claims as matters to verify before making implementation commitments. The questionnaire connected technical questions to business decisions about reliability, security, operating costs and approval boundaries.

Recorded output and outcome

A questionnaire was created and a follow-up email draft prepared. The published activity record records the draft as unsent. Written supplier responses, technical validation, procurement approval and implemented safeguards are not established by this example.

Relevance to professional roles

The example demonstrates structured inquiry, supplier evaluation, risk identification and communication relevant to responsible AI adoption and governance support. It can be assessed alongside my earlier institutional and operational work when considering role-specific requirements.
