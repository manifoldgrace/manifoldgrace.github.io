METHOD

Evidence rule and maturity scale

Preferred standard: publish the strongest claim the evidence actually supports. Speculation may generate a research question, but it is not promoted into a factual statement merely because later events resemble an earlier concern.

  1. Context evidenced — independent evidence confirms the wider condition or trend.
  2. Chronology evidenced — dated evidence fixes the relevant people, actions and events in time.
  3. Transmission evidenced — a document trail shows the matter moving through an identifiable channel.
  4. Acknowledgement evidenced — receipt or consideration is explicitly confirmed.
  5. Implementation evidenced — a later system, process, rule or practice is shown to have changed.
  6. Attribution evidenced — records explicitly connect a contribution to that change.

Important limits: version histories prove changes, not necessarily who caused them; a later policy is not proof of an earlier individual's influence; and a public project archive can establish project context without proving a particular person's work unless that person is independently documented.
