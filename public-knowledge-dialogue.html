Public Knowledge, Dialogue & Learning

An emerging programme for sharing evidence, examining important questions and learning across disciplines. The proposed format is online-first, with public reference material available independently of any future event.

Programme status: proposed. No events, guest speakers, fees, registrations or institutional partnerships are confirmed.

What this programme is for

Research gains value when different people can scrutinise it, interpret it responsibly and ask questions. The programme connects historical inquiry, public health, social research, ethics, technology and cultural knowledge without requiring every contributor to cover the same subjects or agree with Manifold Grace.

Material is distinguished as independent external reference, prospective contribution, confirmed event or completed and documented collaboration. Being referenced here does not imply endorsement, affiliation or an invitation.

EDITORIAL DESIGN · NOT A SPEAKER ROSTER

Knowledge library and contributor features

A subject-led directory can introduce a question, then connect to published work, a short verified biography where permission is appropriate, a selected publication or recorded talk, relevant contrasting research and areas where further evidence is needed. No real contributor profiles, photographs or book endorsements are published in this planning version.

Illustrative feature structure

Question: What evidence best explains a contested historical or social claim?

Sources: Verified original publication, independent scholarship and historical records where available.

Context: What is established, debated or still unknown?

Contributor: Name, profile and expertise only after fact-checking and appropriate consent; reference-only content is labelled accordingly.

Next discussion: Proposed questions, without inventing participants or dates.

For methodologies see Semantic Integrity and Public Communication Assurance and Research, Culture & Creative Expression.

ONLINE FIRST · PLANNING ONLY

Proposed Zoom programme

Quarterly or annual Zoom sessions are a possible low-overhead starting point. Frequency would depend on contributor interest, safeguarding, moderation capacity, accessibility, costs and funding. No public booking or meeting link is currently available.

Research conversations

One topic, evidence pack, moderated questions and a clear record of unresolved issues.

Public workshops

Source evaluation, communication methods, historical interpretation or research skills.

Interdisciplinary panels

Several perspectives on a defined question, with disagreements represented fairly.

Student and independent research clinics

Methods, references and peer learning; not a substitute for supervision or accredited teaching.

Illustrative 75-minute format

Welcome and scope (5 minutes); guest contribution (25 minutes); moderated inquiry (25 minutes); audience questions (15 minutes); summary and next research questions (5 minutes). This is a draft format, not an advertised session.

Once any event is agreed, its page should state the speaker's confirmed role, date, time zone, accessibility options, fee or free status, privacy notice, event host and whether recording is permitted.

Events & Conferences holds listings and any future verified programme entries. Public Speaking distinguishes actual speaking records from proposals.

Mutually useful, independently credited outputs

A prospective collaboration could produce a source pack, an independently authored talk, a moderated recording, accessible excerpts, a written summary and follow-up research questions. Distribution through different channels should follow a mutually agreed editorial and licensing arrangement. Each contributor retains appropriate credit and the right to have their contribution accurately represented.

No recording or reuse is assumed: written approval should address copyright, ownership of raw recordings, permission for platform-specific clips, review of substantial edits, context preservation, withdrawal limitations and audience privacy before a session occurs.

Participation could be voluntary, honorarium-based or paid as a contracted professional service. A one-hour speaking engagement is not necessarily unpaid: fees depend on negotiation, scope, preparation, licensing, budget and any applicable employment or tax rules. No payment offer is made on this page.

Read the proposed contributor and editing protocol.

Research and public-benefit evaluation

Possible measures include attendance, accessibility, comprehension before and after a session, source quality, factual corrections, constructive disagreement, consent compliance and reusable educational outputs. Views and engagement alone should not be treated as proof of learning or research impact.

Potential research question: can evidence-led public dialogue increase understanding and participation without increasing misleading simplification or polarisation?
