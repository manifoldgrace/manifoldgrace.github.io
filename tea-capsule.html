01 · Leaf chamber

Space for cut leaves to hydrate and expand; dose and clearance require laboratory measurement.

02 · Controlled water path

Explore distributed inlet and lower filtration for even wetting, without bypassing the tea.

03 · Filtration

Retain small leaf particles while allowing the finished tea to pass through.

04 · Residual liquid

Compare gravity drainage, programmed machine extraction and an optional post-brew expression mechanism.

Option A · Machine-managed brewing and drainage

Primary development route: the machine controls temperature, brew contact and flow. Test whether a second low-pressure drainage phase reduces retained liquid without over-extraction. Existing compatible-machine operation must be evaluated by qualified designers and the machine manufacturer.

Option B · Separate squeeze or expression accessory

Alternative experimental concept: a cool-down-safe, enclosed receiving cradle for spent capsules with a controlled pressure plate and spill capture. Do not instruct users to squeeze or puncture a hot used capsule by hand. Consider hygiene, hot-liquid exposure, structural failure and disposal.

Option C · Alternative formats

A refillable loose-leaf cartridge or a non-machine infusion pod could provide greater leaf expansion and easier removal, but would require their own performance and hygiene testing. Ingestible supplement capsules are a different regulatory and product category.

02 · Production: from leaf to capsule

1. Origin & procurement

Document source country, supplier, botanical identity, lot number, traceability and ethical sourcing.

2. Tea preparation

Grade, sort, clean, cut and blend tea, and examine moisture and particle size.

3. Capsule engineering

Choose food-contact materials, barrier performance, chamber geometry, filter and safe opening mechanism.

4. Filling & sealing

Validate dose uniformity, seal integrity, machine compatibility and shelf-life conditions.

5. Performance testing

Measure brewed volume, retained liquid, sensory quality, extraction consistency, contamination and disposal claims.

6. Packing & shipping

Pack and label to destination requirements; obtain export and import documentation before distribution.

06 · Market evidence and uncertainty

97.5%

UK Tea & Infusions Association reports that 97.5% of tea purchased in the UK comes in tea bags. This is an industry-published figure, not a 2026 capsule market-share estimate.

UKTIA source

86 billion

The Tea Association of the USA's 2024 fact sheet estimates almost 86 billion US servings of tea in 2023. This measures all tea, not capsule sales.

US association fact sheet

No verified capsule share

No reliable, directly comparable worldwide market share for tea-leaf machine capsules was established in the source review. The niche proposition remains a hypothesis to test through retail audits and customer trials.

07 · Business development and validation

Discovery

Interview machine owners, tea drinkers, hospitality buyers and office managers. Record willingness to pay and common objections.

Sample testing

Compare prototype extraction, residual liquid, brew quality and user preference against loose tea and tea bags.

Trade exhibitions

Meet contract packers, packaging manufacturers and wholesale distributors. Seek sample quotes and machine-compatibility evidence.

Small B2B pilots

Consider offices, boutique hotels and specialty retailers; measure actual reorder demand before scaling.

Distribution

Test direct-to-consumer packs, sampling partnerships and hospitality supply contracts with transparent pricing.

Scale gates

Expand only once unit economics, supply capacity, food safety, complaint rates and repeat orders have been validated.
