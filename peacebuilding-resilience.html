Helpful low-risk bushcraft and outdoor readiness (6)
SkillSafe application
Navigation and orientationRead maps, recognise marked routes, check forecasts and tell a trusted person your planned route.
Weather and exposure awarenessRecognise changing conditions and prioritise appropriate clothing, shade and established safe shelter.
Water and food safetyCarry safe drinking water and familiar food; follow public-health guidance rather than assuming untreated natural water is safe.
First aid and hygieneLearn approved first aid, hand hygiene, blister prevention and recognising when professional help is needed.
Low-impact outdoor livingPractise campsite organisation, waste disposal and Leave No Trace principles in safe, lawful settings.
Communication skillsKeep written contacts, use approved communication devices and practise clear check-ins.

These skills complement—not replace—established shelters, emergency responders, food safety rules and medical care. Avoid unverified wild-food identification and improvised hazardous methods.

Illustrative caseResponse principleRead more

Common: severe storm or floodingRespond to warnings, avoid unsafe travel, protect documents and follow local evacuation advice.Source guidance
Common: cyber or service failureActivate a continuity plan, verify the problem, use clean backups and notify the relevant contacts.Source guidance
Uncommon: evacuation abroad during unrestConsult destination-specific FCDO advice, keep documents ready, and follow local official instructions.Source guidance
Uncommon: family members separated by crisisUse an agreed meeting/contact plan; recognised humanitarian tracing services may help when usual communications fail.Source guidance
Uncommon: prolonged displacementPrioritise safe accommodation, continuity of medicines and documents, social support and recognised humanitarian assistance.Source guidance

These are illustrative scenarios, not claims that any particular outcome is guaranteed. Case-specific advice always takes priority.
