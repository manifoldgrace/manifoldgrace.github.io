Comparative AI Quality, Ethics and Assurance

Research record: 11 November 2026. This page compares published independent safety assessments with preliminary user-observed questions about model quality, contradictory responses, adherence to terminology and information architecture, contributor attribution, transparent limitations and unsolicited conversational redirection. It is an educational research analysis, not a completed audit or an implementation commitment.

The index attributes Anthropic's comparatively higher assessment in part to governance, information-sharing and safety-framework disclosures. It notes OpenAI's broader risk evaluation and external testing. Google DeepMind has documented risk-assessment work but scored lower on governance in this index. None of these grades can establish an individual company's full internal ethical performance.

Proposed assessment design

A pilot may test 3–5 systems before expanding to an exploratory ten-system comparison. A proposed design uses 15 scenarios repeated three times per system (450 responses for ten systems), subject to calibration and revision. This is a research possibility, not an approved implementation, validated sample size or completed evaluation.

Evidence sources and scope limitations

FLI AI Safety Index (July 2026) · Stanford Foundation Model Transparency Index (December 2025) · NIST AI Risk Management Framework · Anthropic system cards · Princeton AI agent reliability research.

Public assessments cannot establish the entirety of internal training pipelines, private logs, contractual arrangements or corporate intent. User observations are evidence of individual interactions, not automatically generalisable across a product or provider. This page does not label any company or person as having committed wrongdoing.
