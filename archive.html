Archive

The Archive preserves selected material behind the present-day work: earlier projects, performances, websites, technical work, research traces and public records. Family-history research itself belongs under Heritage.

HISTORICAL

Projects & digital traces

This section records earlier technical and digital work that remains useful as evidence of practice, experimentation or development.

Items are retained because they help establish chronology, demonstrate skills in context or show how later interests developed. Historical projects are dated and described as historical rather than presented as current portfolio work.

ARCHIVED

Creative & professional material

Selected older music, performance, event, production, audiovisual and professional material will be retained where it helps explain the development of later work. Archived material is labelled clearly so that it is not mistaken for current activity.

The archive is selective rather than exhaustive. Priority goes to material that adds evidence, context or continuity to the public record, while repetitive or purely private material remains outside the site.

Heritage is separate

Genealogy, family history and evidenced historical connections are maintained on the dedicated Heritage page. The Archive answers a different question: what surviving material documents the journey?

This separation keeps historical artefacts and genealogical claims from being collapsed into one category. Heritage evaluates relationships and evidence; Archive preserves the documentary and creative traces around the wider life and work.
