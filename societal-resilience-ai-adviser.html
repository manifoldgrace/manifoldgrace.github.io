Societal Resilience & the AI Public Adviser

An interdisciplinary programme connecting geopolitical developments, the ethics of artificial advisers, probability and prediction, state power, accountable AI, economic adaptation and practical public resilience. It draws upon themes studied at master's level in AI, ethics and society, alongside continuing independent investigation. This page is not a claim that a university module was completed or assessed.

Public adviser status (10 November 2026): A working, browser-only source-comparison demonstration is available below. It is not a deployed language model, live news feed, commercial investment adviser or verified forecast service. It does not scrape, upload or store visitor text on a server.

Public adviser — source-comparison laboratory

The first implementation helps a visitor document a public-interest question and compare two independent source descriptions. It requires original links to be supplied by the visitor. It reports differences and missing evidence; it does not declare an article true merely because several outlets repeat it.

Interactive prototype · local browser processing only

Enter two independently attributed source summaries and choose Compare. This demonstration checks record completeness and common vocabulary; it cannot verify factual claims or infer causality.
What an actual AI adviser would additionally need

Licensed or permissioned ingestion and source APIs; a dated claim-and-event store; primary-source retrieval; multilingual document parsing; recognition of independently sourced versus syndicated coverage; corroboration and contradiction assessment; explicit uncertainty and correction histories; transparent forecasts tested against outcomes; and editorial review for consequential conclusions.

Any public claim about an organisation, business or political event must distinguish confirmed facts from interpretations and scenarios. Political recommendations remain for users and accountable institutions to decide. No automated trading, hiring, benefit adjudication or government decision-making is implemented here.

News coverage register: compare, do not equate

Potential editorial viewpoints and reporting regions can be compared using BBC News, Al Jazeera, CNBC, Fox News, Reuters and independent or local reporting where relevant. Publisher variety does not automatically produce independent evidence: the same wire report may be republished by many outlets. Check primary documents, reporting dates, original evidence, corrections, publication rights and geographical coverage. No live articles are fetched by this page.

The ILO's 2025 task-exposure assessment and OECD Employment Outlook 2026 support scenario-based employment analysis, not a definitive claim that mass unemployment is inevitable. The UK Be Ready guidance supports practical preparation without alarmism.

Engineering, testing and potential product development

Stage 1 — Evidence-led demonstrator (available on this page)

Structured question capture, comparison of two source records, provenance completeness checks and an explicit statement of unsupported conclusions. Demonstration output is rule-based JavaScript and not AI inference.

Stage 2 — Controlled research assistant

Build a licensed/authorised retrieval backend, dated claim-to-evidence records, human-reviewed contradiction tests, web-source coverage reporting and correction/version histories. Compare against GDELT, existing research agents and manual research baselines.

Stage 3 — Calibrated public and enterprise advisory

Connect verified public datasets, business indicators, official risk registers and labour-market data; produce scenario assumptions and confidence limits; evaluate prediction calibration and potential harms before adding public advice features.

Stage 4 — Sustainable delivery and public access

Compare grants, research partnerships, enterprise subscriptions, responsible licensing, donations and cooperative structures. Maintain a freely accessible public-interest evidence layer, with editorial accountability and separation of financial incentives from factual conclusions.
