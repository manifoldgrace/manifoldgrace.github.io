Questions, Papers, Projects & Evidence

Ten connected, independently testable research questions tracing societal change from 2007 through possible futures after 2030. Each has a proposed paper, practical project, evidence base and alternative explanations.

Research status: Working programme and source audit, not ten completed papers or ten completed studies. Published facts and statistics are separate from hypotheses and forecasts. Starting point: historical-to-futures framework.

RQ-S01 · Crisis and restructuring · PROPOSED

Did the 2008 financial shock cause later staffing and service redesign, or did existing digitalisation and business-model trends explain more?

Proposed paper initiative
Financial Crisis to Digital Restructuring, 2007–2020 — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Dated archive of company reports, employment and service-channel changes; segmented pre/post and comparison sectors.
Historical/statistical evidence
ONS recession retrospective; employment and industry archives; 2007 observations as prompts only.
ONS, 2008 recession retrospective (2018)
Falsification / competing explanations
Prior digitisation, sector demand, competitors, regulation; test parallel pre-trends.

RQ-S02 · Who does the work now? · PROPOSED

When customer-service budgets fall, are tasks eliminated, improved or transferred to customers, other staff, charities and public bodies?

Proposed paper initiative
Private Savings, Public Costs: The Service Burden Transfer Audit — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Two-sector case audit: cost per resolved case, abandoned contacts, wait, customer minutes, accessibility and costs outside the firm.
Historical/statistical evidence
Which? 27m-hours estimate; Ofcom 58% broadband complaints satisfaction (2024).
Which?, consumer harm from poor customer service (2024) · Ofcom, complaints handling comparisons (2025)
Falsification / competing explanations
Compare firms with improving services; audit customer samples and contacts not submitted.

RQ-S03 · Why were decisions made? · PROPOSED

What documentary evidence differentiates financial need, technical optimism, competition, discriminatory processes, bias and deliberate profit-seeking?

Proposed paper initiative
Intent, Impact and Evidence in Corporate Service Restructuring — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Decision-evidence register linking contemporaneous minutes, forecasts, approvals, equality assessments and downstream outcomes.
Historical/statistical evidence
Company filings, equality impact assessments, board records; Companies Act section 172.
FCA, Consumer Duty · Companies Act 2006, section 172
Falsification / competing explanations
Avoid inferring motive from effect; record uncertainty and mixed/graded motives.

RQ-S04 · Corporate governance and law · PROPOSED

Do current CSR and consumer law rules address social costs transferred outside firms, and what additional duty would be proportionate?

Proposed paper initiative
A Social Burden Test for Major Service Redesign — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Legal comparison and ex-ante social-impact assessment prototype across financial, telecoms and public services.
Historical/statistical evidence
Companies Act 2006 s172; FCA Consumer Duty; Ofcom sector benchmarks.
Ofcom, complaints handling comparisons (2025) · FCA, Consumer Duty · Companies Act 2006, section 172
Falsification / competing explanations
Regulatory coverage differs; simulate compliance costs, innovation benefits and enforcement feasibility.

RQ-S05 · Knowledge versus cooperation · PROPOSED

Do increased access to information, social media and independent publishing measurably improve trust and collective action?

Proposed paper initiative
Understanding Without Cooperation: A Social-Knowledge Gap — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Longitudinal panel plus official social-capital indicators; compare digital participation to real-world coordination.
Historical/statistical evidence
Community survey 61% 'pull together' in 2025/26 vs 56% 2024/25; ONS trust-government 21.9%.
Government, Community and Engagement Survey 2025/26 · ONS, Beyond GDP wellbeing (February 2026) · Community Life Survey 2023/24, trust and belonging
Falsification / competing explanations
Government trust ≠ interpersonal trust; survey shifts and cohort differences; test improvement as well as decline.

RQ-S06 · Individual and mutual formation · PROPOSED

Does repeated pursuit of personal reward predict weaker reciprocal consideration, or do pre-existing dispositions account for both?

Proposed paper initiative
Experience-Centred Living and Reciprocal Moral Formation — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Voluntary adult longitudinal survey, validated empathy and mutual-responsiveness measures; qualitative interviews.
Historical/statistical evidence
Pew US dating motives: 44% long-term, 40% casual; psychological literature requires separate review.
Pew Research, online dating US (2023)
Falsification / competing explanations
Never equate casual dating with harm, gender or conscience injury; test self-selection and conflicting evidence.

RQ-S07 · Cohabitation, families and choice · PROPOSED

How do mutuality, housing affordability, work, care provision and individual preference influence partnership and family intentions?

Proposed paper initiative
Family Formation Under Economic and Relational Change — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
ONS/OECD historical indicator comparison and opt-in interviews on care, household cooperation and preferences.
Historical/statistical evidence
ONS 2025 TFR 1.39; OECD 2024 economic and social drivers.
ONS, births England and Wales 2025 (released 2026) · OECD, Society at a Glance (2024)
Falsification / competing explanations
Fertility is not a moral score; single parents, child-free households and assisted reproduction are not deficiencies.

RQ-S08 · Pandemic and social reorganisation · PROPOSED

Did homeworking and pandemic-era service restructuring produce lasting benefits or burdens for employment, care and community participation?

Proposed paper initiative
Life Reorganised: Remote Work, Access and Community After 2020 — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Matched occupations and areas; changes in commuting, inclusion, service access, caring time and isolation.
Historical/statistical evidence
ONS 46.6% did some homeworking April 2020; subsequent working-pattern series needed.
ONS, homeworking April 2020 (2020)
Falsification / competing explanations
Distinguish emergency lockdown effects from lasting hybrid-work benefits; control occupation and income.

RQ-S09 · Living by AI forecasts · PROPOSED

Do 2025/2030 labour projections alter educational, corporate and personal decisions before actual capability and deployment are established?

Proposed paper initiative
Living by the Forecast: Anticipatory Behaviour in the AI Transition — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Forecast-to-outcome tracker: publication, claim, assumed timeline, evidence, adoption lag and revision.
Historical/statistical evidence
WEF projects 22% job churn to 2030; UK AI assessment 70% occupational exposure; Skills England reports uncertainty.
Skills England, Annual Skills Report (2026) · UK government, AI capabilities and labour-market assessment (2026) · WEF, Future of Jobs (2025)
Falsification / competing explanations
Exposure ≠ replacement; changing jobs ≠ AI-only effects; audit failures and positive surprises.

RQ-S10 · Paths to flourishing · PROPOSED

Which combinations of human access, social-capital investment, shared responsibility and responsible AI measurably improve wellbeing and cooperation?

Proposed paper initiative
From Private Optimisation to Shared Flourishing: Policy Scenarios — no paper or preprint claimed.
Practical research project
Participatory, non-coercive futures lab with four scenarios, publicly specified indicators and intervention evaluation.
Historical/statistical evidence
ONS beyond-GDP indicators, UK community surveys, OECD family conditions, sector service-quality results.
Which?, consumer harm from poor customer service (2024) · Ofcom, complaints handling comparisons (2025) · OECD, Society at a Glance (2024) · Government, Community and Engagement Survey 2025/26 · ONS, Beyond GDP wellbeing (February 2026)
Falsification / competing explanations
No guaranteed dystopia or war; forecasts conditional; seek nulls, harms of interventions and value disagreement.

Source catalogue

Initial 15 authoritative and research sources

Original source years, jurisdiction, survey populations and methodological limits must be preserved in each eventual paper. Statistics with different definitions or populations must not be plotted together as a homogeneous time series.

  1. ONS, 2008 recession retrospective (2018)

    Historical economic context; no firm-specific causal attribution.

  2. Which?, consumer harm from poor customer service (2024)

    Estimated 27 million hours wasted by UK energy and broadband customers during May 2023–May 2024; modelled survey estimate, not direct observation.

  3. Ofcom, complaints handling comparisons (2025)

    58% broadband complaints-handling satisfaction in 2024; improvement since 2022; counterevidence to universal decline.

  4. ONS, homeworking April 2020 (2020)

    46.6% of employed people did some homeworking in April 2020; lockdown-period snapshot.

  5. Skills England, Annual Skills Report (2026)

    Highlights uncertainty in AI adoption and skills requirements; cites third-party job-ad trends.

  6. UK government, AI capabilities and labour-market assessment (2026)

    Around 70% of UK workers in occupations containing potentially AI-performable/enhanceable tasks; exposure is not displacement.

  7. WEF, Future of Jobs (2025)

    Employer survey models 22% labour-market churn through 2030; conditional, not an observed AI-only effect.

  8. ONS, births England and Wales 2025 (released 2026)

    2025 total fertility rate 1.39; does not identify moral causes.

  9. OECD, Society at a Glance (2024)

    Family formation linked to housing affordability, work, care policies and changing preferences.

  10. Government, Community and Engagement Survey 2025/26

    61% agreed people pull together to improve the neighbourhood versus 56% in 2024/25; survey/method changes must be checked.

  11. ONS, Beyond GDP wellbeing (February 2026)

    21.9% reported trust in UK government (Great Britain, Dec 2025–Jan 2026); distinct from interpersonal trust.

  12. Pew Research, online dating US (2023)

    44% of current/recent users cited long-term partnership as a major reason and 40% casual dating; US sample, not UK prevalence.

  13. FCA, Consumer Duty

    Existing regulated-sector obligation on appropriate consumer support and outcomes.

  14. Companies Act 2006, section 172

    Director duties and considerations; does not guarantee staffed customer-service access.

  15. Community Life Survey 2023/24, trust and belonging

    Neighbourhood belonging roughly 60–63% since 2015/16; undermines assumption of uninterrupted decline.

Cross-project methodology and integrity

  1. Construct a 2007–2026 chronological evidence log separating historical events, lived observations, archival records, statistical series and competing interpretation.
  2. Record the population, period, geography, statistical definition, denominator, uncertainty and publication year for every number.
  3. Pre-register hypotheses and null/contrary scenarios. Prefer quasi-experimental and longitudinal comparisons where credible counterfactuals exist.
  4. Separate corporate intention from effect, potential discrimination from documented discrimination, exposure to AI from actual displacement, and fertility indicators from asserted moral causes.
  5. Use data minimisation, consent and privacy; do not publish names of living participants or private workplace allegations without proper documentary basis and legal review.
  6. Where AI is used, keep human-reviewed source extraction, dataset versioning, model error logs, provenance and independent reproduction. No participant recruitment without governance and ethics review.
  7. Track evidence quality, original publication years, corrections and conflicting studies; expand literature to a systematic review only when research scope justifies it.

Future work: An interactive evidence explorer and reproducible datasets are proposed, not implemented. Formal preprints, validated statistical models, policy pilots and collaborations remain planned.
