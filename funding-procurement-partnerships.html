1. Government contracts and procurement

A contract is usually a purchase of defined services or deliverables. A government agency might procure evidence reviews, digital accessibility audits, systems mapping, quality-assurance studies or research consultancy. Government procurement is competitive and is not the same as a research grant.

UKRI organisational eligibility: a consequential distinction

UKRI official criteria distinguish standard eligibility (including recognised independent research organisations) from opportunity-specific non-standard eligibility for categories such as charities, social enterprises and other third-sector bodies. The non-standard routes include micro (up to £20,000), simple (above £20,000 to £200,000), and regular (above £200,000) assessment thresholds, but an organisation's legal type and a particular opportunity's terms still govern eligibility.

Standard research-organisation eligibility is not automatically available to an ordinary private company: UKRI's standard criteria include UK-based, UK-owned and not-for-profit requirements, as well as established governance, research capacity and financial viability. Businesses seeking innovation funding should separately inspect applicable Innovate UK competitions. UKRI application guidance indicates eligibility assessment can take at least 12 weeks.

3. Research partnerships and commissioned projects

University or eligible host

Seek a documented project role, agreed budget and formal relationship when a grant requires an approved submitting organisation.

Public-sector collaboration

Respond to buyer-led requests for research, evaluation or systems analysis; establish conflicts, data access and procurement requirements first.

Charitable and think-tank partnerships

Develop jointly scoped social-policy or evidence projects with clear ownership, approval, safeguarding and publication terms.

Technical consortium

Combine AI evaluation methods with software development, security, academic review or sector specialists when a project exceeds one organisation's capacity.

Partnerships should be represented as possibilities until agreements and roles have been confirmed. No endorsement, collaboration or bid activity is implied by a link to an external organisation.

6. Submission preparation and opportunity screening

For each live tender or grant, keep a record of the official reference number, eligibility, geographic reach, contract term, deadline, estimated value, deliverables, evaluation criteria and the supplier or project-lead requirements. The following table is a reusable screening framework, not a record of submitted bids.

9. Sustainably managed operations: outsourcing administration

A lean commercial research entity can commission external operational help while keeping research direction and final contractual decisions under the directors' oversight. Suggested lightweight operating model

Management: retain programme strategy, research methods and final approval. Monthly operations: accountant reviews transactions, managed IT provider reports security issues and a part-time administrator monitors grant and tender deadlines. As required: solicitor checks contracts and procurement terms; a grant or bid specialist prepares eligible submissions. Quarterly assurance: review costs, compliance calendar, cyber reports, supplier performance and project delivery.

When selecting a cyber provider, require written scopes, least-privilege access, incident-notification terms, backup testing and exit provisions. See National Cyber Security Centre guidance. When using a personal-data processor, the organisation must satisfy itself about the provider and put an appropriate contract in place; see ICO guidance.

Structure choice: for initial commercial contracts and innovation work, a limited company with outsourced administration is a practical candidate. For charitable grant eligibility and non-profit institute governance, examine a separate company limited by guarantee or CIO only if a viable mission, funding mix and management model justify the additional governance. Before incorporation, verify whether an existing entity can meet the needs, and obtain tailored legal and tax advice.

10. Can incorporation wait until funding or a contract is secured?

In some cases, yes. The sustainable approach is to screen opportunities first and incorporate only when the buyer or funder confirms that this sequence is permitted. A conditional offer, preferred-bidder notification, signed award agreement and first payment are different milestones; neither a promising discussion nor a conditional indication creates guaranteed cash flow. Do not assume a contract awarded to an individual can be transferred to a new company without the buyer's approval.

Lean sequencing: (1) build the research portfolio and capability statement; (2) identify eligible calls that permit individuals or late incorporation; (3) secure written clarification and, where possible, a conditional award subject to formation; (4) engage an accountant and solicitor to confirm structure, liability, payment terms and tax implications; (5) register only when necessary and put the contracting, bank, insurance and compliance arrangements in place before performance or receipt of company funds; (6) plan for payment delays and costs that precede the first invoice or grant drawdown.

Important financial qualification: Contract award does not necessarily mean immediate payment. Procurement can involve payment in arrears, mobilisation costs, approval gates, and delayed invoices; grant funding may be reimbursed against eligible spend. Budget for this gap rather than assuming award equals positive cashflow.

Primary guidance: Procurement conditions of participation; UKRI applicant eligibility; Innovate UK funding rules; Company registration.

Five written questions to put to a buyer or funder
  1. May I apply personally as an independent researcher or unincorporated applicant?
  2. If the proposal succeeds, may a company be formed after a conditional decision but before contract signature or funds are released?
  3. Would the new legal entity need a fresh financial, technical and eligibility assessment, and must the buyer approve any change of contracting party?
  4. What is the earliest evidence of legal identity, bank account, insurance, governance or financial history that you require?
  5. At what point is funding legally committed, what costs must be incurred before payment, and when would the first payment normally be made?

This does not represent existing laboratories or permanent scientific teams.
