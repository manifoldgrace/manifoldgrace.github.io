A source-preserving preparation space for the MA AI, Ethics and Society (2024–25), alongside a separate 2026 landscape review and prospective doctoral work. This is not a replacement for the approved syllabus, proof of qualification completion, or an official university examination. Existing research is substantive; new candidate studies are marked separately.

Programme baseline: 180 credits at Level 7: two 30-credit core modules, 60 elective credits, and a 60-credit independent dissertation. For the two-year October part-time route, the 2024–25 specification places the two taught core modules in Year 1 and the dissertation plus remaining options in Year 2. The dissertation module describes a 12,000-word submission. Original approved specification.

Interactive reasoning and knowledge audit — 50 questions

Five questions in each of ten domains, with dated facts, conceptual distinctions, methodological inference and programme-specific requirements. Answers are not transmitted to a server. Each category reports a percentage of available points, not a genuine population percentile rank; a population percentile would require a validated comparison cohort. Questions are for revision and cannot certify doctoral or degree-level competence.

Dissertation, thesis and viva-style defence

Existing research draft: Ethical Personhood, Bias, and Systemic Moral Accountability in AI, including human bias, unethical delegation, responsibility and value-embedding in socio-technical infrastructures. The uploaded dissertation draft establishes that this is already a developed research direction; the page does not imply it remains unstarted.

Master's requirement: a 60-credit dissertation, conventionally 12,000 words in the referenced module description. Doctoral standard: a defensible original contribution assessed through an institution-specific doctoral process. A viva-style rehearsal for MA work is useful, but does not imply that a formal viva was required by the original module.

Dissertation type possibilities

Normative-philosophical thesis; conceptual analysis; history of ideas; legal/doctrinal analysis; policy evaluation; critical socio-technical case study; qualitative interview/ethnographic work (with ethics approval); quantitative observational study; experimental human–AI evaluation; mixed-method investigation; design science and evaluated prototype; reproducibility/benchmark study; comparative institutional analysis; systematic review with a clearly defined contribution; article-based thesis where the doctoral institution permits it. These are possible designs, not all requirements of the MA.
