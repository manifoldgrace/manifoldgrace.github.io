Year 2 dissertation is independent research, not a substitute taught first year

The documented part-time route has 180 credits: two Year 1 core modules of 30 credits each, 60 optional credits spread across the programme, and a 60-credit Year 2 dissertation. The dissertation is an assessed, supervised, independent written research output; it is not automatically a published peer-reviewed paper, nor does an externally published paper automatically satisfy degree assessment. Publication, supervisor approval, submission, research ethics clearance and viva arrangements are distinct processes. A formal oral viva is not established as a mandatory component solely by the materials reviewed; the viva exercises on this page are developmental doctoral-defence preparation.

How to self-validate reasoning rather than self-certify expertise

The existing 50-item quiz measures within-site percentage correct, not population percentile rank or external academic accreditation. Add a separate evidence-based reasoning portfolio for dates, occurrences, sources, frameworks, theories, concepts, policy, quantitative interpretation, uncertainty, case analysis, oral defence and counter-argument handling. A reliable self-assessment distinguishes recollection, unsupported confidence, correct answer, strength of evidence and ability to defend against alternative explanations. For each item record the source and as-of date; where a contradiction arises, record the correction and reason for revision.

Practical self-validation rubric and viva practice

Use four levels: 0 = cannot state accurately; 1 = recognises the term but cannot defend it; 2 = explains accurately with one source and application; 3 = compares competing sources, limitations and a strong objection; 4 = tests a novel prediction or method with reproducible evidence. A percent score equals points earned divided by possible points, not a statistical percentile. For doctoral viva practice: define the claim, locate its novelty relative to the strongest previous research, justify chosen data and methods, consider rival explanations, identify ethical constraints and describe what would falsify the conclusion.

2026 evidence: skills and qualification bottlenecks

UK government 2026 AI labour-market reporting states that 97% of surveyed respondents identified at least one AI skills gap; 35% of organisations struggled to fill AI roles. Its separate analysis of AI expert vacancy advertisements found that 99% asked for at least a bachelor's degree, with 37% requesting a PhD and 29% a master's degree. These figures describe sampled organisations and adverts, respectively, and must not be conflated with all UK employment. PwC's UK AI Jobs Barometer reports that postings requiring AI skills rose from about 112,000 to 180,000 in 2025. These findings support studying competence signals and pathways, but neither prove nor disprove unfair institutional exclusion in any particular case.

Separate academic vocabulary and cross-sector terminology register

Academic vocabulary covers argument, inference, theory, methods and assessment. Technical/industry terminology names specific control measures, systems and procedures.
