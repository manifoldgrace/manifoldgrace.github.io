International Collaborative Intelligence & AI Safety Consortium Initiative

This proposal explores whether an independent research convener could coordinate complementary AI systems, researchers and institutions to strengthen evidence verification, cross-model reasoning and public-interest safety evaluation. The initiative is a conceptual development of existing research, not an assertion that external organisations have joined or that a legal consortium exists.

Research directory · Research question · Paper initiative and protocol · Productivity log

1. Conceptual idea and context

High-performance AI models often possess distinct strengths, limitations and failure patterns. Sharing structured questions, evidence and qualified outputs among independently governed systems could improve verification without concentrating ownership. A consortium might supply reproducible benchmarks, independent scrutiny and interdisciplinary governance, but also introduces privacy, competitive neutrality, correlation and coordination risks.

Each stage depends on evidence and willing participants.

3. Research question and context

RQ — Under what measurable conditions does an independently governed, cross-organisational multi-model research consortium produce greater safety, epistemic reliability and public-interest value than either corporate consolidation or independent single-provider research?

The question arises because technical coordination and ownership consolidation are not equivalent. Claims about collective intelligence require cost- and compute-matched baselines, careful measurement of correlated errors, partner incentives and genuine independence. Negative results should be preserved. The related but distinct technical question is whether cross-model adjudication increases reliability; the institutional question tests whether a consortium arrangement improves governance, replicability and accessibility.

6. Public status and safeguards

Proposal scoped for research; no institutional participation, funding, incorporation, endorsed partners or operational multi-provider infrastructure is asserted. External data sharing requires appropriate permissions. The project should not publish private records or personal names in the productivity log. Disagreements remain visible rather than being overwritten by a consensus statement.
