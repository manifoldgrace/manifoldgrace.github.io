Comparative AI Systems Architecture & Evidence Atlas

A cross-industry research structure for identifying AI and associated software systems, mapping architectural relationships, comparing alternative designs, investigating their tools, and assessing evidence for technical, legal and ethical claims. This complements broad encyclopaedic accounts by tracking system-level detail and unresolved limitations.

Status: Research framework and sector directory established. Entries are analytical starting categories, not verified inventories of every organisation or system. No organisation, product or deployment is certified compliant or ethical by inclusion here.

Research process

Discover systems → identify source maps → extract components, interfaces and dependencies → compare alternative configurations → verify against documentation and authorised tests → map obligations and ethical impacts → document evidence, contradictions and unresolved questions.

Industry directories

Expand an industry, then expand a second level to inspect its systems, tools, comparisons, governance and evidence gaps. Categories overlap intentionally; cross-industry technologies are linked conceptually rather than duplicated as separate claims.

Historical practice: early-years programming and educational robotics (2017)

2017 — Caterham private-school work: programmable robot ball and LEGO activities

First-person historical account: The researcher reports paid work at a private school in Caterham in 2017, involving programming activities with nursery-age children using a programmable spherical robot and LEGO-based programming. The exact school, model, programme, employment dates and lesson records have not been independently verified here.

Technology and historical sources

The described robot may be the Sphero SPRK or SPRK+ family, which was available for educational programming by 2017; the model remains to be confirmed. This historical experience connects block-based programming, robotics, computational thinking, sensor-actuator feedback and early-years pedagogy.

2017 Sphero Edu educator guide · LEGO Education

Comparative systems questions

Compare command sequencing, programming representations, physical feedback, learner agency, accessibility, supervision and age-appropriate interaction across programmable robot balls and LEGO educational robotics. Differentiate 2017 capabilities from contemporary systems.

Evidence and interpretation

Source status: personal recollection supplied in November 2026. Product guide confirms the existence of educational robotic-ball programming at the time, not employment at the school or the exact device used.
