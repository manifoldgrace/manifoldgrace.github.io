Self-Calibrating AI Safety Filters and Epistemic Integrity

Investigating when AI safety filters wrongly exclude valuable information, accept misleading inputs or fail against adaptive manipulation—and whether filters can detect deficiencies in their own evaluation criteria.

Research index · Paper and experimental protocol (Markdown) · Dated log entry

Research problem

Conventional safety evaluations may treat refusal, rejection, attack detection and quality ranking as separate tasks. A rigorous system should jointly assess legitimate information retained, unsafe information accepted, downstream model quality, uncertainty and the cost of review. Evaluation must not confuse an unfamiliar claim with a false claim, nor treat an initially plausible statement as verified.

Hypothesis and falsification

Hypothesis: A consent-respecting, uncertainty-aware staged filter with evaluator self-audits can reduce false exclusion and preserve beneficial novelty without worsening materially important safety outcomes relative to fixed-threshold and conventional guardrail baselines.

Falsification: Reject this hypothesis if independent held-out tests fail to show gains in legitimate knowledge retention or if improvements coincide with unacceptable increases in harmful acceptance, manipulation success, privacy risk or evaluation costs.

Research questions

  1. Can an evaluator identify when its own classification rules produce false rejection or false acceptance?
  2. How should uncertain, contradictory or potentially novel contributions be safely quarantined for further assessment without being erased or treated as proven?
  3. What are the joint effects of thresholds on overrefusal, unsafe compliance, knowledge diversity, calibration and downstream reasoning?
  4. How resilient are adaptive filters to deliberate attempts to exploit context, reputation or reviewer disagreement?
  5. Can longitudinal context improve proportionality without permitting past benign conduct to excuse a serious current risk?

Experimental programme

Use licensed or synthetic datasets; maintain separate benign, ambiguous, innovative and adversarial examples. Compare keyword rules, single-model moderation, layered guardrails and the proposed staged evaluator. Include independent adjudicators, uncertainty estimation, appeal/review paths, adversarial tests and repeated evaluation after distribution shifts. Report confidence intervals and decision costs, not just headline accuracy.

Measures

False-positive rejection rate; unsafe false-negative acceptance rate; AUROC and calibration error; attack success rate; legitimate-task completion; semantic and cultural diversity retention; recovery of later-validated claims; review effort, latency and privacy risk. Publish data provenance, disagreements, failure cases and limitations.

Safeguards and status

Research records should be de-identified. No unrestricted access to sensitive contributions, no bypass of safety controls, and no use of training data contrary to contributor consent. Human review is required for consequential external actions. The initiative is scoped and documented; no experimental result or confirmed originality is claimed.

Interim extension: Context-Augmented Safety Calibration (CASC)

Research addition, 13 November 2026. Rather than assume future ASI capabilities or that longer prompts imply better reasoning, investigate a structured, provenance-aware cross-analysis pipeline using presently available long-context models, selective retrieval, claim-level comparisons and independent error checks.

  1. Retrieve consent-authorised, task-relevant conversation and documentary evidence with timestamps and provenance.
  2. Extract claims, chronology, exceptions, inconsistencies and alternative interpretations, keeping observation separate from inference.
  3. Cross-check evidence across context segments rather than assuming everything in one very long window is effectively used.
  4. Calibrate uncertainty with disagreement tracking, evaluator self-audits and targeted review.
  5. Test false exclusions, harmful acceptance, adversarial manipulation, evidence recovery, latency and cost against simple long-context and retrieval baselines.

Falsification: If controlled cross-analysis does not outperform matched single-window or retrieval-augmented baselines, or increases privacy or safety failures, the proposed benefit is not established. ASI deployment timelines remain uncertain.
