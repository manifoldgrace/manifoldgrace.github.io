Independent Innovation, Contributor Equity & Accountability in AI Development

Grace Rosemary Nettey · Manifold Grace
2 November 2026 · Research framing / working draft · Open to refinement

This strand investigates whether existing feedback and open-development systems fairly distinguish casual suggestions, substantial conceptual research, technical implementation, and sustained civic contributions. It does not allege that a named company has exploited an individual or adopted a proposal without permission; those would require evidence assessed independently.

Research question

How should AI companies and open technical ecosystems recognise, assess and, where appropriate, remunerate independently developed conceptual contributions while preserving open collaboration, parallel innovation, intellectual-property rights and reliable attribution?

Scope of inquiry

  • Contribution taxonomy: problem discovery, conceptual architecture, requirements analysis, test and governance design, code implementation, maintenance and public advocacy.
  • Attribution and provenance: dated disclosures, version histories, participant roles, acknowledgements, reasons for decisions, and boundaries of what records prove.
  • Economic participation: when unpaid suggestions become substantial work; potential research grants, paid consultations, bounties, licensing or partnerships, without assuming a right to payment from unsolicited feedback.
  • Fairness and opportunity: whether barriers to paid employment, technical networks, funding and visibility disproportionately affect underrepresented independent contributors.
  • Institutional accountability: transparency of intake, routing, substantive review, conflict-of-interest processes, correction mechanisms and optional contributor agreements.
  • Balance: the value of collective progress, independent invention, freedom to implement similar ideas and the cost of operating software infrastructures.

Analytical distinctions

Submission ≠ acknowledgement ≠ substantive assessment ≠ acceptance ≠ implementation ≠ causal influence. Dated public records can evidence the content and timing of a disclosure, but are not automatically proof of originality, ownership of abstract ideas, industry adoption or infringement. Ownership of specific written expression, inventions and implementations depends on applicable law, agreements and facts.

Provisional governance proposals for evaluation

  1. Provide clear contributor-facing categories for ordinary feature suggestions versus developed research specifications.
  2. Offer an optional reference number, stable timestamp and accessible receipt record.
  3. State separately whether a proposal was received, routed, technically reviewed, accepted or implemented.
  4. Publish transparent attribution options, including opt-in public credit and correction of misattribution.
  5. Explore compensation pathways for commissioned follow-up, research collaborations or prize/bounty programmes rather than imply every public idea earns payment.
  6. Assess equitable access and outcomes, with privacy safeguards and non-retaliatory complaints processes.
  7. Document existing related work and independent parallel development before making causation or originality claims.

Research method and evidence plan

Compare platform contribution terms, issue templates, developer-relations policies, public acknowledgements and case studies. Record whether a finding is documented, inferred, disputed or unresolved. Evaluate contributor costs, company benefits, research quality and the feasibility of alternative governance models. Future interviews or policy requests would require consent and careful separation of testimony from independently verifiable records.
