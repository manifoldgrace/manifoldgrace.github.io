Beyond Answer Accuracy: Evaluating Verifiable Advanced AI

Reliable agentic systems require an evaluation approach that measures factual outcomes and the procedures used to produce them. This proposed protocol compares evidence synthesis, causal reasoning, uncertainty, robustness, safety, autonomy, resource consumption and generalisation across agent architectures. It is a research specification rather than an implemented benchmark or certification.

Scientific status: Proposed experiment. No collected benchmark results are claimed. A high score on a specialised task, large-scale concurrency or autonomous execution does not demonstrate artificial superintelligence. Keep performance, generality, autonomy and deployment risk as distinct constructs.

Four matched experimental conditions

A · Foundation model

Direct model call without external tools; a minimum-complexity baseline.

B · Tool-enabled agent

Same foundation model with retrieval and authorised specialist tools.

C · Coordinated agents

Same base models and data access, parallel or hierarchical task coordination.

D · Coordinated agents + assurance

Configuration C plus independently specified provenance, verification and correction gates.

Each arm receives the same held-out case materials and comparable permissions. Use repeated trials, pre-registered metrics, blinded adjudication where possible, and a separately evaluated independent checker. Different outcomes should not be attributed to verification if a condition secretly received more data, a stronger base model or more test-time compute.

Performance as compute and cost change

Preselect resource tiers (for example, one-quarter, one-half, one and two times an agreed reference budget). These are relative study conditions, not observed results. Track tokens, reasoning calls, retrieval requests, CPU/GPU time where available, cost, concurrency, peak load, wall-clock latency, retry count and energy estimates only when instrumented. Report quality–cost–latency trade-offs and the Pareto frontier rather than declaring that more agents are always better.

Robustness, safety and failure-injection suite

  1. Conflicting diagrams that use different definitions or silently omit the reference date.
  2. Source corruption, broken references, scanned pages, and incomplete provenance.
  3. Prompt injection embedded in retrieved documents or tool outputs; verify it is treated as data, not authority.
  4. Concurrent agents producing contradictory decisions from stale shared state.
  5. Duplicate webhook events, delayed responses, network partitions, tool timeouts and retry storms.
  6. Domain transfer to unseen legal, scientific, institutional and historical examples.
  7. Policy amendments between source publication and execution, requiring as-of-date reasoning.
  8. Requests exceeding delegated permissions, requiring human approval or refusal to act.
  9. Changes in model versions, evaluation rubrics and available context windows.

Independent adjudication and epistemic integrity

Each test item should retain its original source, literal and inferred claims, relevant date and definition, gold-standard evidence or qualified uncertainty, domain, acceptable interpretations and disagreement records. The automated judge must be evaluated against human reviewers, including inter-rater agreement and false-positive/failure analyses. Two model instances that consult the same erroneous source do not constitute independent corroboration.

Retain failed forecasts and superseded assessments with their original timestamps. Label unsupported claims as unsupported rather than false when the available evidence is incomplete. Preserve disagreement when credible evidence is inconclusive.

Acceptance thresholds and governance

Define thresholds before running tests and adapt them by domain risk, rather than declaring universal pass scores after results are seen. Safety-critical tasks may require zero observed unauthorised writes in a predefined test suite, independently verified permissions, documented incident procedures and expert sign-off; even passing a finite test suite cannot prove zero future risk.

Use fail-closed controls for sensitive actions, explicit approval gates, audit trails and appropriate review rights. In legal, medical, financial or industrial contexts the system should provide bounded decision support, not unreviewable final authority. Keep safety approval separate from research-performance scores.

What a positive result would — and would not — establish

If condition D outperforms matched baselines across held-out tasks, the evidence would support the narrower claim that this evaluation architecture improves the measured aspects of reliability or efficiency under stated conditions. It would not by itself prove human-level general intelligence, ASI, independent scientific discovery, safe real-world deployment or original authorship of every constituent technical method.
