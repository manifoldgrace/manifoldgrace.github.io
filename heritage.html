01 — Ghanaian corridor

Immediate family line

My paternal family is Ga-Dangme from Accra. The immediate line preserved in my family archive runs through Grace → father → Christopher Akai Nettey → Herbert Mills Nettey. My grandfather Christopher Akai Nettey died on 15 May 2023 aged 88, and his substantial genealogical research into elders, descendants, wives, children and family branches remains a central foundation for this archive.

Swalaba, Sonmenaa and Gbese

The wider association with Swalaba, Sonmenaa and Gbese is strongly supported by public reporting on the Asafoatse C.B. Nettey Family of the Swalaba Quarter of Sonmenaa in Gbese. The exact generation-by-generation path connecting my documented line with the earlier historical figures is likely to be recoverable from surviving family and archival records, and I am researching that path further.

Earlier documented Nettey relationships

A 1936 West African Court of Appeal record identifies Akai Kofi Nettey as the eldest surviving son of Nii Nettey Quarshie in litigation involving Horse Road, Ussher Town. This gives the research a documented parent-child relationship in the historical Nettey record; Herbert Mills Nettey's precise position relative to this branch is likely to become clearer as the surviving family papers and court records are examined.

Horse Road property records

The British Library Endangered Archives Programme catalogues EAP1161/1/3/3/121 — Christopher Brandford Nettey of Horse Road, 20 September 1947 and EAP1161/1/3/3/122 — E. Q. Nettey of Accra, 20 September 1947. These records provide important municipal and property anchors for reconstructing the family environment, and the relationship between the people named in the files is being researched further through the underlying correspondence and plans.

1959 Malm–Nettey research branch

The reported High Court decision Norquaye-Tetteh v. Malm & Anor. [1959] GLR 368 records the Malm land history and identifies Anna Malm and Emmanuel Naki/Nakai Nettey within that litigation. This branch is potentially relevant to the wider Nettey family network, and I am researching the exact relationship further.

Current archival roadmap

  1. Grandfather's original genealogy papers — to map the internal family lists of elders, wives, children and branches.
  2. PRAAD / Judicial Service records for suits 129/1958 and 130/1958 — to recover fuller testimony, pleadings and exhibits.
  3. Lands Commission deed material connected with the 3 December 1958 registration — to inspect the instrument, endorsements and witness block.

Ghanaian corridor conclusion: the immediate family line and the wider Accra/Swalaba/Sonmenaa/Gbese setting are now well-defined, while the earlier generational bridge is likely and is being researched further through primary records.

02 — English corridor

Biological and adoptive family history

My maternal heritage includes English biological ancestry alongside a legal-adoptive family history through the Symondson family. I record biological descent, legal adoption and collateral kinship as distinct forms of family relationship so that each can be followed accurately through its own sources.

Documented musical and military family record

The published Symondson/Simmonds genealogy records Henry Symondson (1832–1911) as a musician, band sergeant and staff sergeant; Henry John Symondson (1860–1926) as a musician with the Scots Guards; Alfred Stuart Symondson (1865–1930) as a professional military musician; and Sydney Frederick Symondson (1862–1927) within the same documented family and musical network.

The connected Danby line includes John Danby (1757–1798), singer, organist and composer, whose professional record includes the Royal Society of Musicians and service as organist at the Spanish Embassy chapel.

Simmonds–Deakin and deeper collateral pedigree

The published genealogy traces the Symondson/Simmonds → Deakin branch, including the 1921 marriage of Alfred Henry Percy Simmonds and Margaret Patience Deakin. The same published pedigree extends through Beaufort-related collateral ancestry to John of Gaunt and Edward III. My grandmother's exact position within the wider Symondson network is likely to become increasingly precise as the adoption and civil records are assembled, and I am researching that connection further.

English corridor conclusion: the legal-adoptive and wider published family histories provide a substantial documented framework, with the precise connection between my grandmother's branch and the wider historical pedigree continuing to be refined.

03 — How claims are presented

Significant family-history statements on this page are linked either to a family-archive source note or to a publicly accessible record. Where the evidence points strongly toward a relationship that is still being completed generation by generation, the wording used is likely / researching further.

  • Documented: supported by a family primary record, legal record, archival catalogue, court record or other identifiable source.
  • Strongly supported: several independent facts align around the same family, place or historical structure.
  • Likely / researching further: the evidence points toward the connection and the precise linking record is being sought.
