01 · Autism, brain imaging & research context

Supporting autistic success

The aim is accessible learning, durable recall and confidence in using knowledge. Success can include retaining vocabulary, solving unfamiliar problems and needing less effort—not conforming to a non-autistic presentation.

What the science supports · outcomes & AI

Cairney, Haebig and Lucas found that retrieval practice with feedback improved associative learning in autistic adults; performance and benefits were comparable to non-autistic participants in their study. This supports offering effective learning strategies, while evaluating each learner’s needs.

Dunlosky and colleagues rated practice testing and distributed practice highly across learning contexts. Dresler and colleagues linked six weeks of mnemonic training with memory gains and changes in functional connectivity; that was not an autism-specific intervention.

AI can help: draft source-checked questions, adjust explanations and difficulty, suggest reviews from errors, and analyse repeated comparable tasks. Akemoğlu, Roberts and Xiong’s review found that only two AI intervention studies met WWC standards without reservations; effectiveness needs stronger evidence. Personalisation is promising, not an assured outcome.

Here: exercises and scheduling use transparent browser rules, not an AI model. Measure delayed recall and transfer, alongside optional effort ratings. Scores do not measure neural change. Images and AI classification results provide context, not a ranking of autistic capability.

Reading Omar Dekhil and colleagues

A Comprehensive Framework for Differentiating Autism Spectrum Disorder From Neurotypicals by Fusing Structural MRI and Resting State Functional MRI · Seminars in Pediatric Neurology, 34, 100805.

The research, methods and findings belong to the original authors. Grace’s contribution here is exploratory reading, source curation and reflection as part of her own research.

Original article · PubMed record · DOI

Reported findings

The authors combined structural MRI features with resting-state fMRI features in a computer-aided classification framework. Their evaluation covered 18 ABIDE datasets and 1,060 participants. The abstract reports structural-MRI accuracy ranging from 75% to 100%, and fMRI accuracy from 79% to 100%. These are ranges across datasets, rather than one overall clinical accuracy.

Regional features were examined to help identify patterns contributing to classification. The authors proposed the approach as a potential aid to interpreting neuroimaging. These results do not establish a routine diagnostic test, identify an individual’s masking, or demonstrate learning-induced neuroplasticity.

How AI enters the study

The paper describes local K-nearest-neighbour classification, feature ranking and random-forest classification, followed by fusion of selected structural and functional features. AI is used to recognise statistical patterns in MRI-derived data.

03a · Memory Studio · ten approaches to try

A self-paced game window: encode → retrieve → check → revisit. Choose a strategy and practise your own vocabulary, formulas or key facts. No speed penalty or competitive leaderboard.

1 · Memory palace

Place one item at each familiar location; walk the route mentally.

Strong mnemonic evidence for trained tasks; generalisation varies.

2 · Spaced repetition

Retrieve, check feedback, then return after an increasing interval.

Strong evidence for distributed practice.

3 · Active recall

Hide the source and retrieve before checking the answer.

Strong evidence, especially with corrective feedback.

4 · Chunking

Group related items into meaningful units that suit your capacity.

Grouping helps encoding; no universal five-to-seven-item rule.

5 · Acronyms & acrostics

Use initial letters to create a memorable cue.

Useful for particular lists; check full meanings too.

6 · Explain simply

Explain the idea to a beginner, then identify missing steps.

Related to self-explanation; the named Feynman routine is not independently guaranteed.

7 · Mind mapping

Connect a central idea to labelled branches and relationships.

An organisation tool; add retrieval rather than copying alone.

8 · Link & story

Connect items in a vivid story, keeping their order clear.

A mnemonic aid whose benefit depends on task and use.

9 · Multi-sensory engagement

Combine a relevant image, spoken phrase or movement with meaning.

Benefits depend on the task; this is not learning-style matching.

10 · Blank-page recall

Reconstruct keywords on blank paper, then check and correct.

A format of retrieval practice, rather than a distinct validated treatment.

Answer & reasoning

8 — 3²−1=8.

07 · Moral philosophy · theories & reasoning
Consequentialism

Evaluate actions by their consequences. Ask whose welfare counts and how uncertain outcomes are handled.

Quiz: Which family judges actions primarily by outcomes?

Answer

Consequentialism.

Deontology

Evaluate duties, principles and rights, including constraints on how people may be treated.

Quiz: Does a desirable outcome automatically override every duty?

Answer

No; deontological approaches can impose constraints.

Virtue ethics

Examine character, practical wisdom and human flourishing.

Quiz: What does virtue ethics foreground?

Answer

Character and practical wisdom.

Ethics of care

Attend to relationships, dependency, vulnerability and responsibilities of care.

Quiz: What can a purely abstract rule overlook?

Answer

The needs and relationships of particular people.

Justice & rights

Examine fair procedures, distribution, recognition and protections.

Quiz: Are equal treatment and equitable support always identical?

Answer

No; relevant differences may require different support.

Moral epistemology

Ask how moral claims are justified, challenged and revised.

Quiz: What distinguishes confidence from justification?

Answer

Justification supplies reasons that can be examined.

Applied ethics

Test principles in technology, healthcare, institutions and everyday choices.

Quiz: What should a case analysis identify first?

Answer

Facts, uncertainty, stakeholders, options and competing values.
