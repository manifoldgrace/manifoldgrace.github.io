Active Research: Evidence-Ready Priorities and Next Deliverables

This page is a navigational and decision-support layer over existing Manifold Grace research. Its purpose is to reduce repeated setup and context switching, identify the next demonstrable research output, and support focused independent investigation. Original pages, submission dates, experiments and deferred investigations remain authoritative and untouched.

Scope and confidence: This is a qualitative, provisional prioritisation, not a measured stochastic forecast, task-completion claim or promise of automatic research. Value and evidence readiness are estimated editorial categories. Uncertainty and effort are relative planning judgements; no numerical probabilities, invented returns or completed experiments are asserted.

Uncertainty-aware prioritisation rather than a fabricated score

1. Evidence readiness

Can the next claim be verified with accessible records or a synthetic test?

2. Practical value

Does it create a reusable research, career, teaching or policy artefact?

3. Complexity and uncertainty

What expertise, compute, sources and independent checks are still missing?

4. Opportunity to reuse

Will the result resolve several open questions without overgeneralising?

Prioritise high-readiness, reusable outputs; maintain lower-readiness hypotheses without pretending to have completed their investigations. Update rankings when new evidence arrives. A future empirical prioritisation model could use observed task completion, effort and failure distributions, but this page does not yet have those data.

A coherent first research-delivery bundle

Rather than expanding every domain simultaneously, combine the first, second, third and tenth streams into one bounded, inspectable demonstration. The proposed bundle has four reusable parts:

  1. Synthetic evidence pack: 15 fictional, non-sensitive claim/source pairs, with several conflicting dates, changes of definition and missing references. Make clear they are illustrative rather than real institutional findings.
  2. Machine-readable claim ledger: statement, source, source version, timestamp, explicit versus inferred relationship, supported/disputed/unknown label, review status and correction history.
  3. Reference rubric: evidence coverage, contradiction correctness, temporal reasoning, abstention when uncertain, authorisation, latency and computation estimates when measured.
  4. Transparent test demonstration: compare a basic summariser with a deterministic evidence-aware checker; only later test actual model agents with comparable resources and independent adjudication.

These are specifications for a first bundle, not claims that the datasets, code, experiments or expert reviews have already been produced. The existing evaluation protocol supplies the methodological constraints and the research lab provides an appropriate educational context.

First reusable artefact: synthetic claim test pack

Version 0.1 contains 15 fictional claim pairs with reference relation labels, rationales and explicit synthetic provenance. The examples cover contradiction, temporal compatibility, changed rules, scope and metric mismatch, missing evidence, formal-proof overclaims, uncertainty, attribution and dependency review. These are illustrative reference annotations, not an empirically validated benchmark. Open the machine-readable JSON test pack.

Starter scoring: evaluate relation classification accuracy per category, false contradiction rate, abstention on ambiguous cases and explanation faithfulness; record human disagreements, model and prompt versions, tool permissions, latency and measured resource use. Do not attach measured accuracy or a confidence interval until actual repeated trials have been performed.

Progression and publication gates

Mapped

Scope, terminology, domain and links to existing research recorded.

Specified

Hypothesis, evidence needs, limitations and proposed measures documented.

Prototyped

Actual executable artefact exists and inputs/outputs are reproducible.

Evaluated

Independent checks, failures, costs and uncertainty recorded.

Published finding

Claims supported by the documented evaluation, not by the existence of a draft page.

Preserve source versions, original contributor records and as-of dates across all stages. A feature request remains a feature request even if a related capability appears elsewhere later; influence and independent invention require separately verified evidence.
