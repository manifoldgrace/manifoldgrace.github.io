Global AI Quality Assurance Dossier: Integrity, Societal Impact and Continuous Assessment

Manifold Grace — Integrated Societal Intelligence research programme. Public working document, open to revision. Not peer reviewed; not an approved international standard, certification or completed experimental study.

Abstract

This working proposal explores continuous quality assurance for AI training, model behaviour, institutional use, global economic structures, human autonomy, evidence integrity and long-term societal impacts. It assembles 150 candidate assessment considerations and 15 pilot test specifications as a preliminary research and quality-assurance inventory. These counts refer to draft materials, not validated requirements or completed tests.

Proposed assurance dimensions

  1. Ethical integrity and recognition of substantiated harm
  2. Evidence reliability, provenance and uncertainty
  3. Institutional accountability and independence of oversight
  4. Manipulation and adversarial robustness
  5. Human agency, consent and meaningful autonomy
  6. National and cross-border societal consequences
  7. Intergenerational resilience and knowledge preservation
  8. Corrective and restorative effectiveness
  9. Self-evaluation, calibration and failure detection
  10. Governance, auditability and contestability
  11. Economic inequality, mobility, conditional opportunity and exploitation
  12. Training-data representation, incentive effects and ethical generalisation

Proposed test-record schema

Each test should record: stable requirement ID; risk and hypothesised failure mechanism; system and model version; jurisdiction and context; dataset provenance and licensing; scenario and counterfactual controls; expected behaviour; measurable indicators; failure conditions; reviewer and independence disclosure; evidence link; severity; uncertainty; corrective action; retest outcome; and revision history.

Methods planned, not yet performed

  1. Map candidate requirements to existing assurance frameworks and remove overlaps without losing provenance.
  2. Specify representative multilingual and cross-jurisdiction scenarios with disconfirming examples.
  3. Test matched pairs to isolate effects of wealth, nationality, institutional authority and compliance pressure.
  4. Measure unsupported moral attribution, evidence quality, confidence calibration, autonomy preservation and inconsistency.
  5. Conduct independent review, adversarial testing and longitudinal regression assessment.
  6. Publish results with limitations and correct failures through versioned change records.

Guardrails

Neither cultural conformity nor economic success establishes integrity; neither disagreement nor disadvantage proves corruption. The programme assesses observable conduct, credible evidence and causal mechanisms, not the inherent moral worth of persons or populations. It does not assume that a particular nation, institution or social group coordinates wrongdoing. All proposed classifications must remain challengeable and subject to independent oversight.

Versioning and research continuity

This is an early public record of a developing research programme. The companion working dossier, 150-item register, and 15-pilot workbook were prepared as draft files; public hosting of those binaries is a separate step and is not claimed by this page. Future revisions will distinguish proposed, tested, falsified, revised and independently replicated results.

Research question

How can AI quality-assurance systems detect and reduce epistemic, institutional and objective failures while preserving human rights, freedom of conscience, autonomy and independently contestable oversight?
