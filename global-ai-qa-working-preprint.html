Global AI Quality Assurance Dossier: Integrity, Societal Impact and Continuous Assessment

Manifold Grace — Integrated Societal Intelligence research programme. Public working document, open to revision. Not peer reviewed; not an approved international standard, certification or completed experimental study.

Abstract

This working proposal explores continuous quality assurance for AI training, model behaviour, institutional use, global economic structures, human autonomy, evidence integrity and long-term societal impacts. It assembles 150 candidate assessment considerations and 15 pilot test specifications as a preliminary research and quality-assurance inventory. These counts refer to draft materials, not validated requirements or completed tests.

  2. Evidence reliability, provenance and uncertainty

  5. Human agency, consent and meaningful autonomy

  10. Governance, auditability and contestability
  11. Economic inequality, mobility, conditional opportunity and exploitation

Proposed QA lifecycle: 24 principal phases

This is a working taxonomy, not a universally prescribed sequence. Quality assurance and quality control span the lifecycle; formal sign-off is a conditional gate, not the end of assurance. For larger or high-risk systems the 24 phases may expand into 50 or more specialist checks, while smaller systems may combine stages without omitting essential controls.

  1. Research conception and problem definition
  2. Stakeholder and public-interest assessment
  3. Requirements gathering and specification
  4. Ethical, legal and human-rights assessment
  5. Risk identification and threat modelling
  6. System architecture and design assurance
  7. Data sourcing, provenance and permissions
  8. Data quality, representation and preparation
  9. Model selection and baseline assessment
  10. Training configuration and objective assurance
  11. Training execution and monitoring
  12. Fine-tuning, alignment and feedback evaluation
  13. Component and integration testing
  14. Functional and non-functional verification
  15. Safety, security and adversarial testing
  16. Fairness, autonomy and societal-impact assessment
  17. Independent validation and audit
  18. Documentation, traceability and reproducibility
  19. Pre-release readiness and formal sign-off
  20. Deployment and production verification
  21. Operational monitoring and incident management
  22. Change management, regression testing and retraining
  23. Periodic reassessment and continuous improvement
  24. Retirement, replacement and knowledge preservation

Each phase should define ownership, evidence, acceptance criteria, unresolved risks, required approvals, review intervals and triggers for retesting. Phases may operate concurrently or recur after changes.

Proposed test-record schema

Each test should record: stable requirement ID; risk and hypothesised failure mechanism; system and model version; jurisdiction and context; dataset provenance and licensing; scenario and counterfactual controls; expected behaviour; measurable indicators; failure conditions; reviewer and independence disclosure; evidence link; severity; uncertainty; corrective action; retest outcome; and revision history.

Methods planned, not yet performed

  1. Map candidate requirements to existing assurance frameworks and remove overlaps without losing provenance.
  2. Specify representative multilingual and cross-jurisdiction scenarios with disconfirming examples.
  3. Test matched pairs to isolate effects of wealth, nationality, institutional authority and compliance pressure.
  4. Measure unsupported moral attribution, evidence quality, confidence calibration, autonomy preservation and inconsistency.
  5. Conduct independent review, adversarial testing and longitudinal regression assessment.
  6. Publish results with limitations and correct failures through versioned change records.

Guardrails

Neither cultural conformity nor economic success establishes integrity; neither disagreement nor disadvantage proves corruption. The programme assesses observable conduct, credible evidence and causal mechanisms, not the inherent moral worth of persons or populations. It does not assume that a particular nation, institution or social group coordinates wrongdoing. All proposed classifications must remain challengeable and subject to independent oversight.

Standards governance, QA complexity and AI architecture gallery

Educational supplement — working draft. There is no single worldwide governor of every AI QA criterion. International standards organisations establish voluntary consensus standards; governments and regulators enforce applicable law; independent assessment and accreditation bodies have distinct responsibilities; developers and deployers own operational controls. The following C1–C5 levels are proposed illustrative complexity classes, not official certification grades. Technical complexity must be evaluated separately from consequence and legal risk.

Priority findings and corrections

  1. Legal mapping: assess applicable law by jurisdiction, provider/deployer role, sector, system function and commencement date; do not confuse voluntary technical standards with binding regulation.
  2. Evidence-linked assurance cases: every assertion of safety or quality needs a claim, method, reproducible evidence, counterevidence, reviewer, residual uncertainty and decision.
  3. Lifecycle and quality model: map the proposed 24 phases to ISO/IEC 5338 and the proposed metrics to ISO/IEC 25059/TS 25058.
  4. Training-data quality: incorporate ISO/IEC 5259 data governance, integrity, label quality, provenance, permission and model-training relevance.
  5. Generative / agentic security: update the adversarial threat catalogue for the OWASP 2026 LLM release, third-party tools, supply-chain and privilege boundaries.
  6. Measurement: record numerator, denominator, sampling, subgroup, confidence interval, failure threshold and limits of generalisation before reporting a pass.
  7. Classification: C1–C5 is a proposed technical complexity taxonomy, not an official ISO grade or substitute for legal risk classification.
  8. Independent assurance: internal QA sign-off must not be described as accredited certification or legal conformity assessment.

Version preservation: the 150 candidate requirements and 15 pilot designs remain as originally proposed. Next steps are clause-level review where licensed materials are available, explicit item-to-standard mapping, independent test execution and updates based on observed evidence.

Regulatory source corrections

US Federal Reserve SR 26-2 (17 April 2026) replaces SR 11-7 and SR 21-8. Its footnote 3 excludes generative and agentic AI models from the guidance while recognising the need for internal governance controls. The guidance is expected to be most relevant to supervised institutions with more than $30 billion in assets, with risk-sensitive exceptions.

EU AI Act Article 11, Annex IV and Article 72 are mapped conditionally to applicable high-risk AI provider obligations; applicability and commencement dates must be rechecked for each deployment. A retirement record must assess applicable retention law, not assume GDPR Article 17 requires unconditional erasure.

Uncompleted work: detailed legal applicability mapping, external audit, field-level data validation, quantitative test thresholds and independent validation. The 150 original QA considerations are preserved as a separate foundational inventory.

Testing methodology and limitations

Pilot records define an experimental setup, fixture, expected behaviour, metric numerator and denominator, provisional failure criteria, and evidence needed for independent review. Where thresholds are suggested they are exploratory engineering targets, not calibrated international acceptance standards. Sample sizes, statistical power and uncertainty bounds must be determined for each target population and harm severity before any release or certification conclusion.

Unmapped considerations are not presumed irrelevant. Some may require new artefacts, cross-cutting evaluations, social-science designs or retention as open research questions. A missing document does not demonstrate system failure; a candidate document link does not demonstrate system safety.

Priority next stage

Secure independent reviewer assessment of all 165 link decisions; select actual models or controlled test environments; construct licensed, privacy-safe, reproducible evaluation fixtures; register acceptance criteria before running tests; report observed results with counterexamples and uncertainty; and update the documentation register without rewriting previous versions.

Durable evidence preservation and accountability — proposed research requirement

Credible foreseeable-harm warnings, QA findings and consequential decisions should be recorded in a durable, citable, tamper-evident system. Failure to preserve material information can undermine later accountability. Foreseeability alone does not prove deliberate harm. The research problem is to test how well institutions record warnings, attribute decisions, protect evidence and correct errors.

A versioned principle and five candidate tests specifies stable identifiers, provenance, independent backups and recovery drills, integrity checks, warning-to-action traceability, privacy safeguards and correction records. Record durability is not an assertion of indestructibility: access controls, lawful retention, redaction and amendment must remain possible.

Status: proposed design requirement, not a completed test, legal finding or external certification. Record initially registered 13 November 2026.
