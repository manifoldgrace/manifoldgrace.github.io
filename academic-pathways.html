Approach sequence — evidence, shortlist, contact and decision
  1. Define two or three doctoral research questions and the methods they would require.
  2. Identify departments that investigate those questions; check each university's official admissions requirements.
  3. List potential supervisors with exact roles, academic interests and official sources.
  4. Read several recent publications per candidate and save source links, publication dates and methodological notes.
  5. Rate alignment: topic, method, supervision availability, department and feasible funding.
  6. Shortlist a manageable number across universities and departments.
  7. Prepare a concise enquiry with the research question, relevant preparation and specific paper-based questions.
  8. Contact only via verified official channels; log dates and responses privately.
  9. Confirm RPL, entry routes, application documents, funding and deadlines separately with admissions.
  10. Record outcomes and revise the shortlist without turning a non-response into a conclusion.

Funding eligibility and application decision checklist
  1. Confirm the specific degree, programme, department and mode of attendance.
  2. Check nationality, fee status, residence and any scheme-specific conditions.
  3. Compare fully funded studentships with fee-only or partial awards.
  4. Verify whether applications require a separate funding form, course application, supervisor approval or institutional nomination.
  5. Check exact course and funding deadlines separately.
  6. Prepare transcripts, references, research proposal, statement and relevant evidence before submitting.
  7. Ask the university directly about non-standard qualifications and recognition of prior learning where relevant.
  8. Save the published terms and dated confirmation of applications for your records.

Important funding distinctions, eligibility and sensible next steps

Funded studentships may cover fees and provide a stipend; many doctoral routes are awarded through the university rather than an individual applying to UKRI. Read UKRI studentship guidance.

Some Master's scholarships cover only part of tuition fees. Loans require repayment and are not grants: check government postgraduate funding separately. Available schemes may depend on home/overseas fee classification, entry qualification, subject, study mode, deadlines and funding already awarded.

  1. Identify an academically suitable programme and verify its admissions and RPL rules.
  2. Check the university's current scholarship search and associated departmental funding pages.
  3. Record award type, covered fees, stipend, eligibility, study mode, separate application requirements and deadline.
  4. Ask the institution whether part-time or non-traditional routes are supported where relevant.
  5. Save the dated official answer and only then shortlist funding applications.

This is a manually researched snapshot with links back to official pages; it does not scrape or update itself automatically. Do not assume an old award remains open without confirming on the provider's website.
