What the public record can establish about an AI skills contract

Working paper · v0.1 · 15 November 2026

Companion project and open evidence gaps

Abstract

This public-record case study examines Wave 6, Lot 3 (AI in Business) of the Oxfordshire Skills Bootcamps procurement, OCID ocds-h6vhtk-0544a3. The records establish the tender baseline, award, published value and signature. They support a programme-level statement that Wave 6 delivery was underway. They leave actual Lot 3 payments and learner outcomes unresolved. The contribution is a method for identifying which missing record matters to which claim.

Question and method

What can an outside reader verify about delivery and payment for one lot? Match each claim to the procurement, lot, wave, period and reporting entity. Compare the specification with the award and contract-details notices, then check the June 2026 scrutiny pack, three council spending workbooks covering July 2025 to September 2026, four council contract-register releases from October 2025 to October 2026, and indexed public disclosure results. Searches used supplier and contracting-body names, the notice and procurement references, and programme terms. This is a bounded public-record audit.

Findings

The tender baseline is visible

The specification sets a minimum of 30 learners, a £4,000 unit-cost cap, at least 60 guided learning hours and three payment milestones of 30%, 35% and 35%. The published contract details give £120,000 excluding VAT, signature on 7 October 2025 and a stated initial term from 8 October 2025 to 30 September 2026, with a possible extension. The tender minimum supplies no observed qualifying-start denominator. Planned dates in the tender and dates in the notice establish different stages of procurement; actual mobilisation and the operative schedules remain open.

The programme update has a limited scope

The June scrutiny pack gives 56% of starts achieved, 70% completions and 118% of the outcome target for Wave 5. It describes Wave 6 delivery as underway. These figures therefore supply no Lot 3 outcome estimate. The completion and outcome claims need a lot-level return with explicit denominators and dates.

Award value and payment require different records

The council workbook searches yielded no payment line attributable to Lot 3. The contract details notice identifies Oxfordshire Local Enterprise Partnership Limited as the contracting authority. Payment by that entity requires a search of the relevant records held by or for that entity. Cabinet Office guidance excludes procurements begun before 1 April 2026 from mandatory section 70 payment publication. Routine section 71 KPI reporting and specific breach or poor-performance notices have distinct triggers. The absence of a matching public record supplies no evidence of payment, non-payment or breach.

Limits and implications

The documents reviewed leave the final signed delivery and payment schedules, any variation, the buyer-validated monitoring return and actual payments unverified. Additional records could change individual findings. In particular, a completion count resolves the count alone unless the applicable denominator is also known. A buyer-validated lot return and matched payment ledger are the highest-priority evidence requests. This study makes a case-specific finding about public verifiability.
