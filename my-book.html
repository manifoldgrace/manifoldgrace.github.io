50 by 30

A reflective book built from an existing record of fifty things pursued, achieved, commenced or deliberately experienced by thirty, with emphasis on what was meaningfully within my control rather than on comparison with somebody else's timetable.

Trojan Horse

When Government Betrays the People It Was Elected to Serve. A research-led examination of what happens when public institutions repeatedly fail to protect economic opportunity, living standards, fair recruitment, civic trust and legitimate interests — and what accountability ought to follow.

The work connects political responsibility, immigration and integration debates, public administration, employment, and the changing role of faith and community organisations. It asks when repeated, foreseeable and uncorrected harm amounts to a betrayal of public trust, while distinguishing policy failure, negligence and deliberate wrongdoing from legal treason.

Read a short introduction

What happens when citizens pursue education, contribute professionally, follow public rules and still encounter institutions that frustrate their ability to progress? What happens when a government knows its choices may cause serious harm but fails to examine, prevent or remedy it?

Trojan Horse begins with lived experience and tests wider claims against public records, evidence and competing perspectives. The manuscript also explores trust, belonging, individual experiences of integration and the difference between genuine community support and the pursuit of institutional influence.

This is a personal and analytical enquiry, not a claim that ethnicity, nationality, accent or immigration status establishes disloyalty. Detailed personal accounts and unpublished findings are reserved for the eventual book.

Breaking Barriers

The Systems We Inherit. The Futures We Build. An evidence-led life story investigating obstacles across education, technology, employment, economic opportunity, faith and public institutions — and the wider consequences when people's knowledge and abilities cannot develop into secure, constructive contributions.

Beginning with the conditions surrounding the 2008 financial crisis, the book examines institutional decisions, changing expectations around technology and childhood education, personal agency, fairness, opportunity and the design of systems before and after artificial intelligence. Documented events, firsthand experience and proposed explanations will be kept distinct throughout.

Read the research-led book introduction

Who makes the rules that shape a person's education, working life and access to opportunity? How are those decisions justified, who can challenge them, and what are the social costs when capable people encounter barriers that may be unnecessary or unfair?

This manuscript follows one lived journey while examining the institutions and social changes surrounding it. The enquiry asks which problems require better human governance, where AI might assist, and which risks could deepen existing exclusions.
